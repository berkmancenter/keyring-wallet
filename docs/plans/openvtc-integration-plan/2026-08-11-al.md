## 1. Cypress RC-1 exists — and we deferred the pin advance deliberately

`VTI-Cypress-RC-1` was tagged across the four OpenVTC repos, pointing at each
repo's current head (VTI +40 commits past our pin, browser-plugin +10;
didcomm-js and the iOS agent already *at* RC-1; `dtgwg-trust-tasks-tf`
unmoved — our `fbe196a` pin is still upstream head). RC-1 is the advance
boundary the plan names.

**Decision: not yet.** Advancing means re-running the ladder bottom-up
(ref-00 → 06v1c) — a proper session of its own — and nothing in the current
work is blocked on the new pins: the binding, the library, and the framework
sit in the tf repo, which hasn't moved. The advance happens as its own
boundary session; sequencing it mid-probe would have mixed two kinds of risk.
New in the RC-1 window and noted for that session: approvals framed as a
ceremony (VTI #927), consent witnesses from canonical bodies (#928),
client-side pre-flight payload refusal (#930 — upstream building what our
§4.2a policy wants), TSP in the setup wizard (#933).

The task layer works on the app's stack, via the official library. The
conclusions that feed the #173 reply, by the editor's own numbering:

  generated `RESPONSE_SPEC` to `consumeInbound` consumes a retained

## 3. Sequencing decision: two rungs before the appendix

The joint appendix (our `vrc/*` + `witness/*` specs in the tf repo) is
authored **from a running exchange, not prose** — the method that has produced
every useful finding so far. Order and why:

1. **ref-06v1d-carrier** first: the binding's ⚠ open carrier question is the
   editor's time-sensitive ask (free to change while draft, MAJOR bump after),
   and its answer decides what carriage ref-06w and the appendix bind to.
   Keyring's chat being a real user surface makes the dedicated-`@type`
   alternative a live contender; the rung measures instead of arguing.
2. **ref-06w-witnessed-exchange** second: the two-thread flow as draft
   documents over the chosen carriage — the qualifying-profile satisfiability
   test the appendix invitation exists for, and the A5 retention cost measured.
3. **Then the appendix**, written against what ran.

Ladder rows for all three are in the plan (§6, Phase C table).

## 4. Position taken: the carrier — dedicated `@type`, favored on early-days grounds

ref-06v1d's measurements (same session) frame the trade: the dedicated type is
less code and leaves chat clean; basic-message's one advantage is *visible*
mixed-fleet degradation — an unaware wallet shows a harmless "Trust Task: …"
line instead of dropping the message silently, which eases migration through
an upgrade window.

**Alberto's position, going into the binding review:** favor the **dedicated
type**. The migration advantage is real but priced for a world with a deployed
fleet to migrate — and neither Keyring's Trust Task support nor OpenVTC has
one yet. These are the early days: breaking-changes tolerance is at its
lifetime maximum (upstream's own norm — "no real deployments out there yet"),
the capability-negotiation gate our migration design needs anyway keeps
unaware wallets from ever being sent a task, and the cost basic-message
imposes on *aware* wallets (protocol traffic in a genuine user chat surface,
or permanent filtering logic) is paid forever. Choose the clean carrier while
choosing is free; `~attach` stays as the attachment convention within it.

Status: a position, not a decision — it goes to the binding review with
Brendan, then to the editor as the answer to his ask #1, with the rung's
measurements attached.
