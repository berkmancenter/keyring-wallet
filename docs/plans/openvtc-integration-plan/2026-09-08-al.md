## §11 Q1 — open, and the binary framing is the thing to reject

Q1 asks whether a cold `propose` requires an out-of-band pairing step, or
whether a correctly-proofed `propose` from an unknown DID is acceptable on its
own. **Not deciding this yet, deliberately** — it should be decided against a
concrete deployment rather than in the abstract, and Keyring does not have one
attached to this path today. The recommendation in §11 (no OOB prerequisite)
should not be promoted to a settled default in the meantime.

What the review adds is that the question is posed as a binary and probably
should not be. Two intermediate positions are worth designing before either
extreme is adopted:

**A shared-membership gate.** A party who can *prove* they hold the same
membership credential the recipient holds is admitted to the `propose` stage —
without being auto-accepted. The proof of shared community is what buys the
right to ask; the disposition is still whatever §5's PDP says. This is a
different axis from pairing: it substitutes a verifiable property of the
requester for a prior bilateral interaction, which is exactly the substitution
the credential model exists to make. It also degrades sensibly — no membership
credential, no cold path, pair first.

**A VTA-side ACL.** Where the VTA already holds an access-control rule that
covers the requester, that rule stands in for pairing. This keeps the trust
decision on the VTA where §5 already puts the PDP, rather than introducing a
second mechanism the wallet has to understand.

Both make the answer *"pairing first, unless something verifiable stands in for
it"* rather than a global yes or no. The design instinct behind that: bearer
invitations and pre-shared secrets are the mechanisms this stack exists to
replace, so making pairing the only admission path reintroduces the pattern in
a new place. Membership held as a credential — presentable to a third party,
portable, not a row in anyone's database — is the intended substitute.

**Not researched yet, and needed before this is decidable:**

1. Whether a membership proof at the `propose` stage is even expressible in
   §4's discovery ladder, or whether it needs a distinct pre-`propose` exchange.
2. What it costs in privacy: proving shared membership before a relationship
   exists discloses membership to a party who has not yet been accepted. A
   selective-disclosure suite makes this cheaper, but "cheaper" is not "free"
   and the disclosure is real.
3. Whether the ACL path and the membership path are one mechanism or two.
4. Anti-spam. §5's Anti-spam subsection is written for the cold-contact case;
   a membership gate changes the threat model rather than removing it.

**Blocks:** §5's exact shape, as §11 already records. **Ours, and not urgent —
this is not on the Prague path.**

## Correction to Finding 2 of the previous day — the wiring, not the manifest

[`2026-09-07-al.md`](./2026-09-07-al.md) Finding 2 noted that `@credo-ts/webvh`
"**is** already a dependency in `app/package.json` at `0.6.3`" and warned that
presence in the manifest is not evidence the method resolves.

That understates it, in a way that weakens the warning. `WebVhDidResolver` is
not merely a dependency — it is **registered in the app's `DidsModule`**
(`app/src/utils/bc-agent-modules.ts`), and has been since `fe9c582`
(2026-07-06). `ref-06x-cypress-stack` measured `unsupportedDidMethod` on
2026-08-17, **a month after that registration**.

So the correct form of the warning is stronger: it is not that a dependency
sits unused in a manifest, it is that a resolver is **wired into the agent and
still does not resolve the method**. A reader who opens the DID configuration —
the natural place to check — sees `new WebVhDidResolver()` in the resolver list
and concludes the capability exists. The measurement proving otherwise was
taken with exactly that configuration in place.

This supersedes the phrasing of Finding 2, not its conclusion: `did:webvh`
resolution is still absent from `vta-carriage_subtask.md`, still on the critical
path of §4 and §5, and still parent Phase D item 3.

# Part II — exchange durability

Review of [`2026-08-24-bam.md`](./2026-08-24-bam.md) Part II (keyring-wallet
[#23](https://github.com/berkmancenter/keyring-wallet/pull/23)). The proposal is
adopted in direction. Three notes: one question answered so it is not
re-litigated, one operational fact the plan is missing, and one caveat about the
evidence any of this gets tested against.

## The Credo question, answered

The concern worth stating and closing: does this reinvent persistence Credo
already provides?

**It does not.** The document is explicit — the work is *"moving continuations
out of the JavaScript stack and into storage that already exists, not inventing
new persistence."* Its inventory is scoped to *"every piece of in-flight
exchange state that is not a Credo record"*, and the retention mechanism it
builds on is the existing `TrustTaskDocumentRepository`, already tagged and
already retaining every consumed document.

So the design sits on top of Credo and the recast rather than beside them. That
is the right posture and it is what makes the estimate credible: the substrate
argument is not optimism, it is an inventory of what already persists versus
what only lives in a stack frame.

**Recorded so the question is asked once.** A durability proposal is exactly the
shape of document that invites "doesn't the framework do this?" from every
subsequent reader.

## The missing middle: `mediatorPollingInterval: 1_000`

Part I fixed why messages were *lost*. Part II asks what the wallet does when a
message is merely *late*. Between them sits a mitigation neither part records,
which is what actually carried the most recent demo:

`app/src/utils/bc-agent-modules.ts` sets **`mediatorPollingInterval: 1_000`** —
a one-second pickup cadence. Its own comment carries the load-bearing condition:

> Safe since the mediator pickup queue moved off askar to postgres on
> 2026-08-26; before that a faster cadence tripped rate-correlated queue
> failures.

Two things follow, and the plan should say both:

1. **This is a mitigation, not a fix.** A 1 s cadence narrows the window in
   which a late message finds no live continuation; it does not persist the
   continuation. Every failure mode in Part II's §1 inventory survives it —
   they just get less likely. The durability work should make this interval a
   tuning choice rather than a correctness dependency, and that is a reasonable
   acceptance criterion for it.
2. **Its safety is conditional on infrastructure, and the condition is not in
   the plan.** The interval is safe *because* the queue is postgres. Anyone who
   lowers it further, or runs against a mediator still backed by askar,
   re-enters the rate-correlated failure mode Part I root-caused. That
   dependency currently exists only as a source comment in one file — a poor
   place for a constraint that binds a deployment.

**The plan edit this implies:** Part II's §2 (what the recast already gave us)
or §6 (phasing) names the current interval, states that durability's goal is to
remove the dependency on it, and records the postgres precondition where a
reader of the plan will find it.

## A caveat on the evidence

Durability is going to be judged by real-device runs, and real-device transport
results are only as good as the environment they were taken in. We have now
been caught twice by symptoms that looked like protocol or wallet defects and
were neither — Part I's own mediator diagnosis is one, and there is a second,
purely local to how a device under test is instrumented.

**The rule, stated generally:** an exchange failure observed on a physical
device is not evidence of a wallet defect until the run has been repeated with
the device instrumented as little as possible — no screen mirroring, no capture
tooling sharing the device's network path. Instrumentation that competes for
bandwidth produces failures indistinguishable from delivery failures, which is
exactly the class of symptom this plan exists to reason about.

This matters more here than elsewhere: a durability feature is *validated* by
inducing lateness and delivery flakiness, so a test harness that induces them
accidentally will produce a passing-looking or failing-looking result for the
wrong reason. Any acceptance criterion Part II gains should say how the run was
instrumented, not only what it observed.

## Position

Adopt Part II's direction. It is the most demo-relevant item in the current
docs set — an exchange that dies because a phone locked or a message arrived
four seconds late is the failure mode with the worst consequences in front of an
audience, and the substrate argument says the fix is mostly wiring rather than
invention. §4's open question is still the gate before any of it is written into
[`trust_tasks_subtask.md`](./trust_tasks_subtask.md).
