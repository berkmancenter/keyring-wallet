## Part II — Credo has DIDComm v2, and the plan says it does not

### The finding

Research into who has run DIDComm v2 on Credo led to `verana-labs/vs-agent`,
which pins `@credo-ts/{core,didcomm,anoncreds}@0.7.1-pr-2704-20260909134930`.
Followed to source on 2026-09-13:

- **PR #2704 on `openwallet-foundation/credo-ts` is "feat: initial DIDComm V2
  support"**, by genaris, open since 2026-03-18, 91 commits, 282 files,
  last pushed 2026-09-09. Its body: "Added here to allow releasing
  pr-versions and test behaviour on different platforms before the initial
  merge to main." `mergeable_state` is `blocked`; the head's CI has the
  Conformance Tests and one unit shard red and DCO unresolved; review
  activity stopped in June.
- It is published as npm snapshots under the `pr-2704` dist-tag, five of
  them since 2026-08-27, based on **0.7.x**. npm `latest` is 0.7.0; Keyring
  and upstream bifold 3.0.22 are on 0.6.3.
- Verana's three pnpm patches are **not v2 fixes** — they add a Data
  Integrity credential format to issue-credential. So the PR's v2 runs
  unpatched in their server.
- Inside the PR: `didcommVersions` config, `did:peer:2`/`:4`, OOB 2.0,
  ECDH-1PU/ECDH-ES envelopes through `kms.encrypt` (Askar — no WebCrypto),
  Coordinate Mediation 2.0, Pickup 3.0/4.0, Routing 2.0, a v2 mediator mode in
  `samples/mediator.ts`, and SICPA `didcomm-rust` interop fixtures.

### What it supersedes

The parent plan states, in three places, that Credo cannot speak v2:

- §3: "**DIDComm**: v1 only (Credo has no v2); … Keyring↔VTA therefore uses
  OpenVTC's JS libraries beside Credo, never through it."
- §5.4 stage 3: "Trust Tasks over DIDComm v2 — `@openvtc/vti-didcomm-js`
  beside Credo".
- Phase D's `ref-07-credo-adapter` row: `binding/didcomm/0.1` "is DIDComm
  v2.1 and unreachable from Credo 0.6.3; the v2.1 leg, where needed, rides
  `@openvtc/vti-didcomm-js` beside Credo".

`pnm_cnm_subtask.md` §5.3 item 4 and P1 repeat it. All of these were true
of released Credo when written and remain true of 0.6.3 and 0.7.0. They are
no longer the whole picture: a maintainer-authored v2 exists, is consumable
without a fork, and is what at least one production Credo user runs.

The positions do **not** flip to "use Credo's v2 everywhere". Two things are
unmeasured and the subtask makes both of them rungs before any app change:
whether Credo's v2 envelope interoperates with `affinidi-messaging-didcomm`
(ref-15b), and whether it can cross the VTI mediator's ATM authentication at
all (ref-18). Until those answer, the VTA leg stays exactly where the PNM
subplan put it, on `vti-didcomm-js`. What changes today is the
**wallet-to-wallet** half: the same reasoning `2026-09-02-bam.md` used for
`TspCarriage` — two Keyring wallets need no ecosystem counterparty — applies
to v2 once our own mediator can route it, and the PR ships that mediator mode.

### Why Credo-native rather than `vti-didcomm-js` for the wallet

Weighed on the pinned clones, not on documentation:

1. **Custody.** `vti-didcomm-js` holds a raw private JWK
   (`pnm-core`'s `Identity`, a `WeakMap` of secrets) and has no injectable
   key-agreement port. The parent's §4.3 says keys stay in Askar, and `ref-09`
   had to build ports to get TSP there. Credo's v2 envelope is `kms.encrypt`
   over Askar out of the box.
2. **Connection model.** Every Keyring exchange keys on Credo connection
   records; `TspCarriage` rejects an envelope whose TSP sender is not the
   connection's `theirDid`. A second stack has no connection records.
3. **Hermes.** `vti-didcomm-js` needs `crypto.subtle` for AES-KW, AES-CBC,
   HMAC-SHA-512 and SHA-256; Keyring polyfills SHA-256 only. Porting it means
   a noble rewrite of its symmetric layer — the HPKE contribution again, for
   a library that is a VTA client rather than a peer-to-peer stack.
4. **Coexistence.** `didcommVersions: ['v1','v2']` on one agent is §1.6 met
   by the framework.

Against: the 0.6 → 0.7 hop (Buffer removal, base64url attachments, Node 22),
an unmerged PR with red CI, and leading upstream bifold by a minor version.
Those are real and they are why V0 is Node-only and V1 is post-Prague.

### Alternatives considered and rejected (standing rationale is in the subtask §4)

- `0.6.4-pr-2704` snapshots (avoid the 0.7 hop): 57 of 91 commits newer than
  the last one; rejected.
- Fork Credo, rebase the PR onto 0.6.3: contradicts §4.3's no-fork posture;
  the snapshots are the maintainers' own no-fork route.
- Wait for the merge: no timeline; the PR asks for platform evidence, which
  we can supply and which shortens the wait.

### Stage A1 built and green — `ref-15-credo-didcomm-v2-hello` (same day)

On a branch off `main` (`feat/didcomm-v2-rungs`), standalone npm tree. The
`pr-2704` snapshot installs and runs on **Node 20.19.2** — the first measured
answer to Q2. 20/20 checks: OOB 2.0 on `did:peer:2`, BasicMessage 2.0 both
ways, the JWE header exactly the VTI mediator's pair, and a v1 connection
alive and delivering interleaved on the same agents. Three findings carried
into the subtask's V0 table and the rung's README:

1. **The inviter gets no connection from the invitee's first v2 message.**
   The receiver looks connections up (DID pair, `theirDid`, keys) and never
   creates one, so the first message is refused as `No connection associated`.
   The PR's own `makeConnection` helper uses a return invitation; the rung
   measures the refusal, then applies that. Consequence for V2 step C12: a
   Keyring v2 connect is either two invitations or a first-contact handler
   that creates the record — which is what the VTA does (ACL on `from`, no
   record). Candidate upstream question on #2704.
2. **BasicMessage 2.0 has its own event** (`DidCommBasicMessageV2StateChanged`).
   Only the legacy VRC dialect listens to basic messages; Trust Task carriage
   has its own `@type`.
3. **`json-canonicalize@2.0.1` is published without its `bundles/`** and
   Credo 0.7's `^2.0.0` picks it under npm; pinned to 2.0.0. V1 step 1 will
   hit the same thing in the yarn tree.

### Stage A2–A4 built and green (same day, same branch)

- **ref-15b, 11/11 — the interop question is closed.** Credo's v2 and
  `vti-didcomm-js` 0.6.2 read each other's authcrypt JWEs both ways on the
  first attempt, on the spec-correct ConcatKDF (vti's `legacyKekUsed`
  detector stayed false), kids agree, tampering is rejected by both. The
  subtask §4 condition on the VTA leg is now only about the Affinidi
  mediator's login and socket (ref-18), not the envelope.
- **ref-16, 16/16 — the binding works, with one Credo wart.** The
  `binding/didcomm 0.2` envelope as a Credo class: document byte-identical
  over v1 and v2, the 0.9.0 pipeline with real payload validation, thread
  and identity rules per spec, anoncrypt refused. Finding: Credo's v2→v1
  normaliser spreads `body` next to `@id`/`@type`; a document's `id`/`type`
  overwrite the envelope's on a plain class and its `threadId` hits a
  getter-only accessor and **throws**. Renamed `Expose` properties absorb
  the four keys. The wallet's `DidCommV2Carriage` inherits that shape;
  candidate issue on #2704.
- **ref-17, 14/14 — v2 mediation on real sockets, and Q3 is yes.** First
  end-to-end run of Credo's Coordinate Mediation 2.0 + Pickup 4.0 anywhere
  (upstream's own e2e is `it.skip`). Mediator derives its routing DID, phones
  are outbound-only, `QueueOnly` carries over from production, ≈300 ms at a
  300 ms poll. Carol holds v1 mediation to a v1-only mediator and v2 to the
  v2 one with both pickup loops live. Three findings that shape C12/C13:
  a v1-default wallet cannot accept a v2 invitation without explicit
  routing; the newest provision becomes the default mediator; the mediator
  side of a v2 pairing needs the return invitation (ref-15 finding 1 is
  now a deployment blocker for §7.1's second deployment until first-contact
  connection creation exists).

- **ref-18, 6/6 — Stage A's gate passed, and V3 is Credo.** A Credo agent's
  `did:peer:2`, keys in Askar, logged into the real Affinidi mediator (the
  public dev instance) with an authenticate message packed by Credo's own
  envelope service, held the live-delivery socket, received a forwarded
  message from a `vti-didcomm-js` peer into Credo's receiver, and sent a
  Credo-packed message wrapped in a Credo-packed forward back through the
  mediator — both directions live in ≈100 ms. The subtask's §4 condition
  is closed: the VTA leg is one Credo transport, not a second DIDComm
  stack; `vti-didcomm-js` supplies `did:webvh` resolution and three
  plaintext builders. Findings: the transport must open the mediator's own
  Pickup 3.0 frames (Credo has no webvh resolver and no Pickup 3.0) and
  send the queue-id ack; `resolveCreatedDidDocumentWithKeys` is the
  connection-DID-to-Askar-key bridge. Supersedes this plan's earlier
  "vti-didcomm-js stays for the VTA leg until ref-18 answers", and
  `pnm_cnm_subtask.md` P1's assumption that the approver rides
  `vti-didcomm-js` beside Credo — P1 can ride the same transport.
