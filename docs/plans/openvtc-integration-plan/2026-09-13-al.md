## Part II — Credo has DIDComm v2, and the plan says it does not

### The finding

Research into who has run DIDComm v2 on Credo led to `verana-labs/vs-agent`,
which pins `@credo-ts/{core,didcomm,anoncreds}@0.7.1-pr-2704-20260909134930`.
Followed to source on 2026-09-13:

- **PR #2704 on `openwallet-foundation/credo-ts` is "feat: initial DIDComm V2
  support"**, by genaris, open since 2026-03-18, 91 commits, 282 files,
  last pushed 2026-09-09. Its body: "Added here to allow releasing
  pr-versions and test behaviour on different platforms before the initial
  merge to main." `mergeable_state` is `blocked`; the head's CI has the
  Conformance Tests and one unit shard red and DCO unresolved; review
  activity stopped in June.
- It is published as npm snapshots under the `pr-2704` dist-tag, five of
  them since 2026-08-27, based on **0.7.x**. npm `latest` is 0.7.0; Keyring
  and upstream bifold 3.0.22 are on 0.6.3.
- Verana's three pnpm patches are **not v2 fixes** — they add a Data
  Integrity credential format to issue-credential. So the PR's v2 runs
  unpatched in their server.
- Inside the PR: `didcommVersions` config, `did:peer:2`/`:4`, OOB 2.0,
  ECDH-1PU/ECDH-ES envelopes through `kms.encrypt` (Askar — no WebCrypto),
  Coordinate Mediation 2.0, Pickup 3.0/4.0, Routing 2.0, a v2 mediator mode in
  `samples/mediator.ts`, and SICPA `didcomm-rust` interop fixtures.

### What it supersedes

The parent plan states, in three places, that Credo cannot speak v2:

- §3: "**DIDComm**: v1 only (Credo has no v2); … Keyring↔VTA therefore uses
  OpenVTC's JS libraries beside Credo, never through it."
- §5.4 stage 3: "Trust Tasks over DIDComm v2 — `@openvtc/vti-didcomm-js`
  beside Credo".
- Phase D's `ref-07-credo-adapter` row: `binding/didcomm/0.1` "is DIDComm
  v2.1 and unreachable from Credo 0.6.3; the v2.1 leg, where needed, rides
  `@openvtc/vti-didcomm-js` beside Credo".

`pnm_cnm_subtask.md` §5.3 item 4 and P1 repeat it. All of these were true
of released Credo when written and remain true of 0.6.3 and 0.7.0. They are
no longer the whole picture: a maintainer-authored v2 exists, is consumable
without a fork, and is what at least one production Credo user runs.

The positions do **not** flip to "use Credo's v2 everywhere". Two things are
unmeasured and the subtask makes both of them rungs before any app change:
whether Credo's v2 envelope interoperates with `affinidi-messaging-didcomm`
(ref-15b), and whether it can cross the VTI mediator's ATM authentication at
all (ref-18). Until those answer, the VTA leg stays exactly where the PNM
subplan put it, on `vti-didcomm-js`. What changes today is the
**wallet-to-wallet** half: the same reasoning `2026-09-02-bam.md` used for
`TspCarriage` — two Keyring wallets need no ecosystem counterparty — applies
to v2 once our own mediator can route it, and the PR ships that mediator mode.

### Why Credo-native rather than `vti-didcomm-js` for the wallet

Weighed on the pinned clones, not on documentation:

1. **Custody.** `vti-didcomm-js` holds a raw private JWK
   (`pnm-core`'s `Identity`, a `WeakMap` of secrets) and has no injectable
   key-agreement port. The parent's §4.3 says keys stay in Askar, and `ref-09`
   had to build ports to get TSP there. Credo's v2 envelope is `kms.encrypt`
   over Askar out of the box.
2. **Connection model.** Every Keyring exchange keys on Credo connection
   records; `TspCarriage` rejects an envelope whose TSP sender is not the
   connection's `theirDid`. A second stack has no connection records.
3. **Hermes.** `vti-didcomm-js` needs `crypto.subtle` for AES-KW, AES-CBC,
   HMAC-SHA-512 and SHA-256; Keyring polyfills SHA-256 only. Porting it means
   a noble rewrite of its symmetric layer — the HPKE contribution again, for
   a library that is a VTA client rather than a peer-to-peer stack.
4. **Coexistence.** `didcommVersions: ['v1','v2']` on one agent is §1.6 met
   by the framework.

Against: the 0.6 → 0.7 hop (Buffer removal, base64url attachments, Node 22),
an unmerged PR with red CI, and leading upstream bifold by a minor version.
Those are real and they are why V0 is Node-only and V1 is post-Prague.

### Alternatives considered and rejected (standing rationale is in the subtask §4)

- `0.6.4-pr-2704` snapshots (avoid the 0.7 hop): 57 of 91 commits newer than
  the last one; rejected.
- Fork Credo, rebase the PR onto 0.6.3: contradicts §4.3's no-fork posture;
  the snapshots are the maintainers' own no-fork route.
- Wait for the merge: no timeline; the PR asks for platform evidence, which
  we can supply and which shortens the wait.

On a branch off `main` (`feat/didcomm-v2-rungs`), standalone npm tree. The
`pr-2704` snapshot installs and runs on **Node 20.19.2** — the first measured

### Correction: first contact was a Credo option all along (2026-09-14)

ref-15 finding 1 ("the inviter gets no connection from the invitee's first
v2 message — Credo only looks up, never creates") and ref-17 finding 3 (the
same, on the mediator, called a deployment blocker) described the
**default** configuration, not a gap. `DidCommConnectionsModuleConfig` has
`autoCreateConnectionOnFirstMessage` (default `false`); with it on,
`DidCommMessageReceiver.findConnection` matches the plaintext's `to` DID
against our sender-role OOB records and creates the inviter's connection
(role `Responder`, state `Completed`, `didcommVersion: 'v2'`), rotating our
DID when the invitation is reusable. Found while wiring the mediator half of
V2 step 4: the mediate-request "refusal" the earlier status predicted never
had to happen.

sentence in the V2 status block, and the plan's reading of ref-15/ref-17
finding 1/3. Both rung READMEs carry the correction inline (the measurement
stands — the rungs ran with the default — the conclusion drawn from it does
not). `ensureV2ConnectionForFirstContact` in `@bifold/trust-tasks` stays as
the fallback for a party without the option, corrected to role `Responder`
(it had `Requester`, a mirror-image mistake the Credo code made obvious).

### The v2 mediator is a second mediation record (2026-09-14)

Design adopted for V2 step 4 and §7.1 rule 2, implemented on the worktree:

- `mediator-server` in v2 mode (`yarn mediator --didcomm-v2`; env
  `MEDIATOR_DIDCOMM_VERSIONS=v1,v2`) sets the first-contact option, uses
  `did:peer:2` for its OOB 2.0 DIDs, and mints a **reusable**
  out-of-band/2.0 invitation beside the v1 legacy one. Its routing DID is
  self-derived from its routing key and its HTTP endpoint
  (`getEffectiveMediatorRoutingDid` falls back to the first endpoint when
  there is no `ws://` one), so no WebSocket transport was added: the wallet
  polls with Pickup 4.0 exactly as it polls v1 with Pickup 2.0. The
  lifecycle script writes both invitations into `app/.env`
  (`MEDIATOR_URL`, `MEDIATOR_V2_URL`), the v2 one blank when v2 is off so a
  stale invitation cannot outlive a restart.
- The wallet, with the developer flag on and `MEDIATOR_V2_URL` baked in,
  provisions Coordinate Mediation 2.0 **once** after agent start
  (`provisionV2Mediation` in `@bifold/core` `trust-tasks/v2Routing.ts`):
  accept the invitation with `getRouting({ useDefaultMediator: false })`
  (ref-17 finding 1 — the v1 default cannot route a `did:peer:2`),
  `provision()`, then `setDefaultMediator(previous)` (ref-17 finding 2 —
  the newest grant would otherwise become the default and move every v1
  invitation's route). The v1 mediator remains `selectedMediator` and the
  default; nothing about existing connections changes. A failure is logged
  and the wallet stays on v1.
- Two pickup loops: the existing Pickup 2.0 loop against v1, then Pickup
  4.0 against the v2 record — in that order, because the credo patch that
  stops every running loop before a v1/v2 start (the anti-stacking guard)
  does not run in the v4 case. `configureMessagePickup` restarts both on
  every foreground resume, so the order is encoded there, not left to
  callers.
- v2 invitations route through the v2 record by id (`getRoutingForV2`),
  unmediated when there is none — never the v1 default. Applied at both
  ends: `createRelationshipInvitation` and `connectFromInvitation`.

Rejected: replacing `selectedMediator` with the v2 invitation (the earlier
§7.1 rule 2). It would have moved a wallet's v1 routing to a mediator that
speaks v1 too, but the point of §7.1 is that no existing connection changes
mediator; two records is what ref-17 measured and it costs one extra poll.

Not done: the witness-server does not enable `didcommVersions: ['v1','v2']`
yet. With v2 on, a bare `createInvitation({ label })` mints a **v2**
invitation (Credo infers v2 when there are no handshake protocols,
attachments or routing) — the witness's two `createInvitation` calls would
change meaning, so that is its own step with its own test pass (the
witnessed variant of V2 step 5).

### Simulator runs of C14 (2026-09-14)

Environment quirk that cost three attempts: the `Pixel_6_API_33` emulator
has a device lock PIN (set by an earlier session), and after a boot Android
keeps user 0 `RUNNING_LOCKED` until it is entered — every app launch
answers "Activity class does not exist" and Appium reports "Appium Settings
app is not running". `adb shell locksettings verify --old <pin>` unlocks
it. Recorded in memory, not in the harness: the harness cannot know a PIN.

Results are appended below as each run completes.

**B7, first simulator result (2026-09-14): the 0.7 hop could not start an
agent on a device.** Every unit gate was green and the iOS app died at
`agent.initialize()` with `Error during call to 'onInitializeContext' method
in module 'didcomm'`. The cause chain (logged from a new try/catch around
initialize, at info level — on iOS dev builds console.error is captured by
LogBox and never reaches the device log): `KeyManagementError: Error
creating key <- CredoError: Could not encode data to utf8 string <- Error:
Failed to construct 'TextDecoder': the 'fatal' option is unsupported`.
Credo 0.7's `TypedArrayEncoder.toUtf8String` constructs `TextDecoder` with
`{ fatal: true }`; the app's `fast-text-encoding` polyfill throws on that
option. Node's native `TextDecoder` supports it, so jest could not see it.
Fix: a wrapper in `app/index.js` that accepts `fatal` and enforces it by
round-tripping the decoded text through the encoder (a lossy decode does not
round-trip), verified in Node against valid, invalid and non-fatal input;
the iOS onboarding probe then completed and the agent initialized. Recorded
in the subtask's V1 section as the fourth breaking change of the hop.
Candidate upstream note: Credo's React Native package could document the
polyfill requirement (or ship a decoder that honours `fatal`).

Second symptom, same cause: once unlocked, the keyguard still sat over the
launched app, its activity paused, and Doze blocked the app UID's network
(`dumpsys connectivity` listed the UIDs with `blockedReasons=2`). The RN
dev client then reported Metro "not running" and "Unable to load script"
while a shell `nc` from the emulator reached Metro — the shell UID is never
dozed, which made it look like a network or Wi-Fi problem for two attempts
(disabling the emulator's Wi-Fi was a red herring). Clearing the lock and
disabling Doze put the JS load at ~5 s. Neither quirk is a Keyring or Credo
problem; both are in memory so the next simulator session does not pay for
them again.

**B7 simulator half: green (2026-09-14).** `yarn e2e:vrc` from the
worktree — Android emulator (Pixel 6, API 33) as wallet A and iOS simulator
(iPhone 17, iOS 26.3) as wallet B, both debug builds on the `pr-2704`
snapshot, both routed through the production v1 mediator baked into
`app/.env` — passed end to end: onboarding on both, invitation from
Android, paste on iOS, relationship proposal accepted, both VRC contacts
visible, the propose + issue Trust Task markers present on Android. That is
the "v1 unchanged" claim of V1 step 1 on simulators; `e2e:vrc:devices` (B7's
device half) and B8 remain.

**C14, first v2 simulator attempt (2026-09-14): failed on the wallet, not
the protocol.** Two Android emulators, the local `mediator-server` in v2
mode. Both wallets onboarded and toggled the developer flag, restarted, and
came back without provisioning Mediation 2.0: the flag is persisted by the
store reducer but `container-imp.ts`'s `LOAD_STATE` rehydration lists every
developer flag by name and did not list `EnableDidCommV2`, so a restart
reset it to `false` and the agent built without v2. Fixed by adding it to
the loader (the same three lines the TSP flag has). The runner's
pre-invitation gate on the Mediation 2.0 grant marker is what caught it —
without that gate the run would have minted a v1 invitation and failed
later with a less legible error.

**C14, second attempt: the grant arrived and the wallet refused it.** With
the flag rehydrated both wallets accepted the mediator's OOB 2.0 invitation
and sent `mediate-request`; the mediator answered
`coordinate-mediation/2.0/mediate-grant` within 300 ms, and each wallet
replied with a problem report, "The message type is not supported". Credo
registers the Coordinate Mediation 2.0 recipient handlers only when
`mediationRecipient.mediationProtocolVersions` includes `'v2'` — the default
is `['v1']` — and the app config had never set it (ref-17's recipient did,
which is why the rung never saw this). Now `['v1','v2']` behind the flag.
Two lessons for the plan: the mediator side of V2 step 4 works on first
contact exactly as the corrected finding says (the mediator created the
connection from the wallet's first message and granted), and every Credo

### The attestation bug the strict badge check found (2026-09-15)

The TSP run on the same two devices passed every marker, but one contact
screen was missing **Secure Exchange** — and the run did not care, because
the shield assertion only logged what it found. Making it strict
(`requireSecureExchange`, and `requireLocality` for the locality variant)
turned the badge into a result instead of a note, and the first strict run
failed on the iPhone's contact for Bob with `Evidence public key does not
match leaf certificate public key`.

**What the iPad's log showed.** Three App Attest keys in one run:

1. `14:51:37` — the attestation prefetch calls `attestKey` on the keychain's
   key id and Apple rejects it (`DCError 2`; the id survives a reinstall, the
   key does not). The native code clears the id and generates key **B**,
   attests it, and caches B's chain — but the JS layer stores that chain
   under the public key it *asked* about, key **A**'s.
2. `14:52:24` — the user authenticates and **B** signs.
3. `14:52:28` — evidence assembly looks up B's chain, misses (the cache holds
   it under A), calls `attestKey` again, and that call clears B and generates
   **C**. The evidence then carried **B's signature with C's certificate
   chain**, which every verifier correctly rejects.

So the bug was not in signing or in TSP or v2 at all: evidence assembly could
destroy the key whose signature it was assembling evidence for. It needed two
things to go wrong together (a cache keyed by the wrong key, and a fetch with
the side effect of replacing the key), which is why it showed on one contact,
on one device, and only after a reinstall.

**The fix.**

- A new native read, `getCachedHardwareKeyAttestation`, returns the chain the
  device already holds for the current key — no Apple call, no key mutation.
  (Android's KeyStore chain is already a local read, so it delegates to
  `getKeyAttestation`.)
- After a signature exists, `HardwareEvidenceBuilder` tries that held chain
  first. The key that signed is known-valid, so the chain it holds is the
  right one, and nothing can replace the key behind the signature's back.
- A chain is **never** paired with a public key it does not certify: when the
  attested key differs from the signing key, the evidence goes out without a
  chain (honest: "no attestation") instead of with a mismatched one.
- A chain fetched for a replacement key is cached under *that* key, so the
  next signature — which will use it — gets a hit rather than a re-fetch.
- The native attest path clears the cached public key and chain whenever it
  clears or replaces the key, so a cached chain can no longer outlive its key.

Four regression tests replay the iPad's sequence (held chain used without a
fetch; mismatched chain never attached; no fetch after signing when the held
chain belongs to a replaced key; a prefetched chain cached under the
replacement key).

**Result — TSP over DIDComm v2, witnessed, strict badges, iPhone + iPad:
green.** Both contacts show **Secure Exchange + Verified**. The device logs
show the new path doing its work: the key is still replaced during the
prefetch ("Attestation was issued for a different key than requested"), the
chain is cached under the replacement, and evidence assembly after signing
reports `Attestation: cached (2 certs)` with no Apple call — every
