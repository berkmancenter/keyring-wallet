# 2026-10-24 — The embedded VTA: UX review, evidence on phones as agents, and a recommended direction (al)

**Subtask:** [`embedded-vta_subtask.md`](./embedded-vta_subtask.md), read at PR #52 head `cd8b86b0`.
**Status:** Proposals only. Nothing here edits the subtask's design.
- **Part 1:** how an "on this phone" option would be offered, from a UI/UX review.
- **Part 2:** the evidence on whether a phone can be an always-answering agent, how the VTA has grown upstream since this subtask was written, and what that means for rooms and community messaging.
- **Part 3:** a recommended disposition for this subtask, for its author to accept or reject.

## Part 1 — How the choice would be offered, if it is offered

The subtask describes a third topology: the phone acting as its own agent. The project already ships the hosted-agent model, so the question is how an "on this phone" option could be offered **beside** it without confusing people. The proposals below come from a UI/UX review against the current navigation. They assume hosted stays the default.

### U1. Where the choice lives and what it is called

- **Location.** In **My Agent → "Where your agent runs"**. Onboarding stays one path, with hosted as the default; the choice is not part of onboarding.
- **The two options:**
  - **"Hosted (always on)"**: the default. It shows the hosting service's name, in provider-generic wording;
  - **"On this phone"**.
- **Wording.** User-facing text never says "VTA". Body text says "your agent".

### U2. The trade-off, in the screen's own words

- **On this phone:** "No service holds your agent. It answers only while this phone is on and online; requests wait until then."
- **Hosted:** "Answers any time, even with your phone off. Runs on the service shown."

### U3. A request that arrives while the phone-agent is asleep or offline

- **What the asker sees:** "Waiting for <name>'s phone", with the time it was sent, and never "failed". If the request expires first, both sides see "Expired before <name>'s phone could answer".
- **What the person sees when the phone wakes:**
  - the request is in My Agent → Requests, marked "Arrived while your phone was offline · 2 h ago";
  - standing rules apply at once, and their answers show in the history as "Answered by your rule";
  - with push available, one quiet notification: "3 requests waited for your phone".
- **The status chip in My Agent** reads "On this phone · online" or "On this phone · answers when you're back".

### U4. Switching later, in either direction

- **Allowed** from the same screen, with the phone online.
- **Standing rules** belong to the person, and are re-applied on the new host.
- **Grants depend on an engineering answer the subtask does not give yet:**
  - if the agent keeps its identifier and only where it runs changes, grants stay valid;
  - if the identifier changes, each community must grant again. The screen then shows a checklist, for example "2 communities need to confirm your agent again".

  This decides how heavy a switch feels to the user.
- **During the move,** requests pause behind a "Moving your agent…" state, then resume.

**Proposed change.** Add to §9 an open question: "Does moving between a hosted agent and the phone keep the agent's identifier, and therefore its grants?"

### U5. Fit with the current navigation

- **No new tab or top-level screen.** The option sits inside the existing My Agent tab.
- **Inline status in the Communities and rooms preview.** Any action there that needs the agent's answer shows the same status inline, for example "Your agent is on this phone; it answers when you're online". Nobody is surprised by a wait.

### U6. What these notes imply for the subtask

1. **Waiting has to be designed, not just tolerated.** A phone-hosted agent turns "instant answer" into "answer when the phone is next online". The asker's "Waiting for …" and "Expired before …" states need a protocol answer: how long a request may wait, and who tells the asker it expired. The subtask should state that lifetime and its owner.
2. **The UX is easiest when the option stays additive.** Keep hosted as the default, add "On this phone" as an explicit choice, and keep both reachable from one screen. Then nothing about today's hosted flows changes.
3. **The identifier question (U4) belongs in the design.** Part 3 explains why it matters whatever is decided about this subtask. It sits beside the wake pipeline (§4.2), because it determines whether communities see a switch at all.

## Part 2 — Evidence: can a phone be an always-answering agent?

**Sources.**
- Apple and Google platform documentation, and published engineering accounts, read on 2026-10-24.
- Upstream VTI at the pin `49f5f1be` (tag VTI-Eucalyptus), the Trust Tasks specs at `7b6bb488`, `vta-mobile-agent-ios` at `035dd65d`, and wallet and bifold `main`.

**Where the evidence is weaker,** it is marked. None of those points changes the conclusion.

### E1. The limit is the operating system, not connectivity

**Phones are rarely offline.** Opensignal measured US users without signal for about 1% of the time (2022–23 data). The limit is that both mobile operating systems deliberately stop background apps from running when the app wants to.

**On iOS:**
- **Background pushes are best effort and budgeted.** Apple's guidance is to send no more than two or three an hour, under a device-wide budget that resets daily. Apps "should never be designed expecting that every background push notification will be received". (Apple Developer Forums threads 757094 and 760244, quoting the documentation.)
- **Force-quit apps get nothing.** An app the user swiped away is not woken by a push at all (Apple Developer Forums 714337).
- **Low Power Mode** disables Background App Refresh.
- **Scheduled work runs on the system's terms.** `BGTaskScheduler` tasks are discretionary, with about 30 s for a refresh task.
- **The one wake channel with guaranteed delivery has a catch.** PushKit VoIP pushes must be reported to CallKit as incoming calls, so they cannot serve as a general wake.
- **Notification Service Extensions** run about 30 s, within a small memory limit (about 24 MB, reported on Apple's forums but not documented). They only run for visible alert pushes unless Apple grants a filtering entitlement.

**On Android:**
- **Doze** defers network, jobs and alarms to maintenance windows that grow further apart while the device is idle.
- **App Standby Buckets** move an app untouched for 8 days into the Restricted bucket, where jobs run once a day (<https://developer.android.com/topic/performance/appstandby>).
- **FCM throttles silent wake-ups.** High-priority messages that do not lead to a visible notification "may be deprioritized" (<https://firebase.google.com/docs/cloud-messaging/android/message-priority>).
- **Long-running work is capped.** Android 15 caps `dataSync` foreground services at 6 h per 24 h.
- **Some manufacturers kill background apps outright** (<https://dontkillmyapp.com/>).

**Conclusion.** A phone can answer reliably while the user is looking at it. It cannot reliably answer requests that arrive while it sits in a pocket. Silent, rule-based answering is exactly the case both platforms throttle.

### E2. Every comparable production system put an always-on server in front of the phone

| System | Shape |
|---|---|
| **WhatsApp** | Abandoned the phone as source of truth in 2021, because companion devices "frequently get disconnected… when the phone has a poor connection, its battery is running low… or the application process gets killed by the phone's OS" (<https://engineering.fb.com/2021/07/14/security/whatsapp-multi-device/>) |
| **Signal** | Queues encrypted messages on its servers for offline devices |
| **Matrix** | A homeserver, with a push gateway that wakes clients |
| **DIDComm** | Mediators, because a mobile agent "doesn't have a permanent IP address" (Aries RFC 0046) |
| **KERI** | KERIA, "KERI Agent in the cloud", with keys signing at the edge. The production Veridian wallet uses it "for messaging availability" |
| **Briar, Berty, Holochain** | Each added always-on helper nodes (Briar Mailbox, replication nodes, Holo hosts) after phone-only operation proved unreliable |

No production system was found in which a phone alone is the always-reachable agent.

### E3. Upstream VTI is a fast-growing, always-on server

**Size.**
- **Rust lines:** 24k (2026-02-26), 391k (Dogwood, 08-30), 685k (the pin, 10-07). About 600 commits landed in September alone.
- **The code this subtask measured on 09-14 has nearly doubled.** `trust_tasks/` grew from about 25k to about 44k lines, and `operations/` from about 47k to about 65k.
- **Trust Task types:** 89 (06-09), 328 (09-01), 587 (10-05).

**What the VTA runs at the pin:**
- keys, ACL, personas, vault, app-state, backup;
- audit, policy and approvals, consent, attestation;
- did:webvh hosting, push binding, mediator protocol management, and data rooms.

**What it requires of its host:**
- about nine background loops, including TTL sweepers, a durable push sweep every 30 s and a drain loop every 2 s;
- 37 storage keyspaces, and a persistent mediator connection per DID;
- to stay online, in its own words: "The VTA stays online so…" (`docs/01-concepts/overview.md`).

**The subtask's own numbers have moved:**
- upstream now has 20 black-box e2e tests, not 10;
- the push specs are at 0.2;
- `vta-mobile-core::push` builds the device-side documents, not the sender side.

### E4. Rooms and community messaging need the member's VTA reachable

- **The member's VTA is the MLS leaf:** "one leaf per member — the member's VTA, not their devices" (`docs/05-design-notes/data-rooms.md` §5.1).
- **It is also the presentation oracle and the record sealer** (`docs/02-vta/data-rooms.md`).
- **Admission sends the Welcome to the joiner's VTA,** and only the owner commits membership changes. The wallet's own rooms subtask says "owners run on an always-on VTA".
- **A member who misses a commit** cannot read later records until it backfills.
- **The planned change feed wakes the phone through the member's VTA** (`openvtc-rooms-plan/rooms_group_messaging_subtask.md` §6.5).

**Conclusion.** A phone could hold the key, but it cannot be reachable enough to be admitted or kept current. It would also become the member's only device in that room. At most, a phone-hosted member could be a reduced, read-only one.

### E5. Upstream's own phone agent approves for a hosted VTA; it does not replace one

`vta-mobile-agent-ios` is an iOS approver for a remote VTA: sign-in step-ups and task consent. On a push it opens a session and drains up to five messages with 5 s timeouts (`AgentModel.swift`). This is this subtask's wake → fetch → act pattern, but serving a hosted VTA rather than standing in for one. It has been dormant since 2026-07-29.

### E6. Phone secure hardware and the VTA's enclave are different grades, each stronger somewhere

| Threat | Stronger | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Someone holding the device | **Phone** (Secure Enclave, StrongBox) | Certified tamper resistance; keys cannot be exported |
| Running the agent's rules in a verifiable place | **Cloud enclave** | The VTA's Nitro mode (`vta-enclave`, `vta-tee`) runs the whole service with third-party-checkable attestation (`spec/vta/attestation/*`). Phone secure hardware cannot run app logic |
| The hosting operator | **Phone** | In the VTA's own threat model, a KMS key administrator can obtain the seed, and the guarantee "holds within the operator's AWS-account trust root" (`docs/02-vta/tee-architecture.md`; `docs/05-design-notes/tee-dual-unlock.md` §2) |

Two caveats on the phone side: StrongBox is far slower than the TEE for bulk work, and many Android phones have none.

### E7. Wallet `main` has already moved away from this topology

- **The hosted-agent model is what ships.** Keyring ships "the phone as manager of its own VTA" (`VtaClient.ts`).
- **Rules are checked on the hosted VTA.** Approval rules are edited on the phone, but checked there (`approvalsPolicy.ts` generates the hosted VTA's policy row).
- **The wake does not fetch.** The push wake deliberately "never opens the agent, starts message pickup or reads the wallet" (`app/src/push/pushHandlers.ts`).

## Part 3 — Recommended disposition

**Recommendation: do not adopt the embedded VTA as a replacement topology.** It would mean re-implementing a large, fast-moving server (E3) on a device whose operating system will not let it run in the background (E1). Every comparable system has rejected that shape (E2), and it would still fail at what comes next, rooms and community messaging (E4).

**Keep three things from it:**

1. **A narrow "answers while you are present" capability.** The phone handles requests when the user is there: in person (QR, NFC, BLE), or through a visible "approve this?" prompt. This works well on a phone. The operating systems now host credential presentation themselves (Android's Credential Manager holder API, iOS 26 IdentityDocumentServices), even when the app is not running. Part 1's UX notes still apply if this is ever offered as an option.
2. **The phone as the holder of the critical key share.** This is where phone hardware is stronger than the cloud (E6). Upstream's draft dual-unlock design, Option C in `tee-dual-unlock.md`, recommended for high-value VTAs, has the TEE seed released only together with a device-held share. That share is unlocked by biometrics on the owner's phone after it verifies the enclave's attestation. It fits Keyring directly: the hosted VTA provides availability, and the phone provides the key share and the approvals. The proposal is to track it as a Keyring-side direction and to follow the upstream draft.
3. **Agent portability as its own question.** Part 1's U4 asks whether an agent can move between hosts and keep its identifier and grants. That matters for any move: off the Farm, between hosts, or onto a self-hosted VTA. It should be answered in the parent plan whatever happens to this subtask.

**For this subtask:** either close it with a pointer to this companion, or reframe it around points 1–3. In both cases the reasoning stays recorded here, so the topology is not re-proposed without it.
