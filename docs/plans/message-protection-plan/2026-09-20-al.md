# 2026-09-20 — Alberto

Why message protection becomes its own plan, what our client actually does
today, and the sequencing argument. Written alongside the creation of
[`../message-protection-plan.md`](../message-protection-plan.md).

## 1. The trigger

Upstream published *The Life of a Message*
(`docs.fpp.storm.ws/message-protection.html`) and shipped it across
`affinidi-tdk-rs` #828–#842. Six of those PRs cite Keyring findings by number:
#828 (VTI-29), #830 (KR-30/VTI-30), #831 (KR-05/KR-06), #833/#834/#837
(VTI-31).

## 2. The document is not what its name suggests, and that is worth recording

"Message protection" reads as a cryptographic specification. It is not: there is
no encryption, key management, signing or MAC content in it. It is a delivery
resilience contract — acceptance, refusal, queueing, handover, release,
collection, expiry.

This is recorded because the misreading is costly in a specific way: a reader
who expects crypto skims it, finds nothing familiar, and closes it — and the
seven stages are precisely the things that have already cost us two findings.

## 3. Why a plan and not a backlog item

The honest case against: we poll every second, so none of the seven stages is
hurting us today, and a list of four fixes is not obviously a plan.

The case for, which I think wins: **the mitigation and the gap are the same
decision.** The reason none of it hurts is the 1-second poll, and raising that
interval is already a live question for low-end devices and battery — it is
stashed pending a wider audience. Raise it without handling the resync signal
(#830) and we convert a latent gap into lost messages. Those two therefore have
to be decided together, by someone who can see both, which is what a plan is
for and what a backlog of four tickets actively prevents.

Second reason: the asymmetry of who pays. An unacknowledged message stays queued
**against its sender**. A recipient-side sloppiness of ours shows up as a
community unable to answer applicants — VTI-29's exact shape. Nothing in our own
instrumentation would show it, so it will not be found by noticing.

## 4. What I measured, rather than assumed

On `feat/prague-farm-membership` at `858c2fe`:

- **We discard the resync frame.** `VtiMediatorSession.startLiveDelivery`
  documents its own backstop poll with "an empty queue answers with a `status`,
  which `handleFrame` ignores." That is the frame #830 now uses to say *resync*.
  Verified in the source, not inferred from the PR.
- **We do not hot-loop on a 503.** `RetryingHttpOutboundTransport` retries only
  a `CredoError` whose cause is `Network request failed` thrown before any
  response, and rethrows everything else. I had expected to find a retry hazard
  here and there is none — the narrowness that was written for the iOS
  stale-socket bug happens to be exactly right for #835. Worth saying plainly
  because the opposite was my working assumption an hour earlier.
- **We do not read the refusal either.** Same narrowness, other edge: a
  queue-full 503 reaches the user as a generic send failure with no
  `Retry-After` honoured.

## 5. Sequencing: why M0 is a measurement and not a fix

Our lab is era D — mediator 0.28.0, built from the **#829 branch tip**, not
upstream main, which is at 0.28.9 and fourteen commits ahead. **Every behaviour
this plan responds to landed in #830–#842, none of which we have ever run.**

Writing client code against a contract we have not once observed is how a
finding gets reported that turns out to be a fixture artefact. So M0 advances
the pins and re-runs the ceremony before anything is implemented, and it carries
two pieces of unfinished business that need the same rebuild anyway: re-measuring
VTI-31 against #834, and dropping the fixture's raised
`queued_send_messages_per_peer` to find out whether #828 has retired our one
declared fixture deviation.

## 6. Why the ack gap outranks the backoff gap

Both M1 and M2 follow M0; between them I put the resync signal first.

A missed refusal costs one action, visibly, to the person who took it — they see
a failure and retry. A missed acknowledgement costs somebody else, invisibly,
and accumulates: the message stays against its sender's ceiling until an expiry
sweeper clears it, which on a 2.6-day-old queue it had not. Failures that are
paid for by another party and are invisible to the party causing them are worth
more than failures that are loud and local.

## 7. What this supersedes

Nothing. No prior position existed on the client side of this contract; the
findings doc recorded the defects we hit (VTI-29, VTI-30, VTI-31) without ever
stating what Keyring owes in return. That gap is what this plan closes.

## 8. Open, and not decided here

- **The exact wording of every quotation.** The specification was read through a
  summarising fetch, which is adequate to plan against and not adequate to quote
  upstream. Re-read directly before any of it appears in a report.
- **Whether Credo flattens a 503 before our code sees it.** M2 is conditional on
  this and M0 is where it gets checked.
- **The poll interval itself.** M3 depends on it and it is not mine to settle —
  it is stashed pending a real-event audience.
