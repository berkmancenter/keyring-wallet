# 2026-10-19 — witness disclosure audit, positions, and the public reply

Author: Brendan A. Miller (bam). Companion to
[`../witness-privacy-hardening-plan.md`](../witness-privacy-hardening-plan.md), which this
session created.

This records one working session: a review of an OpenVTC discussion about witnessing, an
audit of what our witness ceremony discloses, and the positions that came out of both.
The session began from the question "does this discussion follow the witnessing protocol,
and what is being raised?" and ended with a reply posted to that discussion and the plan
this document accompanies.

**Evidence base.** keyring-wallet `origin/main` at `e53fbd34`, which pins keyring-bifold at
`0859403d6`. Bifold paths below are relative to `bifold/packages` at that commit. The local
checkout was dirty (submodule off its pin) and behind origin, so every file was read from the
pinned blobs and nothing was executed or tested. Spec text was read from local clones of
`dtgwg-trust-tasks-tf`, `dtgwg-cred-spec` and `dtgwg-vsc-registry` at `origin/main` after a
fetch (trust-tasks `8641fd52`, cred-spec `2e3c5be`).

Notation: *verified* means read in code or spec at the pin; *inferred* means concluded from
verified parts and not confirmed by running anything. Line numbers drift and are given for

## Context: the discussion

`trustoverip/dtgwg-cred-tf` discussion #42, "Witnessing in practice: independence, witness
identity and disclosure for `vsc/witnessed/1`" (OpenVTC implementing a witness service,
tracked at OpenVTC/verifiable-trust-infrastructure#1068), posted 2026-10-15 with no replies at
the time of review. Companion discussion #43 asks how a witness revokes a VWC.

Its design: a standalone witness host service with its own DID and up to ten witness
identities; refusal of sessions naming the witness's own or excluded DIDs; at submit, a
membership credential from a community the witness serves; automatic minting; no
zero-knowledge path; an attestation record only. Its five questions: witness independence,
a dedicated witness identifier, a minimal-disclosure path, recording eligibility in
`witnessContext`, and revocation.

Verified conformance to the current Trust Tasks specs (per-party sessions, signed responses,
witness as recipient, VWC bound to the session by `taskContext` and digest, the witness
discarding the presentation). Gaps against them: it names `witness/session` and
`witness/session/submit` 0.1 while its membership gate needs `memberPresentation`, which is in
0.2 (committed 2026-10-16, a day after the post); it does not state that a member DID must not
enter the VWC or logs; it does not mention `parentThreadId`. Nobody had raised an objection.

## Finding 1 — the original draft is the origin, not a stale file

`witnessed_vrc_flow.md` in `dtgwg-cred-tf` is the origin of the flow; it was carried into
`packages/vrc-reference/WITNESSED_FLOW.md` and has since evolved through the Trust Tasks
specs, the VSC registry and the keyring implementation. A first reading described it as
superseded; that was an inference, and the lineage is the better framing. It is stated in the
public reply.

## Finding 2 — what the implementation has that the discussion does not

**Evidence (verified).**
- Per-party sessions with a fresh single-use challenge (16 random bytes) and domain, in
  `witness-server/src/trustTasks/WitnessTaskSessions.ts`, closing a shared-challenge flaw the
  legacy dialect still has.
- An id-only `taskContext` binding is forgeable (ref-06w3); `taskDigestMultibase` fixed it and
  was adopted upstream.
- Clock skew: a 296 ms client lag made wallets reject the VWC as "before `validFrom`", so the
  wallet withheld its witness-share and the peer had no badge. The witness backdates
  `validFrom` by five minutes (`c896c830f`).
- A holder-controlled `vrc/relationships/witness-share/0.1` task replaced witness-side
  cross-distribution.
- Locality evidence as an optional BLE leg with three explicit outcomes and a policy
  discoverable through trust-task discovery.
- Extension members hoisted out of `witnessContext` so the registry can schema it and flat
  terms support quad-level disclosure (vsc-migration-plan §3.5).
- A dedicated, stable witness issuer DID separate from per-connection DIDs.

**Not in the implementation:** any membership gating, allowlist or independence check (any
party the wallet scans can witness); `memberPresentation`; revocation beyond the seven-day
expiry; spec 0.2 (the code targets 0.1).

## Finding 3 — what the ceremony discloses (audit at the pin, reporting off)

Defaults (verified): wallet `useWitnessing=true`, `useLocalityConfirmation=true`,
`useHardwareAttestation=false`; witness `localityPolicy='offered'`, shape `vsc`,
`retainMessages=false`, TLS off. `enableReporting` and `WITNESS_REPORTING_ENABLED` default on
for testing and are off in production by decision (below); this audit assumes them off.

The Trust Task path writes no registry or activity-log record and deletes its sessions after
submit (verified by grep of the ceremony and index files). The exposure is mostly the legacy
dialect, the witness's own logs, and what the signed artifacts carry onward.

### High

**H1 — legacy dialect publishes a live map of both relationship DIDs; reporting-off does not
suppress it.** Each legacy VWC issuance records `{vwcId, sessionId, vrcDigest, vrcIssuerId,
recipientDid, issuedAt, eventName}` (`WitnessService.ts` ~2474-2491; `recipientDid` is the
other party's connection DID with the witness). It is served unauthenticated by
`/api/issued`, `/log` and the `/ws` initial state and live push (`WebServer.ts` ~187-268;
`NetworkBroadcaster.ts` ~380), all with CORS `*`. The forwarding gate is
`config.witnessService?.reportingGraph`, an object that is always constructed
(`WitnessService.ts` ~428), so the gate is always true. Two records per session share a
`sessionId`; joined, they give both R-DIDs and both connection DIDs, and a wallet's connection
DID recurs across its sessions. Live push with timestamps lets a venue observer tie taps to
pairs. Documented in the README as an activity log, not as a privacy exposure.

**H2 — the device hardware key is stable and reaches the witness by default.** Verified: one
Android alias `vrc_hardware_signing_key` and one iOS tag/keychain id; `ensureHardwareKey` has
no per-relationship parameter; the attestation chain is cached by public key. With hardware
attestation on, `evidence.hardwareBinding.publicKey` and the chain go into the VRC before the
R-DID signs it — contradicting the VRC's own `issuerScope: 'pairwise'`. With locality on
(default), the same key goes to the witness as `devicePublicKey` in the submit transcript. The
witness can compare keys across sessions; it does not persist them (not in the registry), and
nothing technical stops it.

**H3 — the witness binds both R-DIDs to a persistent connection before the user commits, and
logs them.** The session opens on an unauthenticated request (`proofPolicy 'acceptUnverified'`)
and logs `Task session … opened for parties [R-DID, R-DID]` at the default level
(`WitnessTaskSessions.ts` ~387, ~455). The connection record keeps the wallet's label and DID
(`WitnessService.ts` ~1195-1200). The mapping from R-DID pair to a wallet is inherent in the
design and cannot be removed without wallet-side change.

**H4 — the witness-share bundle carries stable identifiers.** The bundle's `initiating` is the
session document (issuer: the wallet's connection DID to the witness; recipient: the witness's
connection DID); `terminal` is the submit response (`witnessCeremony.ts` ~324-333;
`outcomeEvidence.ts` ~139-141, ~286). The pair is the same across all of that wallet's
exchanges at that witness. The terminal's `ext` carries the whole `observation` including
`deviceKeyId`, round-trip time and signal strength (`WitnessTaskSessions.ts` ~611-621, ~672);
a code comment says "artifact side only", but the artifact is what is shipped to the peer.
This contradicts locality-plan §7.1 rule 3 and §9.1, and §7.2 ("a default show must never need
the artifact side opened"). Sending is automatic, gated only by `isWitnessingPreferred`.

### Medium

- **M1 — what a VWC verifier learns.** `credentialSubject.parties` carries both R-DIDs; the
  subject id, `witnessName`, event and `witnessContext.sessionId`; the locality members (venue,
  sensor DID, millisecond observation time, window, round-trip time, signal strength,
  commitment); `validFrom` (issue minus five minutes) with `validUntil` (issue plus seven days),
  which together give the exact issuance time; `issuerScope: 'directed'` while the witness DID
  is public, against the VSC plan's "our witness declares nothing" (§3.6). The proof is not
  selectively disclosable (no bbs derive code exists), so a show is all or nothing.
- **M2 — legacy error path logs whole messages.** Handlers run inside the `try` that wraps
  the JSON parse, so any handler exception falls into the plain-text branch that logs the full
  content (VP, VRC, evidence) and, with the LLM option on, sends it to a third-party provider
  (`WitnessService.ts` ~1341-1366).
- **M3 — legacy persistence beyond the README (inferred from Credo behaviour).**
  `offerCredential` creates exchange and message records in the witness's wallet holding the
  VWC with the recipient connection; `retainMessages=false` clears only basic messages. The
  in-memory relationship registry is never pruned.
- **M4 — retention and verbose settings** keep or log full VPs and VRCs on the legacy path.
- **M5 — legacy `session-request` is unauthenticated and spoofable** (an existence oracle). On
  the Trust Task path, submit checks only that the VP holder is in `parties`; nothing ties
  `parties` to the VRC, so a party can obtain a VWC naming a third party (a forged edge, not a
  disclosure).
- **M6 — public endpoints:** the verification endpoint is an existence and signature oracle;
  `/api/mock/*` are unauthenticated POSTs with CORS `*`; the activity page builds rows with
  `innerHTML` from unescaped DIDs; plain HTTP and WS by default.
- **M7 — wallet defaults** send locality and witness-share without a prompt.

### Low

- **L1 — reporting residue with reporting off.** The legacy VP's `reportingDid` is logged and
  stored in `session.receivedReportingDids` ungated, and `recordReportingEdge` is called
  ungated (`WitnessService.ts` ~1586-1617; `index.ts` ~209-215), so `/ws` and `/network` can
  show edges between reporting DIDs.
- **L2 —** the Trust Task ten-minute TTL is not enforced at submit; expiry runs only when the
  next session opens.
- **L3 —** failure logs echo up to 1,200 characters of validation text and the BLE device MAC.
- **L4 — documents contradict the code:** the README's Redis registry variables are not wired
  (the registry is hard-coded in-memory; wiring Redis with its default TTL of 0 would hold the
  same data indefinitely); "reporting graph … disabled unless users actively enable it"; "no
  message history is retained"; plan statements listed above.

### Not leaks (verified)

R-DIDs are `did:peer:0` with a fresh key per counterparty and no service endpoint, mediator or
reused key; the BLE advertisement is derived per session from the challenge; DIDComm payloads
are encrypted, so a mediator sees timing and size.

## Finding 4 — removing `deviceKeyId` and `parties` does not break attestation integrity

**`deviceKeyId`, round-trip time and signal strength (verified).** `deviceKeyId` appears in
exactly two places, the type (`locality.ts` ~272) and the assignment
(`WitnessTaskSessions.ts` ~618); no verifier reads it. The transcript digest is a sha256 over
the JCS of the whole transcript including the device key, signature and hardware attestation;
the VWC's `localityEvidenceCommitment` is that digest; neither needs `observation.deviceKeyId`
to exist, and both are salted by the challenge and sensor nonce. The device signs only the
binding `{context, taskDigestMultibase, challenge, sensorNonce, sensorDid}`; the key is the
verification key, not signed bytes. The wallet reads only `confirmed`, `method`, `reason` and
`transcriptDigestMultibase` from the observation and checks the digest against the transcript
its own device produced. No code compares round-trip time to its bound. What is lost is a
hypothetical check by a third party holding the VRC that `deviceKeyId` equals the VRC's
hardware key; the shipped bundle carries no transcript, so no third party can verify the device
signature today. The witness's own signature over its predicates and the commitment is the trust
anchor in every current flow.

**Caveat (inferred from verified parts).** The same key is already in the VRC's hardware
evidence, so the counterparty already holds it; stripping it protects only a third party shown
the terminal document or anyone the peer forwards it to. Hiding it from VRC holders needs a
per-relationship or per-ceremony key.

**Related finding.** The witness never verifies the hardware attestation chain; the
`hardwareAttestation: verified|present-unverified|absent` member is a device self-report copied
into the VWC, though locality-plan §7.3 says to verify at the verifier and never trust a client
self-report.

**`parties` (verified).** The witness uses `parties` at session open (exactly two entries),
stores and logs it, and at submit requires the submit proof and the VP holder to be one of the
parties. The specs define `parties` in the session request (required, exactly two `did:`
strings, deliberately a join key); the VWC is defined as the subject id, predicate, object
digest, optional `witnessContext`, `taskContext` and `taskDigestMultibase`, with no `parties`;
the `witnessed/1` registry profile defines only `witnessContext`; cred-spec says verifiers
ignore members a profile does not define. The witness emits `parties` with one unconditional
line (`WitnessTaskSessions.ts` ~650). The wallet reads `parties` only in the inbound-share party
check, which already falls back to the session document (`ceremony.ts` ~1373-1379;
`witnessShare.test.ts` covers a VWC without it), and a wallet comment at ~1361-1364 already
says a witnessed/1 VWC carries none. Withholding after issuance is not possible: no selective
disclosure code exists. The session document that the bundle must carry names both R-DIDs
regardless.

## Decisions taken this session

1. **Independence from the community is not required.** A community is authoritative over its
   own governance; a witness must not be a party to the event it witnesses; independence beyond
   that is verifier policy. Why independence is sometimes valued (conflict of interest,
   collusion, cross-community trust) is covered by the non-party rule and verifier policy, so it
   does not justify a requirement.
2. **Relationship tracking by a witness is opt-in only.** Reporting is a test aid, off in
   production builds. This makes the audit's reporting-residue items (L1) and the always-on
   legacy feeds (H1) defects against the principle even with reporting off.
3. **Two kinds of witness are distinguished** — a community witness, and a third-party or
   professional witness for hire — as described in the plan's design positions.
4. **Do not publish keyring's internals in the discussion thread.** The reply states
   principles and points to the open-source code.
5. **The plan is proposed; nothing is implemented.** Phase 0 is unblocked, and its order is in the plan.

## Positions posted publicly

Reply on discussion #42:
<https://github.com/trustoverip/dtgwg-cred-tf/discussions/42#discussioncomment-19049267>,
posted 2026-10-19 under the author's account. It states the lineage (the witnessed flow began
as the `witnessed_vrc_flow.md` draft, carried forward through the Trust Tasks specs, the VSC
registry and a working implementation built with Alberto, still evolving); asks which of 0.1
and 0.2 implementers should target; distinguishes the two witness kinds; and takes positions
on the five questions — a witness must not be a party, independence is verifier policy
(Q1); a dedicated identifier, with issuer, recipient and response signer one identity (Q2);
plain disclosure that the witness sees the credential in full, and no stable cross-relationship
identifiers in evidence presented to a witness (Q3); gentle pushback on membership gating,
preferring verifier-side policy, discarding the membership proof and recording a policy
identifier in `witnessContext` (Q4); and short validity with no status list as a legitimate
revocation approach (Q5/#43). It also proposes that a witness retain only the attestation
record by default with tracking opt-in, a minimal VWC, a stated clock-skew tolerance, and
that digest binding fixes a forgeable id-only binding. It links the open-source flow at
`0859403d6` (`vrc-reference/src/Witness.ts`, `witnessedFlow.test.ts`, `WITNESSED_FLOW.md`,
`witness-server/`, `WitnessTaskSessions.ts`, `credentialBuilder.ts`) and says additional
security and privacy hardening is planned.

The reply deliberately names no default or exposure of ours. It leaves out the witness-server
README (which contradicts the code, Finding 3 L4) and points at `WitnessTaskSessions.ts`, not
the legacy `WitnessService.ts`.

## Positions this supersedes

- A first reading that `witnessed_vrc_flow.md` is stale and superseded — replaced by the
  lineage framing (Finding 1).
- The README's description of reporting as opt-in and of the witness as retaining no message
  history — contradicted by the code (Finding 3 H1, L1, L4).
- The locality plan's rule that the device key id stays on the artifact side and the witness
  learns nothing new (§7.1 rule 3, §9.1) — contradicted by H2 and H4; the plan's design
  positions now state the actual limit.
- The VSC plan's "our witness declares nothing" (§3.6) — contradicted by
  `issuerScope: 'directed'`; left as a decision in the plan.
