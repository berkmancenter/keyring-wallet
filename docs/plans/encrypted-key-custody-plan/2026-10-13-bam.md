# Encrypted key custody — filing into Keyring (2026-10-13, BAM)

**Status: Draft.** Plan: [`../encrypted-key-custody-plan.md`](../encrypted-key-custody-plan.md).
Research it was built from: [`2026-10-06-bam.md`](./2026-10-06-bam.md).

The 2026-10-06 note was written outside the repository, with eight open
questions, one of which was whether it belongs to keyring-wallet at all. It was
filed here as a draft plan by decision of its owner. This document records what
the repository answered when the note was read against it, on `main` at

## F1. The note's Q7 — it builds on Keyring

**Concluded.** Yes, as a draft plan. Decision of the plan's owner, 2026-10-13.
**Supersedes** the note's open Q7.

## F2. Q1 and Q3 — issuer keys Keyring operates; holder keys stay on the phone

**Evidence.**

- Keyring's holder-side identity is hardware-attested. `pnm_cnm_subtask.md`
  §9 Q2 frames the choice as a Secure-Enclave key against a VTA-minted one;
  `keyring-on-the-vta-farm.md` §3.2 resolves it *"in favour of hardware
  custody, on this path"*; `vta-carriage_subtask.md` §3 keeps the Relationship
  DID off the VTA in its v1 default.
- The attestation key cannot be a signing key for anything else: on iOS it is
  an App Attest key whose assertion signs authenticator data
  (`keyring-on-the-vta-farm/2026-09-25-cd.md`, citing `ios/Attestation.mm:395-496`).
- The witness-server holds its VWC issuer key whole, as an Ed25519 seed or key
  file on its host (`bifold/packages/witness-server/README.md`,
  `WITNESS_ISSUER_SEED`, `WITNESS_ISSUER_KEY_FILE`, `"keyType": "Ed25519"`).
- `vta-carriage_subtask.md` open question 7 recommends, if an autonomous tier
  is built, a registered companion agent holding its *own new key* — a key that
  must sign while the phone is offline.

**Concluded.** The first candidate is the witness issuer key. The second is the
autonomous-tier key, conditional on that open question. Moving the Relationship
DID or attestation keys into a cloud signer is ruled out, because it gives up
the attestation the custody positions above were taken to keep.

**Supersedes** the note's framing of holder keys as a primary use (its §5 and
§9 item 7). Under Keyring, holder signing is the narrower, conditional case.
The note's Q3 stays open only for third-party ecosystems (EU wallets, mdoc),
which are not in scope.

## F3. Q2 — Ed25519, so FROST(Ed25519)

**Evidence.** *"Keyring's signatures are Ed25519 only. Its Trust Task proofs
and JWS are `eddsa-jcs-2022` / `EdDSA`, and its verifier rejects other
suites"* (`2026-09-25-cd.md`, citing `packages/trust-tasks/src/documentProof.ts:218, 242, 326, 494`).
The witness key is Ed25519. The only P-256 keys are the hardware keys F2
excludes.

**Concluded.** FROST(Ed25519, SHA-512), RFC 9591. Threshold ECDSA is not
needed unless a P-256 consumer is added. **Supersedes** the note's "FROST or
threshold ECDSA, depending on curve" (its §9 item 1).

## F4. Q4 — the phone can approve

**Evidence.** The phone as approver is `pnm_cnm_subtask.md` P1. DTTE's gated
task *"parks for up to 15 minutes waiting on a device's decision"*
(`vta-carriage_subtask.md` §1).

**Concluded.** Yes. The note's "one-tap approval from a held device" has an
existing loop to reuse.

## F5. Q5 — partly answered

**Evidence.** `WITNESS_ISSUER_DID` accepts `did:key`, `did:web` and `did:peer`
(witness-server README). Of these, only `did:web` rotates keys.

**Concluded.** The note's delegation design (§5 item 5) needs `did:web` or a
similar rotatable method for the witness. Which DID a quorum-held witness uses
is left open.

## F6. The TSP ports already take an external signer

**Evidence.** `tsp-reference/ref-09-tsp-core-ports/README.md` item 4: *"The
port needs no further change to accept a real custody-boundary backend"*.
`ref-10` runs the same ports against two real Askar wallets.

**Concluded.** A threshold signer plugs in as one more `SigningKey` adapter.
It does not provide key agreement, so the plan scopes it to signing.
