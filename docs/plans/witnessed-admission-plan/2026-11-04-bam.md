## Part A. The question, and the decisions taken

**The question.** How can a community configure its VTC to admit a member automatically only when they hold a witnessed relationship credential from a specified witness — extended to one, two or three such credentials, with optional Bluetooth confirmation and optional secure biometric signing from the device — and what has to change in VTI and in this repository?

| # | Decision | Reasoning | Where |
|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | The counterparty is the attester; the witness is the helper and token issuer | A witness-as-attester makes every exchange with one applicant the same tag, so *k* counts witnesses (Part C.3) | Plan §2, §3.2 |
| D2 | The community may learn which witness issued the tokens | A hidden witness would need a shared "federation" helper; not worth the machinery for an MVP | Plan §2.2 |
| D3 | Distinct counting is guaranteed within one witness only | Across witnesses it depends on each attester deriving one key per community; accepted | Plan §4.1, §8 |

| D5 | The attestation is made in the exchange's session; deferred attestation is dropped | The point of a witness is a live record of the meeting | Plan §3.5 |
| D6 | The witness runs PCS as a **Rust sidecar** | Native arkworks speed (WASM speed unmeasured), the issuer secret stays out of the Node process, and one process serialises blind signing | Plan §6 |
| D7 | Experimental pilot behind the existing feature flag; external cryptographic review before production | PCS is unaudited ("Research artifact. Not audited"); the VTC feature `vetting-pcs` is off by default | Plan §7 Phase 7 |

Fine-grained labels shrink anonymity sets | Plan §4.1, §8 |
| D10 | The criteria digest is bound at **prove time only**, not at attest time | A criteria edit must not void attestations that cost a real meeting. **Conditional**: needs the `StatementMeta` change in `vti-vetting-pcs` (`meta.rs:20`), which also affects existing hidden vetting | Plan §5 |

## Part B. Findings, by investigation

### B.1 The community's side of admission today

- **Join criteria are the admission policy** (`docs/03-vtc/join-criteria.md`; `vtc-service/src/schemas/accepts.rs:127-170`, evaluation in `join/criteria.rs`). A criterion has `admission` (`automatic` or `review`), a DCQL `query`, `credentialIssuers` (`community`, `recognised`, `any` — `accepts.rs:99-110`), `invitationRequired` and `vetting`. The first criterion met, in listed order, governs; the default policy `policies/default/join.rego` allows when the criterion is met and automatic.
- **Witnessed VRCs.** A VRC is the relationship credential; a VWC is a `StatementCredential` with predicate `https://registry.trustoverip.org/dtg/vsc/witnessed/1`, issued by the witness, subject = the VRC issuer, `credentialSubject.object.digestMultibase` = the VRC's digest (`docs/03-vtc/personhood-and-graph.md:71-100`). `assess()` (`credentials/witness.rs:393-414`) checks, in order: the issuer is in the community's trusted witness list, the session's outcome evidence pairs with the VWC, and the digest binds to a VRC held in the graph.
- **Trusted witnesses.** `[witnesses] trusted = [{did, label}]` or the runtime key `witnesses.trusted` (`credentials/trusted_witnesses.rs`, `config.rs:118-126`). The default is empty, which trusts no one. There is no per-criterion witness.
- **DCQL cannot count.** `match_credentials` (affinidi-openid4vp 0.1.3, the only source available offline; 0.1.4 is not on the machine) succeeds when each query matches at least once; `multiple` defaults to false and breaks after the first match (`dcql.rs:178-193, 601-663`). N / k-of-n over distinct witnesses is code, not configuration. A claim path over the whole embedded `ldp_vc` can pin `issuer` and `credentialSubject.predicate` (`criteria.rs:779-808`), but only for a string `issuer`.

### B.2 Why a witnessed VRC cannot satisfy a join today

Three independent blockers, found in two rounds (the second round found the second and third):

1. **No outcome evidence on the join paths.** `evidence: &[]` is passed unconditionally at `criteria.rs:739` and `routes/join_requests/present.rs:~372`; `witness-host-service.md:325` says "the join paths carry no evidence yet"; only `vtc/members/personhood/assert/0.2` carries it, reads it from `payload.outcomeEvidence` (`trust_tasks/mod.rs:6170`) and does not persist it.
2. **The edge must be in `relationships_ks`, and an applicant cannot publish it.** `resolve_binding` (`witness.rs:241-268`) scans stored relationships. `relationships.rego:60-80` allows publishing only for a current member; the attributed form also hard-checks the subject (`routes/relationships.rs:386-401`).
3. **The wallet's VRC issuer is not the join persona.** `vrc-manager.ts:535-585` mints a per-peer `did:peer:0` and `ceremony.ts:1283-1335` signs the VRC with it; the VWC subject equals it (`credentialBuilder.ts:176, 250-251`); the join applicant is the persona (`vtiJoin.ts:80-105`). The community needs them equal (`SubjectMismatch`).

The wallet also cannot answer any credential criterion (`screens/joinWays.ts:194-198`; `vtiJoin.ts:~154` sends invitations only), and the witness-server's DID handling has its own mismatches with the community's checks: Trust Task replies are signed with the connection DID while the VWC issuer is `issuerDid` (`WitnessTaskSessions.ts:282-303, 362`), and `session.issuer` / `outcome.recipient` are the wrapper's connection DIDs where the community needs the VWC subject (`witnessCeremony.ts:329, 382`). No test feeds a keyring-minted VWC through the community's `assess`.

### B.3 Is a spec change required? (investigated because the first report asserted it)

No. `submit/0.3` has `additionalProperties:false` at the top level, but `vp` is a free object, `extensions` is an opaque object and `ext` is an open reverse-DNS bag (`dtgwg-trust-tasks-tf/website/specs/vtc/join-requests/submit/0.3/payload.schema.json`); `hiddenVetting` already rides `extensions` (`vetting/pcs.rs:475`, wallet `vtiAgent.ts:1985`). A typed member in submit/supplement 0.4 is the proper vehicle for interoperability (the spec calls `extensions` opaque and forbids moving claims into it that would not go in `vp`), not a precondition. VTI-CMP-023 says evidence "MUST travel with the presentation"; stored or fetched evidence satisfies VTI-CMP-022 but arguably not 023.

Binding the VWC to a presented VRC rather than a stored one is spec-compliant (DTG Credentials, security considerations, "Digest integrity": the verifier "must have the referenced credential available, recompute the digest over its JCS canonical form with the top-level `proof` removed, and confirm it matches"; also "Community membership is not a precondition for issuing, holding, or presenting a VRC"). That was recommended as option (a) before the design moved to PCS (Part C.1).

### B.4 Zero knowledge in VTI

VTI contains **no** prover over Ed25519/JCS credentials. The only zero knowledge in VTI is hidden vetting (PCS): sigma protocols over Pointcheval–Sanders signatures on BLS12-381 with a Tag_DDH PRF, no circuit, no trusted setup, suite `ps-ddh-bls12381`. `CredentialFormat::Zkp` (`vta-vault/src/model.rs:69`) is a placeholder for a Circom/Groth16 design that is "Phase-0-gated" and deferred (`docs/05-design-notes/vti-credential-architecture.md:174-185`); the witness docs say "no ZKP presentation is implemented" (`docs/06-witness/README.md:377-383`).

Measured elsewhere in this tree (results read, code not): `zk-ed25519-bench` — RISC Zero proving one simplified Ed25519 verify about 259–808 s and about 9 GiB, two verifies 516–1349 s; SP1 out of memory at a 20 GB limit; `longfellow-bench` — stock mdoc-zk (ECDSA P-256) about 0.71 s prove, 0.40 s verify, 360 KB, 220 MiB, with a custom-circuit extrapolation of 2.7–7.7 s and 0.7–2.1 GiB that is **an estimate for a different statement**. Longfellow's circuits are ECDSA-oriented; Ed25519 plus JCS parsing would need a new hand-written circuit. PCS numbers (design doc, Apple Silicon): attestation about 2.8 ms, 240 B; k=5 proof 1392 B, about 11.6 ms to prove and 11.5 ms to verify. No phone or memory figures exist.

### B.5 The PCS reuse analysis

The role shift in the plan (§2) was proposed by a design review that read the PCS code and docs. The decisive facts:

- A tag `T_j = Tag(usk_j, H0(id))` is deterministic per (attester, applicant) and "k distinct" means k distinct attester keys (`types.rs:293-305`; design doc §1). With the witness as attester every exchange for one applicant yields the same tag and is rejected as `DuplicateAttester`.
- `Issuer::derive(community, secret)` already takes the community (`issuer.rs:91`), has `issue_root` (`:173`) and blind token signing (`token.rs`). The VTA's attester calls exist: `vta/pcs/root-request|root-accept|tokens|attest` (`vta-service/src/trust_tasks/pcs.rs:376-455`; `vetter.rs:389-422`), and the wallet drives them (`hiddenVetterAdapter.ts`, `hiddenVetterRounds.ts`). `Verifier::new(params, hvk, tokens, live_periods)` takes the helper key and the token verifier as arguments (`verifier.rs:~100`).
- A token is a PS blind signature over `(serial, φ(label))` (`token.rs:345-357`, `ps.rs:186-199`); the serial and nothing about the member is in the token, so a token is a bearer object.
- Effort for the whole vision was estimated at about 13–20 engineer-weeks, 7–10 for a single-witness MVP without flags.

### B.7 Checks made before coding

- **The `predicate-credential-system` dependency.** It is published on crates.io (0.1.0, 2026-09-28, not yanked, about 8.4k downloads), one individual owner and no team. `Cargo.lock:8258-8262` records registry source and checksum `ca56c675…`, which matches the local cache; `src/` is byte-identical to the sibling repo's. Its README line "The crate is not on crates.io" (`README.md:71`) is stale and was published inside the tarball. MSRV 1.95, edition 2024. The test-only `test-utils` feature exposes an insecure hash-to-group oracle; no non-dev dependency enables it. Consumers elsewhere in the tree depend on `version = "0.1"`.
- **The tick** is a `u32` opaque key in `vti-vetting-pcs` (`issuer.rs:45-57, 187-226, 287-295`; `token.rs:99-118, 305-342`), not a PCS concept (the library never mentions it); tokens do not carry it; spend reveals neither tick nor member. The community's tick is a time window (`pcs.rs:131-183`, default P3D) that is community policy, not a library invariant. Once-per-`(member, label, tick)` is enforced by an in-memory served set (`token.rs:202, 319-325, 339`) that is lost on restart, with the caller's store the first guard (`pcs_issue.rs:382-388, 687-790`). Quota is per call only (`issuer.rs:205-210`). So a witness can issue one token per exchange using a fresh `member` with `tick = 0`; it must persist its own record and return the original answer on retry, because `AlreadyServedThisTick` after a lost response permanently burns that tick.
- **The VTA is not hard-wired to the community.** Every `vta/pcs/*` payload carries the community's published `parameters`; `community_params` (`operations/pcs.rs:381-413`) reads `suite`, `helperKey`, `tokenKey`, `vetterLabels`, `tokenLabels`, `dripPerTick`. The VTA has no outbound path to the helper — handlers are local transforms and the wallet relays — so the witness endpoint is the wallet's to choose. The community string must be the community's DID (`scheme.rs:35-41`; the proof audience is `params.community()`, `applicant.rs:206`). Member binding is caller-supplied (`operations/pcs.rs:575, 585`).
- **Persona proof.** `id_binding` is still an unkeyed SHA-256 stand-in over public values (`meta.rs:57-93`, `wire.rs:60-62`, `verifier.rs:174-183`). Transport authentication covers `extensions` (`handle_submit`, `trust_tasks/mod.rs:3141-3197`, then `check_presentation_holder`, `orchestrate.rs:~243`). The community does not bind the PCS id to the persona directly: the single-use challenge is keyed to the authenticated caller (`pcs_challenge.rs:52-53, 159`) and bound into the proof context (`verifier.rs:185-197`). `join_did` is **never compared** to the authenticated applicant. Sibling `extensions` members are covered by the envelope but not by the ZK proof.
- **Wallet readiness.** Hidden vetting is implemented on both sides and unit-tested, with a device e2e leg (`e2e/gate/legs/hv.sh`, `gate.sh:45`). **No recorded green run was found**; the last gate-hardening commits are 2026-10-17 to 10-24 (#608). The community used is `keyring-hv-lab1` on `*.ic3.dev`; the local stack has no PCS setup (grep of `scripts/openvtc/local-vti-stack/*`, inferred). The wallet has no PCS cryptography (`@bifold/react-native-pcs` is parked); the VTA proves. An applicant needs `pcs-applicant`, derived only for `admin` or `initiator` at vta-service 0.60. The wallet's courier pattern (the phone carries the agent's request to the issuer and back) already exists in `hiddenVetterRounds.ts`.
- **iOS BLE.** The peripheral README still says "type-checked only"; `locality-plan.md` records the first live iOS runs (App Attest assertions 77/63/72 ms, within the 400 ms RTT bound, 2026-09-13 companion). Still unproven per the README: foreground advertising (a backgrounded iPhone hides the UUID), and the RTT bound is provisional. Later runs were not checked.

### B.8 The VTC publish path (found last, and it decided the question "wallet and bifold only?")

`HiddenVettingConfig` holds `hvk` and `tvk` on the stored criterion (`vetting/pcs.rs:100-103`), and verification reads them from there (`:538-549`). They enter a criterion only through `pcs_issue::publish` (`pcs_issue.rs:420-445`), which derives them from the community's own master secret; `pcs_issue::issuer` (`:464-475`) and `routes/vetting_hidden.rs:215-250` refuse a criterion whose stored keys differ from the derived ones. A witness's keys therefore cannot be published today. **The VTC change is the single required external change** (plan §5). The remainder of `vetting_hidden.rs` was not read; the one-week estimate is inferred.

## Part C. Positions superseded in this investigation

1. **"Present the witnessed VRC and VWC at the join" (the first answer).** The first report gave a path: a `witnessedRelationships` criterion with per-requirement witnesses, outcome evidence carried in submit/supplement (spec 0.3 to 0.4), and a wallet that attaches the VRC, VWC and evidence. It was replaced by the PCS design because: the three blockers of B.2 must all be fixed; the DID alignment either makes the persona the VRC issuer (exposing counterparties to the community and giving the witness a stable identifier for every exchange) or needs a relationship-to-persona linkage that does not exist; and a ZK alternative over these credentials is infeasible (B.4). A tentative recommendation to bind a VWC to a VRC *presented with the join* (option (a), not pre-member publish) was correct as far as it went; it is not needed once no VRC is presented.
2. **"A spec change is required."** Replaced by B.3: it is the right vehicle, not a precondition.
3. **"The witness becomes a PCS vetter."** Raised by the user's wish to reuse the hard ZK parts; rejected after code reading (B.5): *k* would count witnesses.
4. **Short token validity keeps same-session attestation honest.** Wrong. The attester chooses the attestation's `valid_from`/`valid_until` (`verifier.rs:224`); a token's day label bounds when tokens are issued, not when attestations are made (confirmed by the spike test `attester_chosen_valid_from_is_not_checked_against_issuance`). Acceptable once tokens are bound to the applicant.
5. **A witness-side liveness acknowledgement for the attestation.** Proposed as "the witness requires the attester's VTA to return a commitment before the session closes". Evaluated and rejected: the token must exist before the attestation (`vetter.rs:410-422`, `meta.rs:34-36`); a hash the witness cannot check proves nothing; anything over the serial or metadata is linkable; a blind receipt proves no more than the token; a hard gate adds round trips through the mediator to a cloud VTA in the middle of a BLE session. The binding of the token to the applicant is what removes the attacks a receipt targeted.
6. **"Phase 2 library extension" for binding the token to the applicant.** Replaced: the construction works in the wrapper alone (Part D) and moves into Phase 1 of the work it affects, not a separate PCS change.
7. **"The VTA's root and token paths may be hard-wired to the community."** Open when the design was first costed; resolved the other way (B.7). The only VTA work is the applicant's token wallet and export/import, needed for binding only.
8. **"`predicate-credential-system = \"0.1\"` may not resolve."** The lockfile is right and the README stale (B.7).
9. **"Bind the proof, not the attestation" (decision D10, first form).** Proposed as a small rewording; the spike showed the digest binds at **both** attest time (`StatementMeta.requirements_digest`, `meta.rs:20`, into `ctx_j`) and prove time (`id_binding`, `applicant.rs:198`, recomputed `verifier.rs:174`), so existing attestations count zero after a criteria change and the attester needs a fresh token. D10 therefore needs a (small) `StatementMeta` change in the VTI crate, which also alters existing hidden vetting; it stays conditional on the maintainers' agreement.
10. **"A hash-to-field helper is exposed."** Not true; it is trivial in the wrapper (`sha2` and `ark_ff::PrimeField::from_be_bytes_mod_order` are dependencies already).
11. **"Does VTI contain a JCS zero-knowledge proof system?"** (the user's premise). It does not (B.4); the closest thing is PCS, which does not touch `eddsa-jcs-2022` credentials.
12. **"Wallet and bifold only."** Replaced by plan §5: one VTC change is required.

## Part D. The Phase 0 spike

Branch `spike/pcs-witness-helper` in the VTI clone (worktree, local, uncommitted), base `098be289`. One new file, `vti-vetting-pcs/tests/witness_helper_spike.rs`, with a `mod bound` of spike helpers. **No source change in `vti-vetting-pcs` or in PCS.** Re-run (shared-tree rules: `umask 002`, `nice -n 10`, target and temp dirs under `/var/tmp`):

```
cargo test --offline -p vti-vetting-pcs --features harness --test witness_helper_spike
```

First offline build about 3 minutes 18 seconds; a warm rebuild about a second. 15 tests, 0 failed, 0 ignored.

**The central claim holds.** A witness `W` derived with `Issuer::derive(C, secret)` issues roots and tokens; three attester engines each draw one token; an applicant proves *n* = 3; and `Verifier::new(params, hvk_W, TokenVerifier(tvk_W), periods)` accepts, with all three vetters counted and no attester named in the facts. It works because `deployment_label` depends only on the community, so the parameters are shared and `hvk` and `tvk` are only inputs.

**Negative and boundary cases.**

- The same attester twice: both attestations carry the same tag; the engine dedupes silently (usable = 1); forcing both into one proof through the library gives `Error::DuplicateAttester` (`construction.rs:848`).
- Another witness `W'`: an attester under a `W'` root is rejected on receipt (`AttestationRejected`); a full `W'`-world proof at `W`'s verifier gives `ProofRejected(InvalidAttestation)` and is accepted at `W'`'s own verifier; a `W` root with a `W'` token is rejected on receipt; a verifier with `hvk_W` and another `tvk` marks every statement `no-token`.
- A replayed serial: `token-double-spend`, count 2, one anomaly recorded (`MemoryLedger`; the `SpentLedger` trait is the hook and the community must namespace it per witness).
- A label outside the live set: the issuer refuses with `LabelNotLive`; a verifier lacking the label marks every statement `no-token`; after `close_label` all statements fail `no-token`.
- Flags: `token/<epoch>/ble` signs, verifies and is spent with no library change; `scheme.rs:44`'s `vetter/{period}` accepts `2026-11/hw` as an opaque period. A `participant/` prefix is impossible without a change. `Verifier` does not enforce a required flag — it checks only that the label is live; the wrapper counts by `sub.statements[].meta.token_label` (public and bound into the attestation). The AllowList is per proof, so "k of n must be hardware" cannot be expressed that way.
- Token redirection **was possible**: nothing in the token or the drip request names the applicant, so an attester spent all three tokens on another applicant `C`, who was admitted while the intended applicant held nothing.

**Applicant binding (`mod bound`).** `s = SHA-512("tok-bind", community, id_A text, r)` reduced mod the field order; the applicant builds `C = g^ρ · Y1^s` with an opening proof; the unchanged `Issuer::issue_tokens` blind-signs; the applicant unblinds and re-randomises; the attester runs `attest_with_token`; the verifier wrapper checks `serial == H(community, id_text, r)` before `Verifier::submit`. The PS message is `(serial, φ(label))` (`token.rs:345-357`), the wallet draws the serial with `Fr::rand` (~`:460`) but blinds it with plain `Base::issuance_encoding(tvk, serial, 0, rho)`, so a chosen serial signs.

- A bound token spent for another applicant: `AttestationRejected("token-binding-mismatch")`, nothing spent, no ledger anomaly; an unbound random-serial token also fails closed.
- Honest path: three statements counted.
- The old redirection test is renamed `residual_unbound_token_obtained_for_a_can_be_spent_on_another_applicant` and still passes as documentation of unbound behaviour.
- The witness's view: it sees only `C`, the label, the session pseudonym, the tick and the index (`witness_view_of_a_bound_request_does_not_expose_serial_or_nonce`); `C` is perfectly hiding thanks to the fresh `ρ`; guessing the link needs the 255-bit `r`.
- **Not tested or not modelled:** the test of attester substitution is weak (it attests with three enrolled attesters and does not model one who never met the applicant; it shows the verifier is blind to it); production needs `TokenWallet::prepare_with_serials` in the wrapper because the spike re-implements the private opening relation and context from `token.rs`; a per-statement `token-binding-mismatch` needs a small edit near `verifier.rs:205-217` plus the nonces carried on the submission (the wrapper version rejects the whole submission before anything is recorded, so no serial is burned); `r` need not be in `StatementMeta` (the struct is `deny_unknown_fields` and, with a collision-resistant hash, any `r` that passes is the right one); the issuer's in-memory ledger and served set are test-only.

**Caveats of binding.** The witness cannot enforce which identity the applicant binds; an applicant can bind tokens to a sybil identity. Issuance caps become per (applicant, session) rather than per attester.

## Part E. The threat table

| # | Attack | Status | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Token redirection: the attester spends the applicant's token on someone else | Closed for applicant-bound tokens (Part D); open in Phase 3 | Applicant may still bind to a sybil identity |
| 2 | Attester substitution: the attester hands the token to an enrolled colluder | **Open, documented** | The community counts distinct attester tags. Needs the token bound to the attester's key (PCS change, ~3–5 weeks plus review). Attacker needs *k* enrolled devices and *k* sessions involving the applicant |
| 3 | Late or backdated attestation | Open, documented | `valid_from`/`valid_until` are attester-chosen (`verifier.rs:224`); only the day label's liveness at submit bounds it |
| 4 | Remote or relay BLE | Accepted (D11) | Witness proximity standards trusted; no distance bounding |
| 5 | Device farm or sybil ring (*k*+1 phones) | Witness-policy limit | Caps, one root per hardware key per label; a colluding pair satisfies *k* = 1; reinstall resets a hardware key |
| 6 | Malicious or compromised witness mints tokens for non-events | Accepted by the community's trust decision | The community cannot audit it; limit by choice of trusted witness, per-community issuer keys, key custody; rotation invalidates roots (`issuer.rs:17-20`) |
| 7 | Witness and community collude to link a presentation to a session | Mitigated | Blind tokens, helper-unlinkable tags; leaves timing, labels, anonymity-set size. Keep the design doc's group-floor rule; avoid fine labels |
| 8 | Token hoarding or trading | Bounded | Label liveness and caps; useless once bound to the applicant |
| 9 | Counterparty ids to the witness | Residual | The witness sees relationship DIDs, times and locality; drop device-to-`id_B` records each epoch |
| 10 | Quantum retroactive linking (BLS12-381) | Residual | Same mitigation as 9 |
| 11 | Hardware attestation as a tracker | Mitigated | Certificate chain never leaves the witness; the community sees a flag |
| 12 | Attester's VTA operator is the community's operator | Residual | Hiding fails (design doc rule C7; VTI-Q16) |
| 13 | Small anonymity sets | Residual | Only the epoch, day and two flags as labels |
| 14 | Criteria change voids attestations | Resolved by D10, **conditional** | Needs the `StatementMeta` change |
