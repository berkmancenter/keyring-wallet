# Guardian-first setup: who holds control (research, 2026-11-16, b0 for Alberto)

Companion to [`../keri-holder-plan.md`](../keri-holder-plan.md). This is research only: no code, and no change to the plan. It answers Alberto's question, relayed by the coordinator:

> "how feasible would that be, because wouldnt it be that the guardian has control? do some research on that new design please"

Today the **ward** asks ("Ask X to be my approver"). The guardian accepts and then sends their Guardian card. The question is about a **guardian-first** flow ("I'm Romeo's guardian, add me") for wards who can't set this up themselves.

## Short answer

- **It's feasible if the guardian offers and the ward's phone accepts.** The ward's yes stays and the ward's own phone still writes its rules. It's a small change: one new message type and two screens, built on code we already have.
- **A guardian should not add themselves without the ward's yes.** Upstream allows it only if the guardian holds `admin` on the ward's agent. That is custodial control: the ward could undo nothing over the wire. Utah's text also points the other way. The holder's control is "exclusive", and a guardian acts within a scope policy, with every guardian action logged separately.
- **A guardian should not be able to remove the ward's other approvers.** On upstream this already follows from the rules we write, unless the guardian is admin or runs the agent.
- **The real risk is today's flow, not guardian-first.** Once a guardian is the ward's only approver, removing them needs their own yes. That's the step-down deadlock from earlier today, turned around. A coercive guardian keeps a veto over their own removal. This needs fixing whichever way the setup starts (§4).

## 1. Control: what the sources say

### "Approver" and "digital guardian" are different powers

| | Keyring approver (today) | SEDI digital guardian |
|---|---|---|
| What it can do | approve or decline the ward's agent's gated tasks (a check on the ward) | **act for** the ward: "apply for, manage, and present a SEDI on the individual's behalf" (Annex G §4.10) |
| Where the power comes from | the ward's own agent policy (`pnm approvals`), written by the ward | a legal mandate (parent, court-appointed guardian, or a representative the person designates), verified by the Department |
| In our code | `approverRole.ts`, plus the consent filter in `foreignApproval.ts` | the Guardian card is *evidence* only: `guardianEvidence.ts` checks it but grants nothing |

The cred-spec draft draws the same line. A delegation credential (VDC) "establishes delegation and nothing else. Guardianship … and other representative authority that arises from an external mandate are conferred by whatever governed process confers them … A representative so appointed may in turn delegate, by issuing a VDC bounded by the authority it actually holds" (`external/dtgwg-cred-spec-wd02/spec/body.md`, §Delegation and Authority, about line 623).

So guardian-first answers two questions, and they shouldn't be blurred:

1. Who **checks** the ward (the approver role, which we have).
2. Who **acts for** the ward (SEDI guardian presentation, which we don't have).

### Utah SEDI: statute and implementation guide

These come from **S.B. 275, 1st Substitute (2026-02-24 text, le.utah.gov `SB0275S01.pdf`)**. I haven't checked them against the enrolled bill.

- **Who a guardian is.** 63A-20-201(3): "a person authorized to act in the best interest and on behalf of another individual". This covers four people:
  - a representative designated by the individual (under Department rules);
  - a custodial parent of an unemancipated minor;
  - a court-appointed guardian of a minor (75-5-202);
  - a court-appointed guardian of an incapacitated person (75-5-301).
- **A guardian can be a holder.** 63A-20-201(9)(b): a "holder" includes "a digital guardian who manages and presents a state digital identity on behalf of the individual".
- **The person's control.** 63A-20-101(2): "An individual has a right to the management and control of the individual's digital identity". Guide SEDI-BOR-02 adds "The holder SHALL have exclusive control over the storage, presentation, and deletion of their SEDI", and "No other party SHALL be able to present, modify, or revoke the holder's credential."
- **Scope and logging.** SEDI-BOR-02 says a guardian "may only exercise credential management actions within the digital guardian scope policy". That policy "enumerates the permitted credential management action types (e.g., present, delete, obtain)", and "guardian-initiated actions are separately logged". The Department writes the scope policy; it isn't published yet.
- **Consent at application.** 63A-20-302:
  - (2): a minor applies "only with the consent of the individual's digital guardian";
  - (3)(a): a guardian may apply for someone unable to because of youth or incapacitation;
  - (3)(b)(ii): the guardian must provide "the consent of the incapacitated person, as required by the department".

  So even for an incapacitated adult, the statute asks for the person's own consent, in a form the Department sets.
- **How guardians are designated.** 63A-20-202(3)(a)(iii)(B): the Department makes rules for how an individual designates a digital guardian. 63A-20-301(2)(g)(iii): a process for "appointment of a digital guardian".
- **Duty of loyalty.** 63A-20-701 binds a digital guardian too: no practices that "conflict with the best interests of an individual", "exploit", or "cause harm". The guide's SEDI-LOY-01 to 04 restate this.
- **Wallet obligations.** SEDI-WAL-09 (63A-20-401(1)(h)) requires the wallet to support "digital guardian presentation mode". Guardian authorization must be verified first. Guardian presentations must be "distinguishable from direct holder presentations", and "expired or revoked guardian authorizations are rejected."
- **What I didn't find.** Nothing says how a guardianship ends, or whether a ward can revoke a guardian. That is left to Department rules (designated representatives) and to probate law (court appointments). 63A-20-301(5) covers revoking the *SEDI* only.

This also partly answers **KH-Q7**: Annex G and Part 4 are now read for guardians. SEDI-WAL-09 is a wallet obligation we don't meet yet.

### KERI/ACDC (the keripy SEDI test schemas, as pinned in our K0 kit)

These are upstream test schemas, not ones the state has adopted (`2026-10-13-bam.md`). They show the intended shape:

- **Guardianship** (`EDJzXg34…`): issued by the state's issuing agent to the guardian's AID. It carries `role` (for example `parent`), `ward` (the ward's AID), `issuedDate`, `expirationDate` and a legal-language rule. **There is no scope field.** The credential says *who*, not *what they may do*.
- **Social Authorization** (`EB8MUTKR…`): issued **by the guardian to the ward**, chained to the Guardianship credential. It carries `rc`, the authorized resource capabilities (the kit's example is `myEmptySpace: ["all"]`). So in KERI's model the guardian *grants the ward* capabilities. Revoking the Guardianship takes these down with it (`socialAuthorization.ts`).
- **Revocation** is the issuer's, through the registry's TEL. Our verifier refuses a revoked or expired card and re-checks it each time Who approves opens (`guardianEvidence.ts`, `sedi/guardian/chain.ts`). Nothing in the schemas lets the ward revoke.

Fixture paths: `bifold/packages/core/src/modules/sedi/__tests__/fixtures/k0-utah-v2/schema-*.json` and `acdc-G-ok.json` / `acdc-S2-social.json`.

### Upstream VTA: who can change a ward's approvers

Pinned clone: `external/verifiable-trust-infrastructure` at f7b78e29d.

- **Approvers are policy rules on the ward's own agent.** They're edited with `pnm approvals` (approver sets plus `--consent --min N`), which needs an ACL role that can write policy (`docs/02-vta/approvals.md`, "Requiring approval"). The roles are `Admin`, `Initiator`, `Application`, `Reader` and `Monitor` (`vti-common/src/acl/mod.rs`). **No role is limited to "add myself as an approver".**
- **Rules gate changes to the rules too.** A consent rule on `policy/upsert/0.3` "gives you two-person control over changes to the gate itself", and a wedged set is "unrecoverable over the wire *by construction*" ("If you lock yourself out").
- **Only whoever has the machine can wipe the rules.** That's `vta approvals remove|delete-all` with the daemon stopped. There is "deliberately **no** offline command that *adds* a rule".
- **Enforcement is off by default.** `policy.enforcement = true` is needed before any rule gates anything ("Enforcement").
- **An approver sees little.** A consent request carries the task type, a payload digest, the approver set and an expiry, not the rule change itself. This is the residual power banked from #1081.

**What this means for control:**

- A guardian can add themselves only if the ward's agent has given them a policy-writing ACL role (custodial), or if they run the agent.
- They can remove the ward's other approvers only on the same terms, and even then a consent rule on `policy/upsert` still needs the existing approvers' yes.
- Whoever runs the agent's machine can do anything. For Farm agents, that's the Farm operator, not us.

## 2. Four mechanisms

| | A. Guardian **offers**, ward accepts | B. Guardian card **pre-fills** the ward's ask | C. State-issued delegation honoured by the ward's agent policy | D. Guardian **holds** the ward's agent (custodial) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Flow | Alberto: "Offer to be Romeo's approver". Romeo's phone checks the attached card and shows the offer. Romeo says yes, and his phone writes the rule. | Alberto sends the card first. Romeo's phone shows "Alberto is your guardian (Utah)" with a ready "Ask Alberto" button. | The office issues a credential. The ward's agent adds or keeps the guardian whenever a valid card names the ward. | The guardian is `admin` (or operator) of the ward's agent and writes the rules. |

## 3. Recommendation

1. **Build A and B together as one flow: "Offer to be their approver".**
   - Alberto starts it from his Guardian card (or from the contact page), with the card attached under his existing send-my-card yes.
   - Romeo's phone verifies the card against its ward and shows the offer.
   - Romeo accepts, and his phone writes the rule as today.
   - The guardian never writes anything to the ward's agent. Keep it behind the SEDI/KERI Developer switch, like the rest.
2. **For wards who can't use the phone themselves, "set up together" on the ward's phone.** The guardian is in the room, and the ward's phone still holds and writes its own rules. Department rules decide whether an incapacitated person's consent is needed (302(3)(b)(ii)). We shouldn't model that consent until the rules exist. Say "with the ward's yes, on the ward's phone", and nothing more.
3. **Don't build C or D.** C is upstream's to design, and a state-honoured delegation should come from the office, not from us. D is custodial: it gives the guardian everything SEDI-BOR-02 says no other party may have. It's a different product (a guardian-run agent), with its own scope policy and guardian log.
4. **Fix the self-veto first (§4).** It matters more than the setup direction.
5. **SEDI-WAL-09 (guardian presentation mode) is the real "acts for" feature**, and it's a separate piece of work. Note it under KH-Q7.

## 4. The self-veto (coercive-control risk, present today)

If Romeo's only approver is Alberto and a consent rule covers `policy/upsert`, removing Alberto needs Alberto's yes. A coercive guardian can refuse, and only the machine's operator can break the lock. #1081 made the cooperative case work (a step-down keeps listening, and only `changeApprovers` stays approvable). It does nothing for an approver who won't step aside.

Options. None is decided, and each needs Alberto's word:

- **(a) Upstream ask:** a rule shape where *removing an approver* is decided by the remaining approvers, or by the ward alone after a hold period. Upstream already muses about a cancellable hold period for recovery (`docs/05-design-notes/key-roles.md`, open question 5). The ask belongs with the banked "the agent should send the rule change in the consent request" item.
- **(b) Our side, now:** require at least two approvers before the ward's phone writes a consent rule on `policy/upsert`, so no single approver can veto their own removal. This is a policy choice the ward makes when writing the rule, with no upstream change.
- **(c) Card-bound standing:** when the guardian's card is revoked or expired, the ward's phone says so plainly and offers removal. Over the wire, this still needs the approver set's yes, so (c) only helps together with (a) or (b).

Whatever the screens say about removal has to be true under these rules. "You can remove them any time" would be false today.
