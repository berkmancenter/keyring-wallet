# KERI/ACDC holder support: research record

**Date:** 2026-10-13. **Author:** bam. **Plan:** [../keri-holder-plan.md](../keri-holder-plan.md)

Past-tense record of what was read, what was checked, and what was rejected. The plan states the resulting design. Per `docs/plans/CLAUDE.md`, a dated companion is not revised after its session; this file spans one working session (2026-10-13) and later parts supersede earlier ones where they say so (Part VIII over Parts II, IV and VI; Part IX over Parts II, III and VIII; Part X over Parts VII, VIII.1, VIII.3 and IX where it says so). Annotations in square brackets, marked "Part X", point forward to those corrections; the original text is kept.

## Part I. Scope decision

Brendan chose **holder-only**: receive, store, verify and present ACDCs; verify the issuer's KEL, TEL and schema SAIDs; do not manage a KEL, rotate, witness or issue.

**Rejected: a full KERI agent in the wallet** (own AID, rotation, witnessing, issuance). Reasons: it needs a persistent, recoverable KEL with pre-rotation key custody on a phone, and the secure enclave is P-256 only while KERI's default is Ed25519; it overlaps with the VTA, which is already the holder's agent; and no library covers it in TypeScript on Hermes. It would also pull the wallet into witness selection and watcher duties it has no operator for.

**Rejected: acting as a KERI delegate of the VTA.** The DBI plan states the VTA is the holder's agent and not a KERI delegate; this plan keeps that.

## Part V. Other rejected options

- **ACDCs in the credential vault (PR #202).** The vault's receive path verifies a single `eddsa-jcs-2022` proof; an ACDC does not carry one. Making it fit needs an upstream change. Chosen instead: phone-local records, with the record shaped so a later vault adapter is a storage swap.
- **Converting an ACDC to a W3C Data Integrity credential in the wallet.** That is the DBI plan's job, in the VTA, with a proof that the derived credential links to the ACDC. A wallet-side conversion would silently drop the KERI properties (TEL status, KEL anchoring, edges) the credential is chosen for.
- **An on-device ZK prover for selective disclosure.** BLS12-381 runs about 15x slower on Hermes than Node (PR #114); the DBI plan's zkVM benchmark (companion D15 there) found a zkVM guest unviable even on a 2-CPU server.
- **Implementing IPEX as a Trust Task now.** It would couple this plan to an upstream spec that does not exist; wrapped carriage works without one.
- **A KERI mailbox client.** Out of scope for holder-only v1; recorded as R3.
- **A second scanner for OOBI.** The UI/UX plan's one-scanner direction applies.

## Part VI. Why the library is not chosen in the plan

The deciding facts are runtime facts: whether the verify path of keri-ts bundles and runs on Hermes without WASM or Node-only modules. Both the reading and the Hermes pitfalls in `bifold/packages/trust-tasks/vendor/README.md` (TextDecoder, `isWellFormed`, Node `fs`/`Buffer`, ESM-only exports) say it is plausible and neither says it is true. A plan that picked L1 now would state an unmeasured claim as settled.

### VIII.2 Suite profiles

The plan had one primary profile with P-256 conditional (A3). Reading DBI §5.1 and D3/D4 shows P-256 and SHA2-256 are what the Utah customer is proposed to use, so P-256 is the primary case, not a conditional one. The plan now has two profiles (Utah: P-256 and SHA2-256; vLEI/stock: Ed25519 and Blake3), both supported. The digest remains DBI's proposal, not a Utah answer. Caveats recorded from DBI §5.1 and its X6: the `E`/`F`/`2E`/`2F` indexed codes are keripy's extension outside the CESR indexed table; the next-key code defaults to Ed25519; low-S is not normalised, so both `s` and `n - s` are accepted (DBI marks that keripy behaviour "unquoted"); and there is no evidence of P-256 with witnesses or delegation (DBI G2).
