## Locality

Possible locality mechanisms (various levels of security, may be layered):

* **Fixed witness kiosk (NFC double-tap):** highest assurance \- the kiosk's 4cm NFC range physically bounds proximity and defeats relay; cost is everyone queues at a limited number of devices.
  * Extend with some extra devices (e.g. like “your table is ready buzzers?”) Works so long as connected via Bluetooth? Have devices at lunch tables, etc?
  * Just once as you walk into the event?
* **Peer-to-peer NFC tap (no kiosk):** two phones tap directly, mutually biometric-signed; strong for honest parties but iOS peer-NFC is unreliable today, and nothing stops two willing colluders.
* **QR / local-channel peer exchange:** works on every phone right now, but a QR value relays trivially \- weakest proximity guarantee, leans hardest on detection.
* **Ambient witnessing:** each phone signs the set of nearby BLE beacons it heard; a forger must reproduce a live local crowd, but it's probabilistic and weak in near-empty rooms.
* **Shared-key continuity:** the same biometric key chains a person's meetings into one coherent day-trajectory \- cheapest, strongest anti-forgery signal, pure software, cross-platform, kills the stolen-device and teleport cases.
* **Sparse anchor co-witnessing:** people occasionally brush past a known witness device that vouches for their nearby peer meetings \- borrows physics-backed assurance without per-meeting friction.
* **Conference-wide graph corroboration:** many simultaneous ceremonies cross-check each other; fabrication has to stay globally consistent, which isolated forgers and small rings can't manage.
* **Fraud-detection \+ repudiation layer:** since prevention can't fully stop willing collusion, biometric-signed edges feed a privacy-preserving abuse-signal layer and let victims cryptographically disown fake meetings.
* NOTES:
  * Devices will NOT attest to bluetooth or GPS signals
  * Willing collusion between two consenting parties is the one threat no decentralized option fully closes \- only the at event kiosk does


Proximity Confirmation for In-Person DIDComm Credential Exchange
Proposal, prioritized strategy, and implementation plan (v2)
1. Problem statement
Two mobile phones already establish a DIDComm connection via credo-ts, bootstrapped by a QR scan; relationship credentials (VRCs) are exchanged over that connection. We want to add a proximity confirmation layer on top so the exchange carries verifiable evidence that the parties were physically co-present, not merely two DIDs that completed a handshake over the internet.

Requirements gathered:

Support two parties directly, OR two parties connected through a trusted witness (either topology satisfies the requirement; we do not need both in a single exchange).
Produce evidence that is, as far as possible, verifiable by a third party, with residual limitations small and clearly scoped.
Targets Android and iOS, integrated into an existing React Native app as a clean, reusable module.
App-integrity attestation is optional but always flagged in the evidence (addresses attestation flakiness and preserves user choice; the verifier sees exactly what was and wasn't proven).
2. The honest ceiling (read this first)
No proximity mechanism on consumer phones produces unforgeable, transferable proof that humans were physically together. Every phone proximity signal (BLE RSSI, NFC range, UWB) convinces the participating devices; a third party cannot later distinguish a genuine reading from colluding devices that fabricated one. The devices are the sensors, and if the sensors lie in concert the output is indistinguishable from truth.

So we do not chase physical proof. We build the strongest verifiable evidence bundle achievable and scope the residual precisely. Two design choices do the heavy lifting:

A trusted witness partially escapes the collusion ceiling. In a two-party exchange the claim always rests on "assume the two didn't collude," because the only observers are the two interested parties. A trusted witness (an org device, a notary-role party, a VRC issuer in the DTG) is an observer with no incentive to fake the meeting, so the claim becomes "a trusted third party attests it observed both parties co-present." The residual shrinks from "two counterparties colluded" to "both parties and the trusted witness colluded," which for a well-chosen witness is far smaller.

App-integrity attestation moves the trust anchor from the human to the attested app. When present, it lets a verifier check, against Apple/Google roots, that a genuine unmodified app instance produced the proximity evidence, cryptographically bound to this session. When absent (user choice, or attestation service unreachable), the evidence is explicitly flagged as unattested and the verifier weights it accordingly.

What a third party can end up verifying: both parties' DIDs bound to a session nonce, each signature optionally covered by a platform integrity attestation that chains to Apple/Google and commits to the same session binding, plus the proximity method used and (in witness mode) the witness's signed observation. The single clearly-scoped residual is an RF relay attack (below).
3. Core principle: channel binding
Whatever mechanism is used, its output is meaningless unless cryptographically tied to the specific DIDComm session; otherwise an attacker proves proximity on one channel and relays the exchange on another. Channel binding means deriving a value from the established connection (peer DIDs + fresh per-session nonce, ideally an exported key-agreement secret) and carrying that same value through the proximity mechanism and into every signature and attestation.

This is the design pattern from the vetted standards we model on: FIDO hybrid puts the tunnel secret in the QR and derives its BLE beacon from it; ISO/IEC 18013-5 binds its device-engagement to the session it sets up. We reuse the pattern and bind to DIDComm.

4.2 Why BLE is primary despite being the weakest physical signal
Our requirement includes a witness and possibly group co-presence. That decision is topological, and it dominates the physical-strength ranking:

NFC is intrinsically a two-endpoint, one-tap-at-a-time physical act. It has no natural notion of "three devices present together." A witness exchange over NFC would be sequential pairwise taps, a poor ceremony that does not yield a clean group claim.
BLE is a broadcast/scan medium: one device advertises a channel-bound beacon and any number of nearby devices observe it. This maps directly onto "a witness and two parties in the same space," and it is the only option that works across the full platform matrix (including iOS-to-iOS, where NFC peer mode is gone).

So BLE wins on coverage and topology, not on proximity strength. We are explicit about the cost: BLE proves only "within radio range of the beacon," range is environment-dependent, and RSSI is spoofable by an attacker with a directional antenna or amplifier. BLE is therefore treated exactly as FIDO hybrid treats it, as a coarse presence beacon whose security comes from channel binding + integrity attestation + the trusted witness, never from BLE ranging as a security boundary.
4.3 Why NFC stays as optional hardening
For a strictly two-party exchange where at least one device is Android, NFC's centimeter range makes relay physically hard, which is a genuinely stronger proximity claim than BLE can offer. When topology and platforms allow it, offering NFC as an upgrade tier is worth the moderate extra effort. It is never required, because iOS-to-iOS peer NFC is unavailable.
4.4 Why SAS is demoted to channel-auth only
Short-authentication-string verification (both sides display a value derived from the channel; humans confirm it matches) defeats a remote man-in-the-middle, because a relay cannot make the on-screen codes match on two genuine endpoints. But it proves nothing about physical proximity: two willing parties on a video call read matching codes to each other trivially. It is retained only as a connection-integrity check and a last-resort fallback, and it is recorded with zero proximity weight so no verifier mistakes it for co-presence.
5. Topology model: direct or witness-anchored
The requirement is satisfied by either of two shapes; the module supports both and records which was used.

Direct (two-party). A and B bind the BLE beacon (or NFC tap) to their DIDComm session; each observes the other's channel-bound signal; each signs a co-presence attestation. Claim: "A and B assert mutual co-presence for session S." Rests on A-B non-collusion unless integrity attestation is present.

Witness-anchored (hub-and-spoke, witness = hub). The witness advertises the channel-bound beacon; both A and B scan and confirm the same witness-anchored binding; the witness observes both. The witness issues the authoritative attestation "I observed A and B co-present with me at time T, bound to session S," with A and B co-signing. Claim strength derives from trust in the witness. This is the recommended shape when a stronger third-party-presentable artifact is needed, and it also concentrates the hard BLE advertising role in a single node (ideally a controlled or fixed device), leaving A and B on the easy scanning role.

Note on the witness hub: it establishes A-near-witness and B-near-witness, which bounds all three into the beacon's radio range but does not directly prove A-near-B. For our requirement that is acceptable, because "two people connected through a witness" is an accepted topology. If a future case needs A-B mutual proximity independent of a witness, use the direct mode instead.
6. Hardening the one residual: RF relay
With integrity attestation present and SAS excluded from proximity, the residual attack collapses to a single, nameable vector: an RF relay between genuine attested app instances that are not actually co-present. We do not eliminate it (no consumer-phone mechanism does) but we price it up and make it detectable:

Prefer NFC when the two-party topology and platforms allow it, because relay at centimeter range needs specialized low-latency hardware.
Enforce a tight timing bound. The proximity exchange (and, in witness mode, a witness-driven challenge-response round-trip over the established channel) must complete within a bounded window enforced by the app. Relay hardware adds latency; a tight bound makes it detectable. This is a poor-man's approximation of distance bounding without the UWB primitives the OS does not expose.
Record RSSI as corroborating (not decisive) evidence so a verifier can see it, while never treating it as a security boundary.
7. Standards we model on (best-practice references)
FIDO CTAP 2.2, Hybrid transports (§11.5). The pattern for a channel-bound BLE proximity beacon separated from the data channel; our BLE tier reuses this. https://fidoalliance.org/specs/fido-v2.2-rd-20230321/fido-client-to-authenticator-protocol-v2.2-rd-20230321.html#sctn-hybrid
ISO/IEC 18013-5 (mobile driving licence / mdoc). Vetted standard for two mobile devices establishing an in-person session via QR device-engagement plus a transport (BLE / NFC defined). We model our engagement-and-session-binding on it (without adopting the mdoc credential format).
Apple App Attest / DeviceCheck. Hardware-backed attestation of a genuine, unmodified app instance on genuine hardware, with a developer-supplied challenge bound into the attestation nonce (nonce = SHA-256(authenticatorData || SHA-256(challenge))). We set that challenge to our session binding. https://developer.apple.com/documentation/devicecheck ; WWDC21 "Mitigate fraud with App Attest and DeviceCheck" https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2021/10244/
Google Play Integrity API. Signed integrity verdict with a developer requestHash (Standard) / nonce (Classic) bound to the protected action; verified server-side against Google. We set requestHash to our session binding. https://developer.android.com/google/play/integrity/overview and verdict format https://developer.android.com/google/play/integrity/verdicts
Short Authentication String verification (ZRTP SAS; Matrix key-verification SAS; Signal safety numbers). The vetted basis for our channel-auth fallback tier. (Used only for channel authentication here, not proximity.)
W3C Verifiable Credentials Data Model. The co-presence attestation is expressed as a VC-shaped signed object so it composes with the existing VRC/DTG stack. https://www.w3.org/TR/vc-data-model-2.0/

Best-practice notes carried from these sources: bind attestation to the specific action via nonce/requestHash (both platforms explicitly document this and both document that missing action-binding is the common integration flaw); verify integrity tokens server-side / at the verifier, never trust a client self-report; enforce token freshness (timestamp windows) to prevent replay; and do not rely on package/bundle identity as the only app-identity check (Play Integrity documents that requestPackageName can be spoofed mid-request).
8. Implementation steps (React Native / TypeScript), high level
Everything sits above DIDComm behind one reusable module with pluggable providers, so the app integrates via a single call with a clean fallback chain.
8.1 Module boundary
// Ties any proximity check to this specific DIDComm session.

interface ChannelBinding {

  connectionId: string;          // credo-ts connection id

  selfDid: string;

  peerDids: string[];            // 1 for direct, 2 for witness-observed parties

  role: 'party' | 'witness';

  sessionNonce: Uint8Array;      // fresh per exchange

  bindingHash: Uint8Array;       // H(sorted DIDs || sessionNonce || exported-channel-secret?)

}

type ProximityMethod = 'ble' | 'nfc' | 'sas';

type Topology = 'direct' | 'witness';

interface ProximityProvider {

  readonly method: ProximityMethod;

  isAvailable(topology: Topology): Promise<boolean>;

  confirm(binding: ChannelBinding, topology: Topology): Promise<ProximityResult>;

}

interface ProximityResult {

  method: ProximityMethod;

  topology: Topology;

  confirmed: boolean;

  rssi?: number;                 // corroborating only, BLE

  roundTripMs?: number;          // for the timing bound

  evidence?: Uint8Array;         // received beacon EID / tapped binding

}

// Optional, flagged integrity evidence.

interface IntegrityEvidence {

  platform: 'ios' | 'android';

  present: boolean;              // ALWAYS recorded; false = user declined / unavailable

  token?: string;                // App Attest assertion / Play Integrity token

  boundTo: Uint8Array;           // the bindingHash used as challenge/requestHash

}
8.2 Derive the channel binding from credo-ts
After the connection is up, pull peer DIDs from credo-ts, generate sessionNonce, and derive bindingHash from the sorted DIDs + nonce + (if reachable) an exported key-agreement secret. If the underlying secret is not cleanly exportable through credo-ts, bind to DIDs + nonce + a value both sides sign; this still defeats remote MITM as long as the nonce travels only over the authenticated DIDComm channel. (Open item: confirm credo-ts key-export surface; determines strong vs. fallback binding.)
8.3 Provider 1: BLE (primary; build first)
Scanning role (easy): react-native-ble-plx. Parties scan for the channel-bound beacon and match its EID to bindingHash; capture RSSI and receipt time.
Advertising role (moderate-hard): the hub (witness in witness mode, or either party in direct mode) advertises an EID derived from bindingHash. react-native-ble-plx does not do peripheral mode; use a peripheral-capable library or a small native module, and handle iOS advertising restrictions. Strongly prefer a controlled/fixed device or an Android device as the witness/advertiser to avoid iOS foreground-advertising pain.
Enforce the timing bound (§6) around the beacon observation and, in witness mode, a witness-driven challenge-response over DIDComm.
Output: ProximityResult{ method:'ble', topology, confirmed, rssi, roundTripMs }.
8.4 Provider 2: NFC (optional two-party hardening)
react-native-nfc-manager behind the same interface. Exchange bindingHash over the tap; each side verifies equality.
isAvailable('direct') returns false for iOS-to-iOS peer exchange, so the module transparently falls back to BLE. Never offered for witness topology.
8.5 Provider 3: SAS (channel-auth fallback, no proximity weight)
Compute a short code from bindingHash (commit-reveal so neither side reveals first). Humans confirm match. Recorded as method:'sas' and explicitly flagged as proximity weight = none in the attestation.
8.6 Optional integrity attestation (flagged either way)
If enabled by the user and the service is reachable: on iOS call App Attest with clientDataHash = SHA-256(bindingHash); on Android request a Play Integrity token with requestHash = base64url(bindingHash). Attach the resulting token as IntegrityEvidence{ present:true, ... }.
If disabled or unreachable: attach IntegrityEvidence{ present:false }. The exchange still completes; the evidence simply records that integrity was not proven. Verification of the token happens at the verifier/server against Apple/Google roots, never on the client.
8.7 Assemble the co-presence attestation
Produce a VC-shaped signed object containing: sorted DIDs, sessionNonce, bindingHash, topology, ProximityMethod (+ proximity weight), RSSI/timing corroboration, per-party signatures, the witness's observation and signature (witness mode), and each party's IntegrityEvidence (present-or-flagged-absent). Attach it to the VRC exchange record.
8.8 Wire into the flow
QR scan bootstraps DIDComm (existing).
Derive ChannelBinding (§8.2), choosing direct or witness.
ProximityConfirmation.confirm(binding, topology) selects BLE (primary), or NFC where a two-party topology + platforms allow the upgrade, or SAS as fallback.
Optionally gather integrity attestation (§8.6), always recording present/absent.
Assemble and exchange the attestation (§8.7); attach to the exchange record.
On mismatch / timeout / relay-timing failure, abort the credential exchange with a clear "could not confirm you are together" error and a retry path (important since integrity services can be transiently unavailable).
9. What a verifier gets, and the exact residual
A third party evaluating the attestation can independently verify: the DID signatures (resolve DIDs, check signatures); the session binding (nonce + DIDs commit to this exchange); and, when present, the integrity tokens against Apple/Google roots, confirming a genuine unmodified app instance produced evidence committed to this same binding. In witness mode, the trusted witness's signed observation is the authoritative co-presence claim.

The residual, stated as small as it honestly goes:

RF relay between genuine attested app instances that are not co-present is the one attack the evidence cannot exclude. Priced up by NFC (where used) and the timing bound; not eliminated.
Integrity bound to app health, not eliminated: App Attest / Play Integrity raise the cost of a tampered client but are defeatable by determined attackers; the claim is "as strong as App Attest / Play Integrity," a known and citable bound. Where integrity is flagged absent, this protection is simply not present and the artifact says so.
A genuine, unlocked device operated by someone other than its owner is out of scope for any device-based mechanism.
BLE proximity is coarse: it attests radio-range co-presence, not distance; RSSI is corroborating, never decisive.
10. Summary of strategy and why
No single named standard bolts transferable in-person proof onto an existing DIDComm channel; that artifact is not deliverable on stock phones and we do not claim it. We reuse vetted mechanisms (FIDO hybrid's channel-bound beacon, ISO 18013-5 engagement, App Attest / Play Integrity, SAS) and bind them to DIDComm.
BLE is primary because our requirement is topological (witness / possible multi-party) and cross-platform, and only BLE's broadcast model and platform coverage fit; we accept and document that it is a coarse, relay-vulnerable signal whose strength comes from channel binding + integrity attestation + trusted witness.
NFC is optional two-party hardening where centimeter range meaningfully resists relay and platforms allow.
SAS is a channel-auth fallback with zero proximity weight.
A trusted witness is the strongest lever we have, doing more security work than the radio, by replacing "assume two parties didn't collude" with "a trusted observer attests co-presence."
Integrity attestation is optional but always flagged, so verifiers see exactly what was proven, and it is verified at the verifier, never on the client.
Everything is one reusable RN module with pluggable providers and a clean fallback chain.
