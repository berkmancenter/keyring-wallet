# 2026-08-18 — turning the mechanism survey into an implementable plan

Author: Brendan A. Miller (bam). Companion to
[`../locality-plan.md`](../locality-plan.md), which this session created.

The [2026-07-20 notes](./2026-07-20-bam.md) surveyed mechanisms and established
the honest ceiling. This document records the design decisions that turned that
survey into a plan, the evidence behind each, and the positions each supersedes
— including several of my own from July.

Verified against pinned clone `dtgwg-trust-tasks-tf@7e0d755` (Cypress lock) and
the working tree at `1367eed`.

## Finding 1 — the shipped scaffold points the evidence the wrong way

**Evidence.** `bifold/packages/witness-server/src/LocalityService.ts` and
`LocalityProvider.ts` are already in the tree. The design there: the witness
advertises a rotating challenge (default rotation 5 minutes,
`WITNESS_LOCALITY_CHALLENGE_ROTATION_MINUTES`); a device that receives it signs
it and returns the signature; `verifyLocality()` records the proof and
`buildLocalityEvidence()` emits `{ challenge, proofs: [{did, sig}] }` into the
VWC's `witnessContext.localityVerification`. `LocalityProvider.ts` names
`BluetoothLocalityProvider` as the planned transport and the file's own TODO
concedes the missing piece: *"When Bluetooth is implemented, add transport-level
proximity check here."*

**What is wrong with it.** A value broadcast to everyone in range is a shared
secret with a lifetime equal to the rotation period. Any passive listener — not
a colluder, not a participant, potentially a script publishing it to a web page
— can hand it to anyone on earth, who can then sign it and be recorded as
co-located. The signature proves key possession, not presence. And
`verifyLocality()` currently checks only that the signature string is non-empty,
so today's code would accept an arbitrary value.

Worse, it is unfixable in that direction. Strengthening it (shorter rotation,
real signature verification) does not change the fact that the value the device
must produce is one the witness broadcast to the room.

**Decision.** Invert it. The device advertises a per-session ephemeral id
derived from the challenge the witness sent *that party alone* over the
authenticated task channel; the witness's sensor scans, connects, mints a nonce
on the radio link, and records the device's signature over a binding covering
both. The witness's observation is the evidence; the device's is not.

**What this supersedes.** The July notes' §8.3, which assigned the advertising
role to the hub ("the hub (witness in witness mode…) advertises an EID derived
from bindingHash") and the scanning role to the parties. I had it backwards, and
so does the code. The same section's platform reasoning survives intact and in
fact argues for the inversion: it noted that iOS advertising is the painful role
and recommended keeping it off phones — but the whole platform matrix is easier
the other way round, because a Linux sensor scanning is trivial and iOS
*foreground* advertising of a service UUID is well-supported. FIDO CTAP 2.2
hybrid also runs this direction: the phone is the peripheral, the relying
party's client is the central.

**Supporting rationale, from my own July notes.** "Devices will NOT attest to
bluetooth or GPS signals." That note was written as a limitation. It is actually
the design constraint that decides the direction: if a device's radio
observation can never be attested, it can never be third-party evidence, so it
must not be what the protocol collects.

## Finding 2 — the wire seam already exists, and needs no upstream change

**Evidence.** All four documents of a witness session admit the framework's
`ext` extension member at the levels we need — checked in the pinned schemas,
not assumed:
`specs/witness/session/0.1/payload.schema.json` carries `ext` on the request
payload and on the `Response` `$def`; `specs/witness/session/submit/0.1/payload.schema.json`
carries it on both as well. SPEC.md §4.5.1 defines `ext` as "the framework's
sanctioned extension point for ecosystem-defined data that the base
specification does not enumerate", requires reverse-DNS immediate keys, reserves
no namespace for the framework, and states that the signed envelope covers `ext`
like any other `payload` member. §7.2 requires consumers to ignore namespaces
they do not recognize, and — this is the part that matters for policy — lets a
consumer *require* a namespace as local policy, rejecting with `malformedRequest`,
and says such a requirement SHOULD be published via discovery (§11).

**Decision.** Locality rides entirely in `ext` under one Keyring-controlled
namespace, on the merged `witness/*` documents. No new Trust Task type, no
upstream specification change, and no amendment to specifications that are
already merged. Graceful degradation in both directions is normative rather than
something we implement. The witness-requires-locality policy is likewise already
specified — we configure it, we do not invent it.

**What this supersedes.** The July notes' §8.7, which had the module assemble
"a VC-shaped signed object" carrying the co-presence attestation and "attach it
to the VRC exchange record". That was written before the Trust Task recast was
proven, and it would now build a second evidence artifact beside two that
already exist and are already retained: the VWC, and the outcome-evidence pair
(`witness/session` + `witness/session/submit#response`) the holder must keep
under the merged witness specification. Both `#response` variants declare
`proof: REQUIRED`, so the witness's observation record is integrity-protected and
attributable with no additional signing layer. A third artifact would duplicate
that and would have to be separately retained, indexed, and presented.

**Consequence for sequencing.** Because the seam is a task-layer facility, this
work depends on the recast (openvtc plan §5.4 stage 2) and is not retrofittable
onto the legacy chat-message ceremony. That is a scheduling constraint, and the
plan states it as a prerequisite rather than as an option.

## Finding 3 — the advert cannot be the proof, and we already proved why

**Decision.** Two values, two jobs: the advertised ephemeral id is a
**rendezvous locator**, and the signed GATT transcript is the **binder**.

**Evidence for the split, from our own corpus.**
[`ref-06w3-taskcontext-binding`](../../../tsp-reference/ref-06w3-taskcontext-binding/)
staged exactly this failure one layer up: a `taskContext` that is only an `id`
is forgeable in running code, because an id is a name anyone can reuse. The
resolution upstream was locator + digest. An advertised value visible to every
scanner in the room has the identical property — copying it is free — so the
same split applies, and the advert must never be treated as evidence of
anything. It makes the device *findable*, not *believed*.

**Binding runs both ways, and both are required.** The transcript covers the
session document's §4.9.3 task digest and the session challenge (so a transcript
cannot move between sessions, nor be presented under a counterfeit session
document reusing an `id`); and the `submit` request carries the sensor nonce and
transcript digest in `ext` (so a session cannot claim an observation it did not
earn). Omitting the second direction is the common integration flaw both
platform attestation services document.

## Finding 4 — sign the transcript with the existing hardware key

**Decision.** The device answers the radio challenge with the Secure Enclave /
StrongBox key it already uses for VRC hardware attestation
(`docs/HARDWARE_ATTESTATION_FLOW.md`; native chain verification against Apple
App Attest and the three Google roots is already shipping), not with a fresh
session key.

**Why it matters.** It upgrades the claim from *a device was in range* to *the
key that hardware-signed this credential was in range*, which is the step that
makes the locality assertion about **this** credential rather than about some
device that happened to be nearby. It costs nothing — the key, the biometric
gate, and the attestation path all exist.

**Free consequence.** The July notes' "shared-key continuity" idea — the same
key chaining a person's meetings into a coherent day-trajectory — becomes
available without building anything for it, because every locality transcript is
signed by that key. Not in scope; recorded so it is not re-derived later as new
work.

## Finding 5 — the timing bound is a long-distance discriminator, not a distance bound

**What the July notes said.** §6: "Enforce a tight timing bound … Relay hardware
adds latency; a tight bound makes it detectable. This is a poor-man's
approximation of distance bounding."

**Correction.** The first half holds for long-haul relays; the framing oversells
the local case. iOS enforces a 15 ms connection-interval floor and commonly
negotiates 30 ms, so an honest GATT round trip is tens to low hundreds of
milliseconds — while a relay on the same LAN adds single-digit milliseconds.
There is no bound compatible with honest devices that separates those. And it
does not need to: **the parking lot is already inside BLE range**, so a local
relay buys an attacker very little that walking outside would not.

**Decision.** State the bound's actual value — it excludes intercontinental
relays, nothing finer — and refuse to pick the number by assertion. `ref-06p2`
measures the honest RTT distribution on real radios; `ref-06p4` stages a real
relay with injected latency and measures where detection begins; the bound comes
from both, with its false-rejection rate published. A bound that rejects honest
devices converts a probabilistic security gain into a reliable availability
loss.

**Related honesty fix.** The evidence says "at the venue", not "in the room".
BLE range is building-scale and varies by tens of metres. Room-scale needs the
NFC-kiosk tier or multi-sensor RSSI narrowing, and both are typed as distinct
`method` values rather than folded into the same claim.

## Finding 6 — annotate by default, gate by exception

**Decision.** Wallet setting `useLocalityConfirmation` defaults **on**. Witness
policy is `off | offered | required`, defaulting to **`offered`** — attempt the
locality leg, annotate the outcome either way, do not refuse.

**Why annotation is safe here.** Only because the evidence is typed (`method`,
`confirmed`, `topology`, `residuals`) and negatives are explicit. A verifier who
needs locality demands `confirmed: true`, so the witness does not have to
enforce it on their behalf. This is the same position the OpenVTC plan already
took for witnessing under remote connection — type the evidence axes so
remote-witnessed and co-present-witnessed are distinct honest claims, and
locality becomes a named premium tier rather than a diluted default.

**The rule that makes it work.** A witness running locality MUST emit the
assertion even on failure. Absence of the member means "this witness does not do
locality"; `confirmed: false` means "it tried and did not succeed". Collapsing
those two into an omission is the single easiest way to make the whole artifact
misleading, and it is the failure mode I most expect from a partial
implementation.

**Bug surfaced in passing — APPLIED, later the same day.** `useHardwareAttestation`
initialises to `false` in `bifold/packages/core/src/contexts/store.tsx` while
`bifold/packages/core/src/modules/vrc/vrc-manager.ts` reads it from AsyncStorage
with `?? true`. The two defaults disagree. `useLocalityConfirmation` must default
`true` in both places; the attestation flag's split default should be fixed in
the VRC module rather than mirrored.

*Applied:* both read sites in `vrc-manager.ts` now fall back to `false`.
`e2e/lib/flows.js` (*"Enable the 'Hardware Attestation' preference (OFF by
default on fresh…)"*) and `docs/HARDWARE_ATTESTATION_FLOW.md` ("default **off**
in store") both document off-by-default, so `false` is the value that matches
intent; the `?? true` meant a fresh install ran attestation while Settings
showed it disabled. VRC module suite green, 162 tests. Uncommitted in the
`bifold/` submodule — it needs its own commit with the `Signed-off-by` trailer,
not a ride-along on a plan branch.

## Finding 7 — sensor identity is a day-one decision, not a later refactor

**Decision.** Observations are signed by a **sensor DID** from the first
implementation, even though phase 1 has exactly one sensor and its DID equals the
witness's.

**Why now.** The July notes' "devices at lunch tables" and "sparse anchor
co-witnessing" ideas both reduce to *more sensors, one witness*. If the
observation is defined as something the witness produces about itself, adding a
second sensor means changing the evidence schema after VWCs carrying the old
shape are in the wild. Defining it as signed-by-sensor costs a field today.

**Deployment recommendation.** Run the whole witness server at the venue on a
small Linux machine with a BLE adapter — the server already dials out to a
mediator over WebSocket and needs no inbound ports, and BlueZ gives a Node
process the scanning and GATT-central roles the sensor needs. The
remote-witness-plus-remote-sensor split is what the sensor-DID decision keeps
available; it is not phase 1. This is question Q2 in the plan because it depends
on where the first real event's witness is hosted.

**Decision.** Four rungs plus an attestation rung, as `ref-06p*` — a lettered
line under `ref-06` for one topic, matching the existing `v1` / `w` / `x` lines,

## Finding 10 — pinning the cred-spec immediately corrected two claims

Finding 8 argued the pin was owed. Within an hour of taking it, the clone
falsified one statement in my own plan and supplied a second I had not known to
make.

**10a — `taskDigestMultibase` is not in the merged credential schema.** My first
draft of the plan said the VWC "carries" it. The pinned cred-spec at `b89f389`
(HEAD, #14 merged) defines the VWC as `taskContext` (REQUIRED, still described
as the exchange's `threadId`), `credentialSubject.id`, `credentialSubject.digest`
(now REQUIRED — that is what #14 did), and an OPTIONAL `witnessContext` of three
OPTIONAL strings. There is no `taskDigestMultibase`; it arrives with #18, which
is open. So the sequencing gap the OpenVTC plan's §8.2 describes is live, and I
had quietly written past it.

The plan is corrected, and the correction carries a consequence worth stating:
**locality does not depend on #18 landing.** The digest locality needs for its
own binding is over the *session document*, which is a framework facility
(SPEC.md §4.9.3) rather than a credential one. The credential-side gap affects
the VWC↔session pairing generally, not this axis specifically.

**10b — the holder is expected to be able to withhold `witnessContext`.**
cred-spec Privacy Considerations: *"Issuers should include only what the
witnessing purpose requires, and holders should be able to withhold
`witnessContext` details when proving the attestation."* That sentence points
directly at the member this plan is about to load with a venue, a time window
and a sensor identity — the most location-revealing content a Keyring credential
would carry. It is now §9.1 of the plan, with the honest caveat that
`eddsa-rdfc-2022` gives no member-level selective disclosure, so phase 1 offers
present-or-withhold-the-whole-VWC and must say so in the UI rather than implying
finer control.

## Finding 11 — the locality evidence is not covered by the VWC's proof

Found while checking where the assertion's members would land. It is the
sharpest implementation constraint in the plan.

**Evidence.** VWCs are signed with `eddsa-rdfc-2022`
(`WitnessService.ts` verification path), which canonicalizes the RDF dataset —
so a JSON member whose term is undefined in the credential's `@context` does not
enter the dataset and is not covered by the signature.
`bifold/packages/vrc-contexts/src/witnessedExchangeContext.ts` defines
`localityVerification` as a term, but defines **none** of the members the
shipped `LocalityEvidence` nests inside it: `challenge`, `proofs`, `did`, `sig`.

**Why nobody has noticed.** The only shipped provider is
`NullLocalityProvider`, so `buildLocalityEvidence()` never returns anything and
the object is never attached. The defect is latent in code that has never run
with data — which is exactly the kind that ships the day a provider is added.

**What it changes.** Every member of the new assertion, at every nesting level,
needs a term definition with an explicit `@type` where it is typed, in a file
that both the app and the witness server import (it says so itself: "SINGLE
SOURCE OF TRUTH … both MUST import from this package"), or canonicalization
diverges between signer and verifier. And `ref-06p`'s acceptance criterion is
now **round-trip coverage, not presence**: sign a VWC carrying the assertion,
mutate one nested value, require verification to fail. A test asserting only
that the member survives serialization would pass today against evidence that is
not signed at all.

## Finding 13 — the witness runs at the venue

**Decision.** The witness server itself runs on location, on a small Linux
machine with a BLE adapter, and is its own sensor. Q2 closed.

**Why it is clean.** The server already dials out to a mediator over WebSocket
and needs no inbound ports, so a venue machine on any ordinary network is a
complete deployment — no tunnel, no static address, no certificate. BlueZ gives
the Node process the scanning and GATT-central roles the design assigns the
sensor.

**The consequence I had not drawn out before.** It is what makes `venueClaim`
honest. A remotely-hosted witness relaying a sensor's reports is asserting
something about a place it is not in; a witness *at* the venue is asserting
something about where it is. The sensor-DID decision (Finding 7) still stands
and still keeps the split available, but phase 1 should not be designed around
it.

**New question it creates (plan Q2):** who supplies and operates the venue
machine at the first real event, and what adapter is in it. `ref-06p2`'s measured
RTT distribution only transfers to production if production runs comparable
hardware — a measurement taken on a good adapter and deployed onto a cheap one
would silently move the false-rejection rate.

## Finding 14 — gating moves information out of the credential

Brendan asked for the argument rather than the recommendation. Writing it out
changed how strongly I hold the position: it is not a balance of convenience, it
is a direction-of-information argument.

**A witness in `required` mode makes a promise that does not travel.** Its VWCs
all mean co-presence — but only a verifier who already knows this witness gates
can rely on that, and a gated VWC is byte-indistinguishable from an ungated one.
So gating converts a property of the artifact into out-of-band knowledge about
the issuer, which is the opposite of what a portable, third-party-verifiable
credential is for.

**Annotation makes gating unnecessary; gating does not make annotation
unnecessary.** A verifier who requires co-presence demands `confirmed: true` —
and must do that check anyway, since they cannot otherwise know the witness
gated. Once the check exists, the gate adds nothing for the verifier.

**And the gate's cost lands on the wrong people.** Locality fails for boring
reasons — Bluetooth off, permission denied, a far corner of a hall, a sensor
whose adapter dropped. Under `required` each becomes "no credential for you", at
an event, in a queue. Meanwhile the attack it targets — two parties who agreed in
advance to fake a meeting — is not blocked by BLE at all. It inconveniences
precisely the population it is not aimed at.

**Decision.** `offered` is the default; `required` is a per-event deployment
choice with a stated door-experience cost.

**The precondition, promoted to a MUST because the argument depends on it.**
Annotation is only safe because failure is *representable*: `confirmed: false`
with a reason, never an omitted member. If a failed observation could be
expressed by leaving the member out, every implementation bug would be
indistinguishable from a policy outcome and gating would become the only honest
option. This is why the plan states the emit-negatives rule normatively and
tests it, rather than leaving it to implementer taste.

## Finding 15 — witness-free peer-to-peer locality is closed, not deferred

**Decision.** Keyring will not offer a locality claim for an unwitnessed
exchange, and will not emit an evidence member that could be read as one.

**The argument, in one line:** in a two-party exchange the set of observers is
exactly the set of interested parties, so any artifact the honest run produces,
the agreed run produces identically. Hardware attestation does not help — it
raises the claim to "two genuine attested apps produced this", which two
cooperating owners of genuine phones satisfy by definition.

**What this supersedes.** The July notes carried three witness-free options in
the mechanism ladder — peer-to-peer NFC tap, QR/local-channel peer exchange, and
ambient beacon witnessing — each rated as weaker rather than as impossible. That
framing was wrong in kind, not in degree: they are not a low rung, they are not
on the ladder. The same notes already contained the reason, in the line "willing
collusion between two consenting parties is the one threat no decentralized
option fully closes"; I had recorded it as a residual rather than following it to
its conclusion.

**Scope of the closure.** It is about the *locality* axis only. An unwitnessed
exchange still carries ceremony and device evidence, which remain worth having
and are unaffected.

## Finding 16 — NFC promoted from "accommodated" to specified

**Decision.** The NFC kiosk becomes a designed phase 2 (plan §4.4) rather than a
tier the evidence typing merely leaves room for.

**Why it deserves the detail now, while it is not being built.** It is the only
mechanism in the plan that closes willing collusion, so it is the tier someone
will reach for the moment an event needs a claim BLE cannot support — and that
is exactly the moment when nobody wants to rediscover the platform constraints.
Writing it while the binding design is fresh also proved something useful about
the design itself: the kiosk changes the *rendezvous* and nothing else. The
transcript, the evidence assembly, the retention, and the verifier are the same
code, which is why §4.4's acceptance criterion is that the unmodified `ref-06p3`
verifier accepts an NFC transcript. If it cannot, the `method` typing has failed
and that is the finding.

**The external fact it is blocked on**, named as blocked rather than unknown:
Apple grants HCE card emulation only under an entitlement issued case by case.
If we do not have it, iOS inverts to the phone-reads-a-kiosk-tag direction, which
Core NFC supports but which cannot mint a nonce inside the tap and is strictly
weaker. Phase 2 scheduling should not be committed before that is answered with
a citation.

## Finding 17 — two suites, and I had conflated the question

Brendan challenged the `eddsa-rdfc-2022` claim in Finding 11: weren't we moving
to JCS? Both halves turn out to be true, about different layers, and the plan
was thin on which was which.

**Verified.** `docs/CRYPTO_SUITE_FOLLOWUP.md` records the credential decision as
shipped on 2026-07-17: `DataIntegrityProof` + `cryptosuite: eddsa-rdfc-2022`
when the counterparty speaks RCE ≥ 3, `Ed25519Signature2018` otherwise, with the
witness mirroring the presented VRC's family (`getMirroredJsonLdProofOptions`).
**Decision 3 considered the `jcs` variant and rejected it** — "matches Keyring's
DTG spec feedback §3; graph canonicalization already runs on-device". So Finding
11 stands for the credential.

The JCS move is real and is the **task layer**: the OpenVTC plan's §4.5, forced
by upstream #880 making REST `/auth/` require a DI-signed `auth/authenticate/0.1`
under `eddsa-jcs-2022`, and carried as Phase D item 2 ("real `eddsa-jcs-2022`
proof verification"). Every rung in the corpus already stubs task proofs with
`cryptosuite: "eddsa-jcs-2022"` — ref-06w4's `STUB_PROOF` is the shape. And DTG
Core Credentials mandates no cryptosuite for the VWC at all (pinned clone
`b89f389`); it uses JCS only to compute `credentialSubject.digest`.

**Why this matters rather than being a labelling nicety.** Locality evidence
lands in *both* artifacts, and they are signed under different rules:

- the `ext` transcript on the four Trust Task documents → `eddsa-jcs-2022` over
  RFC 8785 → **every member covered, no vocabulary work at all**;
- the assertion in the VWC's `witnessContext` → `eddsa-rdfc-2022` → **only
  members with defined JSON-LD terms are covered**.

**The design change this produced.** Put the full transcript in `ext`, where JCS
covers it for free, and keep the VWC assertion to the decision-relevant summary.
That shrinks Finding 11's vocabulary work from "every field of the transcript" to
a short stable list, and it puts the volatile part — sensor internals,
corroboration detail, future `method` variants — where adding a member never
requires a context change. Finding 11's defect and its round-trip test are
unchanged; its *surface* is much smaller.

**Process note, worth keeping.** Finding 11 was reached from one code comment in
`WitnessService.ts` mentioning `eddsa-rdfc-2022`. The conclusion held, but the
supporting claim was thin and the neighbouring layer was moving the other way —
a challenge from someone who remembered the JCS thread was what forced the
distinction. The plan now carries a named dependency table (header) precisely so
the next reader can check each coupling instead of inferring it.

**Also settled today:** the namespace root's hostname —
`atl.seas.harvard.edu`, so `edu.harvard.seas.atl.keyring`. Plan Q1 closed;
`ref-06p` can freeze fixtures.

## Finding 18 — `ref-06p` is green, and it sharpened Finding 11

Built and run the same day: `tsp-reference/ref-06p-locality-binding`, **23
checks**, no radios, on published artifacts (`@openvtc/trust-tasks` 0.9.0,
binding 0.2, framework SPEC.md 0.3, cred-spec `b89f389`).

**What it settles.** The four forgeries are rejected by named checks rather than
by argument — replay across sessions and a lifted transcript both die on
`taskDigestMismatch`, an EID copy dies on `unknownKey` and then on
`signatureInvalid` when the copier borrows the victim's key id, and ref-06w3's
counterfeit-`id` forgery dies the same way one layer down. The task→physical
direction is exercised too: a submitted transcript naming a nonce the sensor
never minted is rejected on `sensorNonceMismatch`, which is the check that stops
a session claiming an observation it did not earn. `ext` crosses the published
§7.2 pipeline byte-identically on all four documents, and a locality-blind peer
handles the same document and declines to echo a namespace it does not
understand — so the "no upstream spec change needed" claim is now run rather
than asserted.

**Where it corrected me.** Finding 11 said undefined JSON-LD terms are "an error
rather than a silent drop" under safe mode. That was half the story, and the
half I stated is the less dangerous one. The rung runs both: safe mode —
what the DI signing path uses — **refuses to canonicalize the credential at
all**, so the failure is loud at signing time; with safe mode off the members
**drop to zero quads** while still sitting in the JSON, which is the mode that
would actually ship and would produce evidence that looks signed and is not.
Both are now in the plan, and the rung authors the missing term list as
`fixtures/locality-context-terms.json` so the app and witness server can import
one agreed copy.

**Measured, so the plan stops estimating:** locality adds **1,927 bytes per
session** — transcript 523, witness observation 515, VWC assertion 551, sensor
directive 235 — on top of ref-06w's 2,213-byte retained pair.

**What it deliberately does not touch**, restated because a green rung invites
over-reading: no radios, no RTT, no relay, no proof verification (task proofs
are stubbed as in every rung), and a minimal inline-context document in act 6
rather than a full `credentials/v2` VWC. The timing bound in the plan is still a
placeholder — `ref-06p2` and `ref-06p4` are what turn it into a number, and
until they run, §5.5's honesty about what the bound buys is the only thing
standing between us and a claim we cannot support.

## Finding 19 — the suite question was already answered upstream, by us

Brendan asked whether VWCs should be JCS too, given recent cred-spec PRs. I had
built §6.1 on a suite split that a live proposal of ours is trying to remove.

**What I found.** [cred-spec #18](https://github.com/trustoverip/dtgwg-cred-spec/pull/18)
— Alberto's, open, updated the same day — already proposes exactly that for DTG
credentials: `DataIntegrityProof` with the suite in `proof.cryptosuite`,
**`eddsa-jcs-2022` RECOMMENDED**, `bbs-2023` as the selective-disclosure path in
a proof set. Its rationale names our own case: JCS needs no `@context`
resolution, so credentials verify offline — "including credentials formed in
person and synchronized later", which is the witnessed exchange described from
the outside.

So this plan's §6.1 documents a *current* state, not a stable one, and Finding
17's "two suites, two layers" framing is right about today and possibly wrong
about next month.

**What I changed, and what I deliberately did not.** The plan now marks the
direction of travel at §6.1 and makes the **term-definition work conditional**:
if #18's recommendation is adopted for VWC issuance, the JSON-LD vocabulary this
plan spent Findings 11, 17 and 18 characterizing is *deleted*, not built. Step 6
of §10.2 says so in its acceptance criteria — "either the terms are in … or the
suite moved and this step is struck with a line saying so" — which is the only
step in the plan a decision elsewhere can erase.

What I did not do is flip anything. #18 is open, and it says RECOMMENDED, not
MUST, so Keyring is conformant on `eddsa-rdfc-2022` today. Pre-emptively
reversing a shipped issuance path on an unmerged PR would be the same mistake in
the other direction.

**The transcript-in-`ext` / summary-in-the-VWC split survives either outcome**,
which is worth stating because it was justified by canonicalization in Finding
17. It is *also* justified by size and by the privacy point in §9.1 — a smaller
assertion is less location-revealing and cheaper to retain. A design that only
made sense under one suite would have been the wrong design.

**The three findings' status, since they read as a chain:** Finding 11's defect
is real and shipped in our tree today; Finding 18's measurement stands; Finding
17's "two canonicalizations" is accurate at the pin. What changes if #18 lands is
only whether we *fix* the vocabulary or *remove the need for it*. `ref-06p` act 6
measures the property either way, so it is evidence for the argument now and a
regression guard afterwards.

## Finding 22 — "drop RDF entirely" was right only under an assumption that is false

**The position I argued, and its reasoning.** With selective disclosure out of
scope, dropping RDF canonicalization entirely is clearly correct. The argument
holding it in place was Decision 7's dual-verify-forever — *"stored 2018
credentials make that verify path effectively permanent"* — and that premise is
void twice: we are pre-production, **and** `DEFAULT_CREDENTIAL_EXPIRATION_DAYS =
7` in both `vrc-manager.ts` and `WitnessService.ts`, so the entire credential
population self-clears a week after issuance stops. That would have been true
even in production; the "effectively permanent" claim was wrong on its own terms
for this credential type.

**Why it is superseded.** `bbs-2023` is RDF-canonicalized, and it is the only
path to member-level selective disclosure — cred-spec #18 says so explicitly,
which is why it proposes a *proof set* rather than a single suite. If ZKP
presentation is a requirement, RDF cannot leave the codebase.

**What survives, and it matters.** *"Keep RDF for `bbs-2023`"* is not *"keep the
legacy suites."* `Ed25519Signature2018` and `eddsa-rdfc-2022` issuance still go,
and so does the negotiation ladder, for exactly the reasons above. Conflating
those two questions is what made the first answer look like a bigger reversal
than it is: the end state keeps RDF machinery for one purpose and deletes it for
two others.

**Recorded because the shape of the mistake is reusable:** I answered a
narrower question than the one that mattered, decisively, and the decisiveness
was the problem — not the analysis. "Is there a reason not to drop X" is only
answerable against the full requirement set, and ZKP had been sitting in the
OpenVTC plan's §4.6 the whole time, listed as scope nobody had costed.

## Finding 23 — the member layout is the part that cannot be retrofitted

**The organising insight of this whole revision.** A second proof can be added to
a credential later without changing the credential. **A credential's member
layout cannot be re-cut once credentials exist** — every issued artifact carries
the old shape, and verifiers written against it.

So the sequencing is:

1. Fix the layout now (Finding 24), while nothing is issued.
2. Author the terms and land the guard (Finding 28).
3. Switch issuance to `eddsa-jcs-2022`, drop the legacy suites.
4. Add the `bbs-2023` proof and SD presentation when tooling is answered.

Steps 1–2 are worth doing **even if step 4 slipped indefinitely**. Step 4 is
worth nothing if step 1 was skipped. That asymmetry is why Brendan's "don't worry
about bbs-2023 tooling right now" is a safe instruction rather than a deferral of
the hard part — the hard part is the shape, and the shape is decidable today
without a single line of BBS+.

## Finding 24 — six rules for the assertion, each with a failure it prevents

The old assertion was a nested object with a key id and a residuals array. Every
one of those choices is wrong under selective disclosure. The new shape and its
reasons:

**1 · Flat — no nested object.** BBS+ discloses at the level of RDF quads, and a
nested object is a blank node whose path must be revealed before anything under
it can be disclosed. `keyBinding{}` and `corroboration{}` therefore cost
granularity for nothing; they became flat members.

**2 · Prefixed `locality*` — required, not stylistic.** cred-spec already defines
`witnessContext.method`. An unprefixed locality `method` collides with it. This
one is a plain correctness fix that the flattening exposed.

**3 · Predicates, not identifiers.** `localityKeyMatchesCredentialSigner` asserts
the relation the witness verified in-session; the **device key id leaves the
credential** and lives only in the artifact-side transcript. See Finding 25.

**4 · Tiered by disclosure.** Tier 1 (`localityConfirmed`, `localityMethod`) is a
complete claim on its own — *a witness I trust says these parties were
co-present, BLE tier* — with no venue, no time, no sensor, no corroboration.
Tier 2 adds venue/time/provenance; tier 3 is forensic and opens the artifact
side. `ref-06p` asserts a tier-1 subset canonicalizes on its own, so the tiering
is a property of the shape rather than an intention.

**5 · Negatives emitted, never omitted.** Unchanged from the earlier design, and
now more important: under SD a *withheld* member and an *absent* member look
similar to a careless verifier, so the three states — confirmed, attempted-and-
failed, witness-does-not-do-locality — have to stay mechanically distinguishable.

**6 · No `residuals` member.** New, and the sharpest of the six. What a method
cannot exclude is a deterministic function of the method, so a verifier who reads
`localityMethod` already knows the residuals. Carrying them as a disclosable set
would let a holder **reveal the flattering half of a threat list** — disclose
`venue-scale-range`, withhold `rf-relay` — which is strictly worse than not
carrying them. The residual table lives in the plan's §9.2 where nobody can
cherry-pick it.

*General principle, worth stating once:* **under selective disclosure, every
member you add is a member a holder can withhold.** Anything whose value depends
on being seen *together with* its siblings does not belong in a credential.

## Finding 25 — the hardware key is a correlation vector, and I called it a bonus

**What I wrote in Finding 4:** signing the radio transcript with the long-lived
Secure Enclave key "gives the notes' shared-key continuity signal for free — the
same key chains a person's meetings across a day — without building anything for
it now."

**Why that is backwards under ZKP.** A stable key id appearing in every VWC links
every ceremony its holder ever performed. The unlinkability the `bbs-2023` half
exists to provide is defeated by a field we were putting in for free. I described
the correlation as a feature because I was evaluating it against forgery, not
against privacy.

**The fix, and why it costs nothing.** The witness sees both keys during the
ceremony, so it can verify the relation itself and assert it as a predicate. The
credential says `localityKeyMatchesCredentialSigner: true`; the key id stays in
the transcript on the artifact side, where a verifier who genuinely needs to
re-check the binding can open it — and pays the linkability for that specific
show, deliberately.

**What continuity becomes.** Still available, but as a proof a holder *chooses*
to make, never a property of artifacts they hand out. That is the right shape for
it and it should be designed that way if it is ever wanted.

## Finding 26 — the tension between evidence and unlinkability, stated rather than hidden

Locality evidence and unlinkable presentation pull against each other. The
OpenVTC plan's §4.6 already records the ZKP task force's finding: presenting
outcome evidence contributes the longest-lived linkage in a bundle, so a show
that includes it forfeits unlinkability.

That is not a problem to solve; it is a trade to structure. Tiering is how:

- **Tier 1 is designed to be sufficient**, so the default show never needs the
  artifact side opened. If a verifier can be satisfied by "a witness attests
  co-presence, BLE tier", the holder pays nothing.
- **Tier 3 is where the price is**, and it is explicit. Opening the transcript to
  prove the key binding costs the show's unlinkability, and the plan says so at
  the verification step rather than leaving it to be discovered.

Nothing gets both fully. What the design buys is that **the holder chooses when
to pay**, per show, instead of paying once at issuance for every future
presentation.

## Finding 27 — commitment, but bounded

The ZKP TF's witness seat signs a `collision_commitment` over the encounter's
*public* parts rather than a digest of the credential, so the witness "never
learns the shared secret". We adopt the pattern — the assertion carries
`localityEvidenceCommitment` (a multihash over the JCS transcript, hiding because
the sensor nonce supplies entropy) rather than the transcript's contents.

**The boundary matters, and "commitments instead of digests" would be wrong as a
general claim.** cred-spec's `credentialSubject.digest` is REQUIRED and stays —
it binds the VWC to the specific VRC and #14 made it mandatory. What becomes a
commitment is the *evidence summary*, not the credential's edge binding. Two
different jobs; conflating them would break the pairing check that makes a VWC
identify an edge at all.

One honest difference from the TF's construction: our witness genuinely knows the
venue and the time — it *is* the venue. There is no secret to keep from it. The
commitment is not protecting the holder from the witness; it protects the holder
from every future verifier who does not need the detail.

## Finding 28 — the vocabulary stops being conditional and becomes CI

Finding 19 marked the JSON-LD term work conditional, on the theory that a move to
JCS would delete it. Under the proof set it is **mandatory**, and for a reason
stronger than signing: a member with no term never enters the RDF dataset, so it
is not merely unsigned under the `bbs-2023` half — it is **undisclosable**, since
a derived proof discloses from the dataset. Rule 4's entire privacy design
depends on the terms existing.

Given that, "define your terms" cannot stay a discipline. We have already failed
it once, silently, in shipped code. So the plan carries a **guard** (§10.3 item
12): a test that expands every credential shape we issue against the real context
document and fails on any dropped term, with the acceptance criterion that
deleting one term turns the suite red. `ref-06p` act 6 is the prototype; the
guard is that act pointed at production shapes.

**This is the durable answer to Finding 11**, and it is worth noticing that the
fix is not "be careful" but "make carelessness fail loudly" — the only kind of
fix that survives a team and a year.

## Finding 29 — rejected: locality as its own credential

**The option.** Issue co-presence as a separate credential rather than as members
of the VWC's `witnessContext`, so a holder can present relationship evidence and
locality evidence independently.

**Why it is rejected.** `bbs-2023` already gives member-level control, which is
the only thing the separation would buy — and it buys it *within* one credential,
without the costs. A second credential doubles retention, pairing and revocation
burden, needs its own `taskContext` binding and its own outcome evidence, and
abandons the `witnessContext` seat cred-spec defines for exactly this. The
separation solves a problem the suite already solves.

**Recorded because it will be re-proposed.** "Split it into its own credential"
is the intuitive answer to "this credential reveals too much", and it is only
wrong if you know that member-level SD is on the table.

## Finding 30 — deferred deliberately: BBS+ tooling and the issuer's second key

Not investigated, on Brendan's instruction, and recorded as a note to pick up
rather than an omission:

- **What `bbs-2023` implementation we can consume** — what exists in the
  `@digitalbazaar` / `@digitalcredentials` families, whether it composes with our
  Data Integrity path, and **whether BLS12-381 runs on Hermes**. That last is the
  same class of problem the noble HPKE work solved for TSP (no WebCrypto on
  Hermes), and plausibly has the same answer: a pure-JS curve implementation.
  `@noble/curves` already ships BLS12-381, which is a promising start and not a
  survey.
- **The issuer's BLS key.** Every DTG credential issuer needs one alongside its
  Ed25519 key — the witness server, and the wallet, since VRCs are wallet-issued.
  A DID adds a key rather than rotating one (openvtc §4.6), so this is a
  DID-document change to design rather than a migration to run.

**Why deferring is safe** (Finding 23): the layout is fixed without any of it,
and the second proof is additive. **Where it stops being safe:** at §10.3 item 13,
selective-disclosure presentation, which is the first step that cannot proceed on
an unanswered tooling question. Check it then, not before.

## Finding 31 — what this revision did to the rung

`ref-06p` moved from 23 checks to **26**, and its assertion was re-cut to the new
shape. Three new checks encode Finding 24 so the design cannot drift back:

- the assertion is **flat** — no member is an object, and its key set is exactly
  the three tiers;
- the credential contains **no key id**, while the artifact-side transcript does;
- a **tier-1-only show** canonicalizes on its own and every quad in it is present
  in the full credential's dataset — which is what makes the tiering real rather
  than aspirational.

Evidence cost moved from 1,927 to **1,887 bytes per session** (the assertion
shrank from 551 to 511 as the key id and residuals left). The term list grew its
own justification: fourteen definitions that are now mandatory implementation
input rather than a conditional artifact.

*Not in the rung, deliberately:* no BBS+ proof is produced or derived. The rung
fixes and measures the **shape**, which is the part that has to be right first —
and, per Finding 23, the part that cannot be fixed later.
