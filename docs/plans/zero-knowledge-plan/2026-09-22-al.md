# 2026-09-22 — AL: opening the zero-knowledge workstream

**Plan:** [`../zero-knowledge-plan.md`](../zero-knowledge-plan.md)

## Question

The workstream opened with three questions: how does the ZK technology work, where does it live (on the device or in the VTAs), and how compatible is it with Keyring, taking *The Agent Is the State* as the governing principle. The working premise was that the VTA and VTC carry the heavy lifting and the UX layer does not, which would make the second question close to a non-question. This session checked whether the public evidence agrees.

## F1 — Proving belongs in the VTA. Supersedes the 2026-08-05 position that proofs are generated on the device

**Concluded.** Keyring runs no prover and holds no ZK secret (plan §3.1).

**Evidence, public only.** (1) VTI's credential architecture, at our pin `187ad9cd`, calls the Groth16 path *"server-side VTA proving"* and says a holder needs no BLS key to derive a disclosure. Its `credential-exchange/present` already assembles `Bbs2023` presentations from the VTA vault, so the agent is already the proving party for the format we would use. (2) *The Agent Is the State*: *"Do not build a second copy of anything the agent already is."* (3) Our own reasoning, in the plan's §3.1: rate limiting without names needs a component awake on its own schedule, which a phone is not.

Added for the phone case: neither the Secure Enclave nor StrongBox supports BLS12-381 or BN254. A device-held ZK secret would therefore be a software key, and on-device proving gains no hardware custody.

**Supersedes.** The local research notes of 2026-08-05 (`docs/spikes/20260805-tsp-zkp-research-notes.md`, not committed) said: *"Keyring = holder; proofs generated on-device behind the biometric gate"*, and that Keyring would later install *"circuit artifacts (few MB) + a prover (snarkjs WASM or a native RN module)"*. That was written from the DTG ZKP lab's framing, where phones are the proving tier ("mediated proving tiers"), and before either upstream design above existed. It is replaced by the plan's §3.2: the biometric gate stays, but it gates the VTA's operation through a step-up approval rather than a local prover.

**Not superseded.** `ref-03d`'s measurement that BLS12-381 runs on Hermes at about 15× Node. It still answers `openvtc-integration-plan.md` §4.6's open "whether BLS12-381 runs on Hermes" question, and the answer is yes. The plan now places that capability as a fallback, not a design.

## F8 — The work in flight decides how Z1 attaches, and one of it is a precondition

**Concluded.** Plan §7.0 and the ZK3 rewrite.

**Evidence.** Three pieces landed or opened while this plan was held. `pickOwnVetterGrant` (keyring-bifold#70) made one chooser authoritative for a vetter's grant and split refusal into two points — the desk refuses to cut a ticket, the module refuses to redeem one already in the wild. The desk pair (keyring-bifold#71, keyring-wallet#95) added standing wording and withheld "New ticket" while no grant is live, with a gate asserting the desk and the applicant name the same fact. A refusal harness exists (`E2E_REFUSAL=dead-grant`, `run-vetter-grant-lifecycle.js`).

**Why it matters to Z1.** *At capacity* is a third standing on that same machinery, not a new subsystem — but only one of the two dimensions may be visible. Grant standing is already public, so withholding on it is safe; token state is exactly what hidden mode hides, so it may surface only as a per-request decline. Writing that down now prevents the natural implementation, "grey out the desk when out of tokens", which would reintroduce the busy-vetter signal the design removes.

**The precondition.** A build with a baked-in lab cannot satisfy §3.5's operator separation, because one operator then holds the VTA, the mediator and the community. The dynamic-leg work — any VTA, any community by link, and a persona riding the mediator its own DID document names — is therefore not a sibling of Z1 but its testability precondition. Recorded in §7.0.

## F9 — Every citation in this plan is public (2026-09-23)

**Concluded.** The plan and this companion cite publicly available artifacts only: openvtc's `vetting-process.md`, VTI's credential-architecture note, the DTG ZKP task force's repositories, the three open spec issues, our own reference rungs and our own sibling plans. Design positions that rest on none of those are stated as **ours**, with the reasoning in view, rather than attributed.

**Why it was worth a pass.** This workstream began from material that is not public, and the first draft quoted it — including figures, finding numbers and the path of a non-public source document. Publishing that in a public repository would have leaked another party's unpublished design, and the standing rule here is that public artefacts cite public sources. The scrub removes the quotes and the protocol-specific identifiers (task names, key kinds, token parameters, message field names) and keeps the engineering: where proofs run, what the client must never do, and what remains unmeasured.

**What it costs, stated plainly.** Three claims in §2.2 and §4.1 no longer carry a citation, because the only source for them is not public. They are marked as ours, and ZK0 replaces them with citations to a specification once one exists. A reader who wants the provenance should ask Alberto rather than assume the claims are unsourced.

## F10 — §3.1 was over-general: the applicant's side is not the vetter's side (2026-09-23)

**Concluded.** Plan §3.1 now splits by role, and the applicant's placement becomes open decision D4. New rows in §4.1, and §4.1.1 on what a hidden-mode community must not require.

**What was wrong.** The plan argued "Keyring runs no prover" from three facts and applied all three to both roles. Two of them — the unattended collection schedule, and not keeping a second copy of what has been spent — are properties of the **vetter's** position. The applicant is not the anonymous party: the community learns their join DID at submit under either design, their proving secret is minted for one application and dies with it, and nothing about their side is rate-limited. So the strongest argument for the agent proving simply does not reach them, and the plan asserted a settled architecture where it had an assumption.

**What stands.** The vetter's side is unchanged and, if anything, better supported: rate-limiting an anonymous vetter requires collection on a schedule that does not track activity, which needs a component awake on its own.

**What changed in the plan.** §3.1 states the split and keeps "the VTA proves both sides" as a working assumption with its reasons, rather than as a finding. D4 names what would decide it: whether the published task family puts the applicant's engine behind the agent, whether offline submission is a requirement, and the cost of a second proving stack against `ref-03d`'s measured numbers.

**Three client requirements added to §4.1**, each reasoned from the goal rather than from any specification: fail closed on a criterion the client cannot honour, because falling back to named statements hands over what the mode withholds; the applicant's checklist cannot be a list of held credentials, because on this path no statement credential is issued; and a freshness challenge is single-use, so answering "needs more" means a fresh challenge and a fresh proof rather than re-sending a stored one.

**And one constraint on communities (§4.1.1)**, which lands on us specifically: a criterion requiring a relationship credential with a vetter cannot coexist with hidden vetting, because a VRC names both ends. Keyring is the client most likely to propose that, being the one that mints VRCs.

**Provenance.** This correction was prompted by material we cannot cite; nothing above depends on it. Each argument is stated from the construction's own goal and is checkable on its own terms, which is the test this plan applies to anything it cannot source publicly.

## F11 — Why a bespoke construction, and a caution about claiming novelty (2026-09-23)

**Concluded.** Plan §2.5 sets out why the standard primitives do not answer *k distinct attesters, unlinkable* — single-issuer selective disclosure says nothing about how many people signed; plain ring signatures give no distinctness and grow with the eligible set; linkable ones tie the tag to a ring that policy changes reshape; group signatures keep an opener who can name the signer; threshold signatures need the signers in one coordinated session, which vetting days apart is not; SNARKs need a per-circuit ceremony; STARK proofs are large for a mobile submission. These are textbook properties, so the section is checkable without reference to any implementation.

**Why it is in the plan at all.** The convention here keeps rejected alternatives as standing rationale, and "why not just use BBS+ / a ring signature / a SNARK" is the first question any reviewer asks. Without the answer written down, it gets re-proposed.

**The caution.** Marketing language around new constructions tends toward "first system that…". Anonymous-credential literature has long treated counting and limiting anonymous actors — *n*-times anonymous authentication, scoped nullifiers, pseudonym systems — and prior art should be checked before we repeat a novelty claim in a talk, a specification or an upstream comment. Nothing in this plan depends on the construction being first; it depends only on its being fit for this job. That is also the safer claim to make on someone else's behalf.
