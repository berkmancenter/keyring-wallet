# 2026-09-22 — Alberto (with Claude): opening the zero-knowledge workstream

**Plan:** [`../zero-knowledge-plan.md`](../zero-knowledge-plan.md)

## Question

The workstream opened with three questions: how does the ZK technology work, where does it live (on the device or in the VTAs), and how compatible is it with Keyring, taking *The Agent Is the State* as the governing principle. Maintainer side-channel guidance, recorded as received: *the VTA and VTC do the heavy lifting; don't push that into the UX layer.* That guidance makes the second question close to a non-question. This session checked whether the evidence agrees.

## F1 — Proving belongs in the VTA. Supersedes the 2026-08-05 position that proofs are generated on the device

**Concluded.** Keyring runs no prover and holds no ZK secret (plan §3.1).

**Evidence.** Three upstream sources agree independently. (1) The hidden-vetter review makes the VTA the PCS engine and openvtc *"the UI and message carrier only"*, and gives its reasons: VTA-authoritative state, the attest gate, the always-on token drip, and *"openvtc never has to carry arkworks"*. (2) VTI's credential architecture, at our pin `187ad9cd`, calls the Groth16 path *"server-side VTA proving"*. Its `credential-exchange/present` already assembles `Bbs2023` presentations from the VTA vault. (3) *The Agent Is the State*: *"Do not build a second copy of anything the agent already is."*

Added for the phone case: neither the Secure Enclave nor StrongBox supports BLS12-381 or BN254. A device-held ZK secret would therefore be a software key, and on-device proving gains no hardware custody.

**Supersedes.** The local research notes of 2026-08-05 (`docs/spikes/20260805-tsp-zkp-research-notes.md`, not committed) said: *"Keyring = holder; proofs generated on-device behind the biometric gate"*, and that Keyring would later install *"circuit artifacts (few MB) + a prover (snarkjs WASM or a native RN module)"*. That was written from the DTG ZKP lab's framing, where phones are the proving tier ("mediated proving tiers"), and before either upstream design above existed. It is replaced by the plan's §3.2: the biometric gate stays, but it gates the VTA's operation through a step-up approval rather than a local prover.

**Not superseded.** `ref-03d`'s measurement that BLS12-381 runs on Hermes at about 15× Node. It still answers `openvtc-integration-plan.md` §4.6's open "whether BLS12-381 runs on Hermes" question, and the answer is yes. The plan now places that capability as a fallback, not a design.

## F2 — Hidden vetting fits Keyring only under option B of the vetting subtask's §2.4

**Concluded.** Plan §3.3 is a conditional design.

**Evidence.** The PCS `usk` is a VTA key kind, and the drip needs an always-on agent. Under option A there is no VTA. My memory notes record B as decided in a session on 2026-09-17, but `community_vetting_subtask.md` §2.4 on `origin/main` (`63e44b0`) still reads **Open**. The plan follows the committed document and flags the difference here rather than acting on the note.

## F3 — Keyring's own credentials need a hand-off, not a mirror

**Concluded.** The in-person exchange stays device-signed. The SD/ZK half is produced by the VTA from a deposited copy (plan §3.4).

**Evidence.** `community_vetting_subtask.md` §2.6 keeps Keyring's own credentials as durable local state, with no VTA in the in-person path, so ZK over them cannot be VTA-only unless the VTA receives a copy. `pnm_cnm_subtask.md` step 1 already plans the vault client (`vault/credentials/receive`).

**Open.** Two facts are unmeasured: whether an R-DID can carry a BLS G2 key (VTI says *"only durable `did:webvh` issuers mint BBS credentials"*), and whether a proof set accepts a proof added after issuance. Recorded as D2.

## F4 — The operator-separation rule reaches the Farm plan

**Concluded.** A Farm that hosts both a member's VTA and the community's VTC voids hidden mode for that member (plan §3.5). No client-readable signal for this exists yet (B5).
