# 2026-09-22 — AL: opening the zero-knowledge workstream

**Plan:** [`../zero-knowledge-plan.md`](../zero-knowledge-plan.md)

## Question

The workstream opened with three questions: how does the ZK technology work, where does it live (on the device or in the VTAs), and how compatible is it with Keyring, taking *The Agent Is the State* as the governing principle. The working premise was that the VTA and VTC carry the heavy lifting and the UX layer does not, which would make the second question close to a non-question. This session checked whether the public evidence agrees.

## F1 — Proving belongs in the VTA. Supersedes the 2026-08-05 position that proofs are generated on the device

**Concluded.** Keyring runs no prover and holds no ZK secret (plan §3.1).

**Evidence, public only.** (1) VTI's credential architecture, at our pin `187ad9cd`, calls the Groth16 path *"server-side VTA proving"* and says a holder needs no BLS key to derive a disclosure. Its `credential-exchange/present` already assembles `Bbs2023` presentations from the VTA vault, so the agent is already the proving party for the format we would use. (2) *The Agent Is the State*: *"Do not build a second copy of anything the agent already is."* (3) Our own reasoning, in the plan's §3.1: rate limiting without names needs a component awake on its own schedule, which a phone is not.

Added for the phone case: neither the Secure Enclave nor StrongBox supports BLS12-381 or BN254. A device-held ZK secret would therefore be a software key, and on-device proving gains no hardware custody.

**Supersedes.** The local research notes of 2026-08-05 (`docs/spikes/20260805-tsp-zkp-research-notes.md`, not committed) said: *"Keyring = holder; proofs generated on-device behind the biometric gate"*, and that Keyring would later install *"circuit artifacts (few MB) + a prover (snarkjs WASM or a native RN module)"*. That was written from the DTG ZKP lab's framing, where phones are the proving tier ("mediated proving tiers"), and before either upstream design above existed. It is replaced by the plan's §3.2: the biometric gate stays, but it gates the VTA's operation through a step-up approval rather than a local prover.

**Not superseded.** `ref-03d`'s measurement that BLS12-381 runs on Hermes at about 15× Node. It still answers `openvtc-integration-plan.md` §4.6's open "whether BLS12-381 runs on Hermes" question, and the answer is yes. The plan now places that capability as a fallback, not a design.

## F10 — §3.1 was over-general: the applicant's side is not the vetter's side (2026-09-23)

**Concluded.** Plan §3.1 now splits by role, and the applicant's placement becomes open decision D4. New rows in §4.1, and §4.1.1 on what a hidden-mode community must not require.

**What was wrong.** The plan argued "Keyring runs no prover" from three facts and applied all three to both roles. Two of them — the unattended collection schedule, and not keeping a second copy of what has been spent — are properties of the **vetter's** position. The applicant is not the anonymous party: the community learns their join DID at submit under either design, their proving secret is minted for one application and dies with it, and nothing about their side is rate-limited. So the strongest argument for the agent proving simply does not reach them, and the plan asserted a settled architecture where it had an assumption.

**What stands.** The vetter's side is unchanged and, if anything, better supported: rate-limiting an anonymous vetter requires collection on a schedule that does not track activity, which needs a component awake on its own.

**What changed in the plan.** §3.1 states the split and keeps "the VTA proves both sides" as a working assumption with its reasons, rather than as a finding. D4 names what would decide it: whether the published task family puts the applicant's engine behind the agent, whether offline submission is a requirement, and the cost of a second proving stack against `ref-03d`'s measured numbers.

**Three client requirements added to §4.1**, each reasoned from the goal rather than from any specification: fail closed on a criterion the client cannot honour, because falling back to named statements hands over what the mode withholds; the applicant's checklist cannot be a list of held credentials, because on this path no statement credential is issued; and a freshness challenge is single-use, so answering "needs more" means a fresh challenge and a fresh proof rather than re-sending a stored one.

**And one constraint on communities (§4.1.1)**, which lands on us specifically: a criterion requiring a relationship credential with a vetter cannot coexist with hidden vetting, because a VRC names both ends. Keyring is the client most likely to propose that, being the one that mints VRCs.

**Provenance.** This correction was prompted by material we cannot cite; nothing above depends on it. Each argument is stated from the construction's own goal and is checkable on its own terms, which is the test this plan applies to anything it cannot source publicly.

## F11 — Why a bespoke construction, and a caution about claiming novelty (2026-09-23)

**Concluded.** Plan §2.5 sets out why the standard primitives do not answer *k distinct attesters, unlinkable* — single-issuer selective disclosure says nothing about how many people signed; plain ring signatures give no distinctness and grow with the eligible set; linkable ones tie the tag to a ring that policy changes reshape; group signatures keep an opener who can name the signer; threshold signatures need the signers in one coordinated session, which vetting days apart is not; SNARKs need a per-circuit ceremony; STARK proofs are large for a mobile submission. These are textbook properties, so the section is checkable without reference to any implementation.

**Why it is in the plan at all.** The convention here keeps rejected alternatives as standing rationale, and "why not just use BBS+ / a ring signature / a SNARK" is the first question any reviewer asks. Without the answer written down, it gets re-proposed.

**The caution.** Marketing language around new constructions tends toward "first system that…". Anonymous-credential literature has long treated counting and limiting anonymous actors — *n*-times anonymous authentication, scoped nullifiers, pseudonym systems — and prior art should be checked before we repeat a novelty claim in a talk, a specification or an upstream comment. Nothing in this plan depends on the construction being first; it depends only on its being fit for this job. That is also the safer claim to make on someone else's behalf.

## F12 — The member is a VTA persona, but the client signs for it; §3.3 said otherwise (2026-09-24)

**Concluded.** §3.3 is rewritten; §3.1, §3.2 duty 4 and D4 carry the consequence.

**What was wrong.** §3.3 described the decision as "the persona is the member, **the VTA signs**". The second half is not what was decided or built. The VTA mints the persona and holds its keys, but upstream's reference client fetches the secret with `keys/export-secret/0.1` and signs and seals locally — its join test states that submission *"never touches the VTA"* — and Keyring does the same, borrowing the persona's key-agreement and signing keys into the wallet's KMS (built in 220).

**Why it matters beyond a citation fix.** §3.1's supporting premise was that the agent holds secrets and the client asks it to act. For the ceremony layer that premise is false in the ecosystem's own client, which weakens the case for placing the **applicant's** proving in the VTA. The **vetter's** side is untouched: its argument was never custody but the unattended duties — collecting rate-limiting material on a schedule and holding one authoritative record of what has been spent.

**What follows.** D4 now names the two open questions from the subtask's companion — whether borrowed keys may persist, and where the membership card lives under the no-mirror rule — as the things that should settle it, and says plainly that this plan's working assumption is the weaker side of the argument. If borrowed secrets may persist, a phone-side applicant prover is consistent with what is built rather than an exception to it.

**A note on the source.** The restated §2.4 is in keyring-wallet#155, which was **open, not merged**, when this was written: `main` at `79722c9` still reads "Open. Two architectures are in play", and the companion is not on `main` either. The citation in §3.3 names the PR for that reason, so a reader who checks `main` and finds the old wording is not misled.

## F13 — Four changes from the operation layer's likely shape (2026-09-26)

**Concluded.** New §3.6, a salt rule and §4.2.1 in Z2, a badge duty in §3.2, and a time-bound list of pre-settlement asks in §8.

**1. The client's contract is with a declared extension, not a construction (§3.6).** Standardising one zero-knowledge method would freeze a young research area into a wire format, so the operation layer is better kept method-neutral: a community publishes its parameters in a namespace it controls, marks it as one a client must understand, and a client either honours it or refuses. Everything ZK1 builds — read the parameters, gate the attest step, render outcomes, refuse what it cannot honour — is the same whichever scheme a community picks. §2.2's comparison of constructions is background for a reader, not an interface the client branches on.

**2. Salting is optional to produce, mandatory to honour (§4.2).** A verifier that ignores a salt computes the wrong digest and rejects a valid credential; one that demands a salt rejects an unsalted one. Our read path must follow what the credential carries. This is the read-side half of the enumerable-digest problem already tracked there.

**3. Task completion becomes a credential, and disclosure is a choice with a cost (§4.2.1).** A confirmation — a claim that a named task completed, digesting that task, signed by whoever completed it, held and disclosed at the holder's choice — fits the credential/artifact wall rather than displacing it. But the task digest is the same durable correlator §4.2's first bullet already names, so two shows including one confirmation are linkable. Because disclosure is optional, a screen must ask, and the honest wording states that cost rather than presenting it as a free extra.

**4. A badge for non-disclosing presentations, with two cautions (§3.2).** It must say *nothing was disclosed* rather than *verified* or *private* — §2.4's boundary between disclosure and assurance, and the metadata that leaks around a perfect proof, both forbid the stronger wording. And it must look clearly different from the hardware-attestation badge, which claims something about the device rather than about disclosure.

**On D4, no change.** The working assumption that the VTA proves both sides stands, and the two questions from the vetting subtask's companion still decide it (whether borrowed keys may persist, where the card lives). F12's counter-signal also stands: the ceremony layer borrows persona secrets to the phone today.

**Why the list in §8 is time-bound.** A client-facing shape is cheap to add before other implementers depend on it and expensive afterwards, and the window for that is short.

## F15 — Both paths ship; D4 closes (2026-09-28)

**Concluded.** New §3.1.2, constraint C6-b, and D4 recorded as decided rather than open.

**The decision.** Agent-side proving is Keyring's default; an on-phone path ships alongside it for a holder who chooses it. F14's and §3.1.1's argument survives intact as the reason for the *default* — an exported proving key cannot be gated by our hardware-attested approval, and the agent already holds prover secrets of this kind without exporting them.

**Why it cannot be the only path.** [[ZKP-SPEC]]'s Accessibility Considerations section states that mediated proving *"MUST NOT be the only path available to a holder"*, with proving cost on constrained devices as the surrounding rationale; read together, the concern is that a holder is never forced to delegate. Independently of the specification, delegation means the agent can compute what the holder proves, and that is a trust choice to offer rather than to impose.

**Two things checked rather than taken on report.** First, the quoted sentence sits inside a section `spec/body.md` itself marks *"This section is informative"*, and the document's status is *"Proposed · 2026-08-25"*. A MUST NOT in an informative section has no clear normative force, so conformance cannot be claimed against it as written — recorded in §8 as an ask to upstream, because the right response to an apparent spec defect is to raise it, not to quietly pick a reading. Second, PR #11 routes hardware-bound keys — the Secure Enclave and StrongBox keys we hold — to **issuance-time attestation** opened at presentation, conditioned on the identifier's key profile, rather than to in-proof derivation. That matches §2.3's hardware fact and tells us where our hardware story attaches: at issuance and at approval, never inside the proof.

**What it costs us.** The phone path is a second prover to ship and maintain, and a proof made there cannot be gated by anything on the agent side — the honest table in §3.1.2 says so, and the UI must not present the two paths as equivalent.
