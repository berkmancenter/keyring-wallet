Building the Keyring core steps 7.2 (the pairing machine), 7.3 (the VDC) and 7.4 (the VTA client additions) showed that several acceptance lines cannot be checked as written. Some describe the pairing design D48 replaced. Some name a rule no step owns. One asks the VTA client for a narrowing the specification refuses. The review of the first code pull request asked that every change to these lines be proposed on its own, with the text it replaces in view, before any code is reviewed against them.

This companion is that proposal. Each finding gives the line as it stood, the line proposed and why. The plan's text in the same pull request is the proposed text. It takes effect only if Brendan accepts the pull request.

**It asks Brendan to rule on:**
- the eleven proposals below (findings 1 to 8 and 10 to 12), and the forced correction of finding 9, which needs a confirmation, not a decision;
- the number's length (finding 2). The old line said four and contradicted §3.2, D8 and §11 Q8, which say two until VTI accepts the ask for four. The line now agrees with them.

**Nothing is decided here.** What happens when an agent's six-character handle is also taken stays open (finding 5).

**Sources and commits.** The plan at `feat/agent-connect` `9aa6b7215` (the base of this pull request); the Trust Tasks specification at the pin, `dtgwg-trust-tasks-tf` `2a66a0351` (`scripts/openvtc/PINS.json`; the clone in `external/` checked against it). ### 1. 7.2: the version rule moves to the chat, step 7.9, and is written there

- **Old (7.2):** "every message carries `version`, another major is refused with `unsupportedVersion` and a higher minor with extra members is accepted".
- **Proposed (7.2):** "Keyring sends the `auth/oob` tasks at the versions of §3.2; a VTA that serves none of them refuses the claim, and Keyring stops with nothing granted (the `version` member and its rule for majors and minors are the chat control messages', §7.9, Step 6)".
- **Proposed (7.9, added):** "Every chat control message (`settings`, `agent-room-offer`, `revoked`, the VDC grant) carries `version` (§3.2, "Versions"): Keyring accepts its own major, and a higher minor whose extra members it ignores while the members it needs are present; it does not act on another major and shows "This version of Keyring and the plugin can't connect yet. Update Keyring or the plugin." (the plugin's side of the rule is §7.6's, Step 10)."
- **Why:** under D48 pairing sends no Keyring message. Its messages are `auth/oob` Trust Tasks, versioned by their task URIs (§3.2, "Transport" and "Versions"). §3.2 "Versions" gives the `version` member to the chat control messages. So the rule cannot be tested in the pairing machine, and leaving it only in 7.2 means no step owns it. It moves to 7.9, written out, so it is not lost. The reply "`unsupportedVersion`" is not carried over: a chat message has no refusal reply, and §3.2 says what Keyring shows instead. The plugin's side is already in 7.6 ("it keeps its last `settings` on an unknown `settings` version").
- **Plan sections touched:** §7.2, §7.9.

### 2. 7.2: the number's length aligned with §3.2, D8 and §11 Q8

- **Old (7.2):** "the number is four digits, zero-padded, the same on both sides and a four-digit entry is required".
- **Proposed (7.2):** "The number: Keyring takes exactly `MATCH_NUMBER_DIGITS` digits, one constant: two, the specification's `MatchNumber`, as §3.2 step 5, D8 and §11 Q8 have it; four only if VTI accepts the ask for four (AC-36, ask A12), and then that constant is the only change. An entry of any other length is not sent and uses no attempt".
- **Why:** the old line contradicted the rest of the plan, and this aligns it.
  - **§3.2 step 5:** "The number is the specification's: two digits … Until the specification carries four, the number is its two digits".
  - **D8:** "it is two while the specification keeps two".
  - **§11 Q8:** records four as an ask of VTI that "ships two if VTI declines".
  - **The upstream item:** the ask for four stays what it is, AC-36's ask A12 on trustoverip/dtgwg-trust-tasks-tf #1386. The blind-guess bound under two digits is the one §3.2 "Lockout" already states: 6 in 100 an hour.
  - **The code:** the length is one constant, so VTI's answer is a one-line change.
- **Plan sections touched:** §7.2.

### 3. 7.2: "the same on both sides … differs when `K_t` changes" is replaced by what Keyring can be held to

- **Old (7.2):** "the number is the same on both sides for the same inputs and differs when `K_t` changes"; and "Keyring never renders the number".
- **Proposed (7.2):** "Keyring never computes, holds or renders the number: it sends the typed digits unchanged in `prove`, and no state holds anything the VTA answers beyond the disclosure Approve shows. That the starter and the VTA compare one number, chosen afresh for each request, is the VTA's (§3.2 step 3), measured on the lab, not in Keyring."
- **Why:** the old property described the design before D48, where both sides derived the number from the pairing's keys. Under D48 the VTA chooses the number at the claim and shows it only to the starter (§3.2 step 3; step 5, "chosen by the service at claim and shown only to the starter"). Keyring has no derivation, so nothing in Keyring can be determined by `K_t`. What Keyring can be held to is stronger than "never renders": it never holds the number at all.
- **Where the old property is checked:** at the VTA. It covers two things: the number `prove` compares is the one `redeem` showed the starter, and each request gets its own number. Only a live `vti-oob-server` can show either, and it does not serve `deviceEnrol` yet (AC-36). **Proposed as a rung case for step 7.1**, not edited here because 7.1 is the test lane's. The case runs once a lab service serves `deviceEnrol` (AC-36): the rung opens two requests, shows their numbers differ, and checks that `prove` accepts the starter's number and declines another. It cannot run today, because no VTA at 0.79.0, 0.84.0 or 0.87.0 serves `auth/oob` (`unsupportedType`, step 7.1's fixtures `vta-<version>.json`). It gates the `deviceEnrol` path of step 7.5, not the P0 exit, which pairs by the fallback. The test lane has agreed to take it when the lab can serve it.
- **Plan sections touched:** §7.2.

### 4. 7.2: "a handle is spent" means the request id

- **Old (7.2):** "a handle is spent on cancel, wrong number, expiry and first claim".
- **Proposed (7.2):** "Keyring never claims a request id it has already claimed, cancelled, seen fail on a wrong number or seen expire."
- **Why:** in the same step, "handle" also names the agent's mention handle (§3.6). The request's state is the VTA's (§3.2, the service holds the request). Keyring's part is to refuse to claim an id twice, so that is the testable line.
- **Plan sections touched:** §7.2.

### 5. 7.2: the six-character collision stays open

- **Old (7.2):** "two agents with one name get different handles and a suffix collision extends to six characters" (§3.6 says no more).
- **Proposed (7.2):** the same, plus "what follows when the six-character handle is also taken is open (§3.6) and is not part of this acceptance".
- **Why:** §3.6 does not say. An earlier draft of this proposal decided it (fail the pairing), and the review rightly pointed out that a contract must not record a decision nobody made. The code fails safe meanwhile: it refuses the pairing with `handle-collision` and names the entry for removal. Its pull request says so. Two of one owner's agents would have to share their first 30 bits.
- **Asks:** a ruling in §3.6 when convenient. Nothing waits on it.
- **Plan sections touched:** §7.2.

### 6. 7.2: "every refusal in §3.10" becomes the rows that belong to pairing, with the others assigned

- **Old (7.2):** "jest covers every step and every refusal in §3.10".
- **Proposed (7.2):** jest covers steps 1 to 10 of §3.2 and the fallback, and ten named rows of §3.10. Two more rows are assigned by name: "The plugin's refusal of a `redeem` naming another VTA is §7.6's (Step 10, the plugin), and the mediator's 403 and a refused self-grant are §7.9's (Step 6, the chat): pairing sends nothing through a mediator (D48)". - **Plan sections touched:** §7.2.

### 7. 7.2: the 251-byte limit binds the fallback's link

- **Old (7.2):** "`triggerLink.ts` gains `agent-connect` (draft, … at most 251 bytes, …)"; "a link over 251 bytes or with a lifetime over 300 s is refused".
- **Proposed (7.2):** the limit is the `agent-connect` link's (the P0 fallback's). The VTA's own `auth/oob` link, the sign-in flow with the VTA's DID in `_from`, is not held to 251 bytes until step 0.1(h) measures its length. The parenthesis "(extending the flow config if it cannot yet accept a `did:key` contact)" is dropped, because the flow config already takes `allowedVidTypes`.
- **Why:** §3.2 and AC-16 already measure the `auth/oob` link's length in 0.1(h), because it depends on the VTA's DID. A reader that refused it at 251 bytes before then could refuse a real code.
- **Plan sections touched:** §7.2.

### 8. 7.3: what "verify" checks, whose issuer, and where re-issue is asked

- **Old (7.3):** "the grant and acceptance verify, with `@context` …"; "the issuer is the persona"; "Re-issue: a grant is re-issued when 48 hours or less remain, at app start and on Extend, only while the entry is confirmed present".
- **Proposed (7.3):**
  - **Verify:** every member rule is checked on the credential, and its proof under the issuer's key. The proof is a `DataIntegrityProof` with `eddsa-jcs-2022` and `assertionMethod`, as `keys/sign-document/0.1` makes it (0.1(f)). jest checks the signer's requests and answers against that task's schema, and the proof through an injected verifier. The real verifier resolves the issuer's DID through the agent, so step 7.7 wires it.
  - **As first proposed, before 0.1(f) was measured:** "its proof through the verifier that matches the signer 0.1(f) names. Until then jest uses an injected verifier and a fake signer, and the real signer is a named seam."
  - **Issuers:** the grant's issuer is the connection's `ownerPersonaDid` and its subject `K_a`; the acceptance's issuer is `K_a` and its subject that persona.
  - **Re-issue:** a policy over a connection and the clock. When it is asked (app start, opening the agent list or details, Extend, as §3.4 lists them) is step 7.7's.
- **Why:**
  - **Verify:** the proof format depends on which VTA task signs as the persona, which is 0.1(f). Step 7.1 has since measured it on VTA 0.84.0 and 0.87.0: `keys/sign-document/0.1` with the persona's `signingKeyId`, returning the credential with an `eddsa-jcs-2022` proof. D19's envelope fallback is dropped there. The proposal names that proof, where it first named only the seam.
  - **Issuers:** "the persona" could mean any persona. `editable-multi-profile-plan.md` governs which one, and the connection records it (plan §3.11).
  - **Re-issue:** the old line named two triggers where §3.4 names four. A pure step can test the policy but not the triggers, which are screens.
- **Plan sections touched:** §7.3.

### 9. FORCED CORRECTION, §7.4 and §3.15 item 4: narrowing an entry's `act` goes through `acl/revoke` 0.3

**This is not a proposal.** The specification has never allowed what the old text says, so the ruling asked for is a confirmation, not a decision. It covers the narrowing in both places; the rest of the 7.4 line is finding 12, which is a proposal.

- **Old (§7.4):** "`narrowOrWidenCapabilities` (`acl/update`, capabilities and `act`)".
- **Proposed (§7.4):** "`narrowOrWidenCapabilities` (capabilities, and `act` widened, with `acl/update` 0.3; `act` narrowed with `acl/revoke` 0.3 `{kind: "act"}`, since `acl/update` 0.3 refuses a narrower `act` with `narrowingNotPermitted`; a change that does both narrows first)".
- **Old (§3.15 item 4):** "the owner narrows (removes a memory: `acl/update` shrinks `act`, effective on the agent's next request, §2.1)".
- **Proposed (§3.15 item 4):** "the owner narrows (removes a memory: `acl/revoke` 0.3 narrows `act` with `{kind: "act"}`, since `acl/update` 0.3 refuses a narrower `act`; effective on the agent's next request, §2.1)".
- **Evidence:** at the pin, `dtgwg-trust-tasks-tf` `2a66a0351`, `specs/acl/update/0.3/payload.schema.json`, member `act`, reads: "NARROWING THE ACT SCOPE IS A REVOCATION and a consumer MUST refuse it here with `narrowingNotPermitted`, directing the caller to acl/revoke". `specs/acl/revoke/0.3/payload.schema.json`, `$defs.NarrowAct`, reads: "Narrow the entry's act scope to `act`, which MUST be strictly narrower than the stored act scope". `acl/update` has refused narrowing since its first version: 0.1's `scopes` reads "NARROWING THE SET IS A REVOCATION and a consumer MUST refuse it here", from the task's introduction (trust-tasks-tf `d299cf637`, #150, 2026-07-28), and 0.2's `act` reads as 0.3's does. So upstream did not remove an option; the plan's text described one that never existed. `acl/update` 0.3 replaces `capabilities` wholesale in either direction ("A replacement that removes a capability the entry held is a privilege reduction"), so a capability change in either direction stays on `acl/update`.
- **Why "narrows first":** a change that does both never holds more than either end state.
- **Also for step 7.1 (not edited here; the test lane's):** 0.1(p)(5) reads "`acl/update` removing a memory from `act` refuses the agent's next memory call on it". At the pin that update is refused with `narrowingNotPermitted`. The measurement is the same with `acl/revoke` 0.3 `{kind: "act"}`, which is what Keyring sends.
- **Plan sections touched:** §7.4 (the method's description), §3.15 item 4.

### 12. 7.4: the writes never step down, the listings are read to their end, the measurements are named

The proposal half of the 7.4 line; finding 9 is its forced correction.

- **Old (§7.4, the acceptance):** "*Acceptance:* unit tests with the older-version fallbacks and with paged `device/list` answers; an entry is found by `deviceId` after its subject changed; the rung's measurements agree (0.1).".
- **Proposed (§7.4, the acceptance):** "*Acceptance:* unit tests: the access-list writes are sent at 0.3 only, and a VTA that serves no 0.3 is refused with a clear error and never stepped down to (0.1 and 0.2 cannot state purposes, and 0.1 not listed capabilities either, so a step down would write an entry that grants every purpose but `modelTraining`); `device/list` is read at 0.3, else 0.2, every page of either, and a listing counts as complete only when its last page says nothing remains; each payload validates against the specification's schemas at the pin; an entry is found by `deviceId` after its subject changed; the rung's measurements 0.1(b), (c), (d) and (l) agree.".
- **Why:**
  - **No step-down:** 0.1 and 0.2 have no `purposes` member, and 0.1 no listed capabilities (`acl/_shared` 0.1 to 0.3). An entry written there grants every purpose but `modelTraining`, wider than the owner chose. The VTAs the stack meets serve `acl` 0.3 (0.84.0 and 0.87.0, as step 7.1 measured); one at 0.79.0, which serves 0.2 only, is refused with a clear error and nothing is written.
  - **Listings read to their end:** §3.9's revoke counts on "a full listing (every page, no error)", and the old line named only paged `device/list` answers. The proposal says when a listing counts as complete.
  - **The schemas:** each payload is checked against the specification's own, so a test cannot accept a shape the VTA would refuse.
  - **Named measurements:** "the rung's measurements agree" named none, so it could not be checked.
- **Plan sections touched:** §7.4 (the acceptance).
