## F13 — Membership completes, two ways; the first vetter can be appointed

**Evidence.** Fixtures under `tsp-reference/ref-20-local-vetting/fixtures/`:

- `submit-0.2-invitation-allow.log` — an `InvitationCredential` issued by `POST /v1/invitations` for a fresh DID, presented inside `submit/0.2` on a community whose only criterion is the invitation: verdict `allow`, with the signed `MembershipCredential` and role `EndorsementCredential` **inline in the verdict**.
- A community with no criteria answers `refer`; the request is `pending` in the admin queue; `POST /v1/join-requests/{id}/decide` approves it and mints the same two credentials. The applicant was the phone's identity, and it appears on `GET /v1/members`.
- `vetter-grant-after-invitation.log` — `POST /v1/vetting/vetters` for the invited member: `201`.

**Position adopted.** The order a vetting community is stood up in is: invitation-only criterion → invite the first vetter's identity → it is auto-admitted → grant it the vetter role → add the vetting criterion. Criteria are conjunctive, so a community that asks for vetting from the start can never admit its first vetter (`submit-0.2-invited-requestMore.log`: invited *and* vetting-required → `requestMore`).

**Corrects.** `ref-20`'s own finding 7 ("seeding the first vetter is a dead end") and the private working notes that called this an upstream blocker. The dead end is real for the *administrator's* identity — `routes/invitations.rs` gates on the ACL, `vetting/vetters.rs` on the member roll — but not for a fresh identity. Recorded upstream-facing as VTI-01 (medium).

**Supersedes.** §2.5's "the vetter admitted first" as an unexplained step — the order above is the mechanism — and §3.2's last sentence, which now says *when* a vetting community may carry a non-vetting criterion rather than that it never may.

## F14 — Keyring holds no membership card, and the reason is architectural, not a missing handler

**Evidence.** After the manual approval the VTC pushed both credentials as `credential-exchange/issue/0.1` to the phone's identity (`vtc-service/src/credentials/delivery.rs`); they sit at the mediator, four messages deep, for a DID the app no longer remembers because it mints a new `did:peer:2` on every connect. Upstream's receiver is `vta-service/src/messaging/handlers.rs` `handle_credential_issue`, which files the credential in the **VTA's** credential vault; `pnm cred-vault` reads it; the browser plugin at the pinned commit holds no community credential at all.

**Position adopted — as an open decision, not a design.** Two architectures are in play and the subtask must choose before P6:

- **A — the phone is the agent.** What is built. The phone's identity is the member; the community mails it the card; everything the ceremony signs is signed on the phone. No VTA.
- **B — Keyring drives the user's VTA.** What §2.4 and §7 assume ("keys held by the VTA", "the legal name stored on the VTA in a dedicated face"). The VTA is the member and holds the card; Keyring enrols as its manager and is the control surface. Needs the enrolment step first (§9 request 2), against the local `alice` agent until a Farm can host it.

The transport, the screens and the invitation flow survive either choice; the Vetting Card is a signed object, so the choice decides which side the ceremony code lives on. Rungs that are the same under both go first: keep one identity across restarts; read the credentials out of an `allow` verdict and accept a later `credential-exchange/issue`; the invitation flow end to end. See the explainer *Joining a Community* for the two drawn side by side.

**Supersedes.** §2.4's first sentence stated as settled. §6 P4's "enrolment by QR" did not happen; the phone connected to the community directly instead, which is A.

## F19 — Decision: Keyring has its own VTA (option B), and what that means mechanically

**Decision (user, this session).** Every Keyring user has a Personal VTA; Keyring is its manager. The goal is not "the phone joins a community" but "the phone's agent does, and the phone drives it". Recorded in §2.4.

**Evidence for the mechanics.** `vti-setup` `developer/03-joining-a-community.md` (verified against VTA 0.17.0 / VTC 0.11.58): a person stands up a Personal VTA, installs the TUI, **mints a persona** (`did:webvh`, hosted by a DID-hosting server the VTA has rights on — *"No WebVH server available from the VTA"* otherwise), pastes the community DID, and the client submits the join request *as that persona*; an invitation can be pasted to skip approval. Upstream's VTA registers no join task of its own (no `vta/*` task mentions communities or joining); the `persona/*` family is what it exposes to a client. So: **the client runs the ceremony, the VTA holds the keys, signs, and receives the card.**

**What this changes.** The transport P4 built is kept; the identity it presents changes from a phone-minted `did:peer:2` to a VTA-minted persona, and signing moves to the VTA. The community-facing screens are unchanged. A new phase, P4b, carries enrolment, the persona, and the approver rung.

**What is not yet known, and blocks P4b's design.** How the reference client seals a DIDComm envelope *from* the persona when the persona's key agreement is in the VTA — the VTA packing and forwarding on its behalf, or the client holding the agreement key with only signing delegated. Measured first, against `OpenVTC/openvtc` at the baseline, before any of P4b is built.

**Supersedes.** F14's framing of the choice as open; §2.4 as written before this.

## F20 — The persona mechanic, measured: the VTA is a custodian, the client seals

**Evidence.** `OpenVTC/openvtc` at `9a2d174` (added to the pinned clones for this): a persona's keys are recorded as non-secret `KeyRef`s — *"Key material lives at the VTA and is fetched at runtime"* (`openvtc-core/src/config/account.rs`) — and when the client must act as the persona it calls `client.get_key_secret(key_id)` (`openvtc-core/src/config/keys.rs`, three sites), which is `spec/keys/export-secret/0.1` returning `privateKeyMultibase`, and then signs and seals **locally**; with no VTA session the persona is *"unavailable"*. `openvtc-core/tests/join_lifecycle_e2e.rs` states the consequence: join submission is *"pure DIDComm — the applicant talks to the VTC through a mediator and never touches the VTA."*

**Position adopted.** Under §2.4 B the community leg keeps addressing the community directly, as built; what changes is the identity presented — a VTA-minted persona — and where its key comes from: borrowed from the VTA over the manager's session and imported into the wallet's KMS for the session, never persisted elsewhere. `VtaClient` (manager session, who-am-I, contexts, servers, persona mint, key borrow), `VtiIdentityStore` (manager identity and personas as Credo generic records, behind an interface) and `vtaKeys` (Multikey and bare-seed import) are the implementation; `enrol-manager.sh` is the enrolment stand-in; `e2e/run-vta-enrol.js` drives it.

**Also measured, standing the personal VTA up:** a persona needs a hosting server the VTA is *registered* with, and a self-managed DID-hosting daemon without a mediator mints its own DID with no services so no VTA can register it (VTI-18); a force re-provision keeps a DID bound to a dead hostname (VTI-17); `pnm`'s resolver bursts into rate limits (VTI-19); a serverless mint prints a log nobody serves (VTI-20). With the daemon re-provisioned behind the shared mediator, `alice` registered it and minted a server-managed persona from the terminal, hosted and resolving at the daemon.

**Supersedes.** F19's open question. What remains open is only how a persona's `DIDCommMessaging` service is populated at mint (`addMediatorService`) so a community can deliver to it — the phone's probe asks for it; whether the terminal path set it is unverified.

## F21 — The invitation door, measured and built: DID-bound invitations, an unsigned presentation, a link as the missing channel

**Evidence.** `POST /v1/invitations` (VTC 0.11.58) answers `{subjectDid, validUntil, vic}` where `vic` is an `InvitationCredential` whose subject is the invited DID and whose `scopes` carry the role. The presentation that earns `allow` is **unsigned** (`{@context, type, holder, verifiableCredential:[vic]}`, ref-20 fixture): the community takes the holder from the sealed envelope's sender (VTI-9), so what binds the invitation to the applicant is the persona's key, not a signature on the VP. `allow` carries the card inline as `verdict.with.vmc` (`MembershipCredential`) and `verdict.with.roleVec` (`EndorsementCredential`, role `member`). A phone-minted persona advertises a `#vta-didcomm` service whose endpoint is the mediator's `did:peer` (`accept: didcomm/v2`) — a community can deliver to it.

**Consequence for the flow.** Because an invitation is bound to a DID, the persona must exist *before* the admin invites it — which is the wireframe's order: *share my identity → the admin invites it → the invitation reaches me → Join*. Upstream has no channel for the third step (the operator copies the credential); Keyring's stand-in is a link the admin's console can show as a QR, `keyring://vti/invitation?c=<base64url(vic)>`, and that link is the ask to the farm: push the invitation to the invitee's DID over DIDComm (§9 request 3).

**Built.** `VtiCommunityStore` (invitations, memberships), `vtiInvitation` (the link), `vtiJoin` (ensure persona → connect as it → manifest → submit with the invitation → keep the card), `VtaClient.ensurePersona` (one persona per community, reused across restarts), the My Agent tab's identity / invitations / communities / activity sections, and `e2e/run-vti-invite.js` with `invite-persona.sh` as the admin. Green on Android from the real tab, not the developer screen; the persona persisted across an app restart.

**Supersedes.** F20's open question (the persona's service is populated at mint). §7.1's S5 as "apply with an empty presentation" — the invited path is now the first real join.

## F22 — The approver protocol, measured: a held task, signed requests, a single-use grant

**Evidence** (`vta-service` 0.28 at `53a7cde4`, `trust_tasks/policy_gate.rs`, `consent_request.rs`, `task_consent.rs`). A policy is one rule per task type — `pnm approvals require <type> --consent --set <set>` — drawing on a named approver set (`pnm approvals approvers add <set> <did>`). When a manager submits such a task the gate **refuses** it with `permissionDenied`, `details.reason = "auth:consent_required"`, and `details.consentRequests`: the very `task-consent/request/0.1` documents it has just pushed to every approver, each signed by the VTA (eddsa-jcs-2022) and carrying `challenge`, `taskType`, `payloadDigest` (salted), `requester`, `approverSet`, `minApprovals`, `expiresAt`, and the task's declared side effects. An approver answers with `task-consent/decision/0.1` — exactly `{challenge, payloadDigest, decision: approve|deny, reason?}`, unknown fields refused — whose authority is the **proof on the document**, not a token: the proven signer must be in the set. At the threshold the VTA mints a single-use grant (10 min), pushes `task-consent/granted/0.1` to the requester (threaded on the request), and the requester **re-submits the same payload**, which consumes the grant.

**Consequence for Keyring.** The manager session already signs every document with the manager identity, so a phone in the approver set can decide with no new key. What the phone needs is (1) one session per VTA for the whole app, so the granted notice reaches whichever screen is waiting, (2) a place unsolicited messages go — consent requests to an Approvals section, granted notices to the waiting task — and (3) the wait-then-resubmit inside the client, invisible to the screen. Built as `vtaAgent` (the controller, mirroring `vtiAgent`), `VtaClient`'s consent wait and `decideConsent`, the My Agent Approvals section, `approver-setup.sh` (pnm) and `e2e/run-vti-approve.js` — Android as manager, iOS as approver, the policy on `keys/export-secret/0.1` so the moment a persona's key leaves the VTA is the moment a second person must say yes.

**Supersedes.** §6 P4b "approver rung: design open" — designed and being run.
