# 2026-09-04 — BM: non-core needs to be a stated constraint, not an implicit reading

Companion to [`keyring-on-the-vta-farm.md`](../keyring-on-the-vta-farm.md), written
on review of the plan as proposed. Settles nothing AL's measurements concluded —
§3 and §4 stand as written. This addresses only where the plan text lets a
reader draw a conclusion the plan's own structure does not support: that Farm
work could pull shared, core work onto its own timeline.

## What this document settles

1. VTA Farm support is valuable and worth building, and it is **optional and
   non-core**: no phase of `openvtc-integration-plan.md`, `pnm_cnm_subtask.md`,
   or `reference-app-sdk-packaging.md` is blocked on this plan, and none should
   become so as the work proceeds.
2. The one place the original text reads otherwise — §3.4's *"This plan is what
   makes it urgent rather than eventual"* — describes a real risk to *this
   plan's own* F0–F4, not a case for reprioritizing the parent's Phase D. The
   need for `did:webvh` resolution was never Farm-created.
3. The §7 contribution track (React Native as a supported OpenVTC runtime) is
   spare-capacity work in service of the Farm/ecosystem relationship
   specifically, not a precondition for any core phase.

## Why this needed stating, not just implying

The plan already gets the structure right: it is filed as a *sibling*, not an
edit to the parent's phases, `ref-16-farm-membership` sits at the end of the
ladder rather than inside it, and `ref-05-local-vta` stays the CI default. On
structure alone, "optional side path" already holds.

The wording is what drifts. §3.4, as written, reads:

> "Resolution is therefore on the critical path of every level, starting at
> L0... This plan is what makes it urgent rather than eventual."

Taken alone, that sentence licenses treating `did:webvh` resolution as
Farm-motivated work that should move up the parent's queue. It shouldn't,
because it isn't Farm-motivated in the first place:

- `openvtc-integration-plan.md`'s own ladder already proved the need with zero
  Farm involvement: *"ref-06x-cypress-stack ✅ ... local release-binary VTA
  (self-hosted webvh, capability ladder read wallet-side) ... Two measured
  findings: Credo 0.6.3 cannot resolve `did:webvh` (`unsupportedDidMethod` — a
  ~20-line wallet-side adapter suffices, proven; candidate Credo
  contribution)"* — done **2026-08-17**, against Keyring's own self-hosted VTA,
  three weeks before this plan's 2026-09-03 measurements existed.
- The same document lists it as **Phase D item 3**, on the parent's own
  roadmap: *"(3) the `did:webvh` resolver adapter on `didwebvh-ts`"*.
- `pnm_cnm_subtask.md` cross-references the same item and marks it explicitly
  shared: *"`did:webvh` resolution, shared with parent Phase D item 3."*

So the accurate claim is narrower than the original sentence: the Farm doesn't
change the adapter's priority on the parent's roadmap, it changes **this
plan's own exposure** to the adapter not existing yet — every level below F1
is blocked without it, where self-hosted work can sequence around Phase D's
own pace instead. That's a fact about F0–F4's fragility, not a lever on Phase
D's schedule. F1 may still end up building the shared adapter itself — the
same ~20-line surface either way — but Phase D is not blocked waiting on F1's
timeline, and F1 is written to stand on its own regardless of Phase D's.

The distinction matters because the plans are read by different people at
different times, per [`CLAUDE.md`](../CLAUDE.md)'s reason for writing plans
down at all: an implementer who reads only §3.4's urgency framing, months from
now and out of the conversation that produced it, has no way to recover that
the urgency was scoped to this plan's own risk rather than a directive to the
parent. The fix belongs in the plan, not only in this companion, which is why
it is stated at the approach in §1 and §3.4 themselves, not left as a
between-the-lines inference from "sibling."

## What changed in the plan

- A **Dependency direction** line in the header, alongside **Siblings**,
  stating the non-core relationship as plainly as the ownership split already
  is.
- A paragraph in §1 stating the constraint directly, with the same citations
  as above, and pointing to this companion.
- §3.4 reworded so the urgency claim is scoped to this plan's exposure, not
  the parent's pace, and explicit that F1 does not ask Phase D to move sooner.
- A sentence in §7's sequencing paragraph: the contribution track is
  spare-capacity work and phase-gates nothing, including the two items (#1,
  #2) cheap enough to open immediately.
- The `docs/plans/README.md` index row now carries the same one-clause
  summary, so it survives being read outside the plan itself.

## What this does not change

- None of §3's or §4's measurements, and none of the positions AL's companion
  adopted (hardware custody, no WebAuthn, the packaging-defect framing) are
  revisited here — they stand.
- F0–F4's scope and acceptance criteria are unchanged. This only makes
  explicit what their placement (a sibling document, an added terminal rung)
  already implied about their relationship to the parent plan.
- The rung-numbering note in §9 Q5 is untouched; it is a separate, narrower
  issue (a naming collision between this plan's `ref-16`, `pnm_cnm_subtask.md`'s
  `ref-10-pnm-core-hermes`, and `reference-app-sdk-packaging.md`'s own
  `ref-08-pnm-core-hermes`, which also collides with the parent's existing
  `ref-08-rn-hello` and is not fully accounted for by keeping `ref-10`) and is
  raised separately, not folded into this companion.
