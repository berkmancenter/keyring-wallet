## 3. What this supersedes

**It supersedes my own conclusion of an hour earlier**, recorded in the findings
doc at 1.21, that "the Farm is far behind us". That was generalised from the
mediator alone, before the VTA and VTC had been measured, and it is wrong in the

## 4. The decision: the intermediate step goes ahead, scoped

**The protocol surface is at parity.** Every Trust Task our client issues —
persona minting, contexts, `webvh` DIDs, the vetting family — is served by the
VTA and the VTC, and both are exactly our versions. So a Farm run exercises the
*same contract* we have been testing, against infrastructure we did not build.

**That last clause is the whole value, and the lab cannot supply it.** Our green
ceremony currently depends on: six services behind `*.ngrok.app` sharing one
wildcard certificate (which produced today's `421` from HTTP/2 coalescing), a
`RUST_MIN_STACK` export without which a VTA aborts, hand-built ACLs in two
directions, a DID host registered by hand, and a **raised
`queued_send_messages_per_peer`** — our one declared fixture deviation. None of
those exist on the Farm. A ceremony that passes there has none of our fixture's
assumptions underneath it.

**What the old mediator changes is delivery, not protocol.** VTI-29, VTI-30 and
VTI-31 can all still appear on the Farm, because their fixes (#828 → 0.27.0,
#830 → 0.28.1, #834 → 0.28.x) are newer than what it runs. Those must not be
read as regressions there.

There is an upside to that, and it is not a consolation prize: the Farm runs the
**default** `queued_send_messages_per_peer`, which is the limit VTI-29 says is
too low. If VTI-29's shape reproduces on the Farm it is an independent
reproduction on infrastructure that is *not ours*, at the stock setting, which
is materially stronger evidence than anything our fixture can produce.

**Out of scope on the Farm: anything needing a community we administer.**
`vtc.ic3.dev` is shared and carries one `vtc_did`; we are members of it, not its
administrators. Publishing criteria, granting and revoking a vetter, and driving
a capacity limit therefore stay in the lab, exactly as the lab-and-field rule
already says. The refusal paths (B4) are lab-only and this does not change that.

## 7. Parity, and who can actually deliver it

Only the mediator differs, so parity is one decision, not a programme. Three
ways to get it, and they are not equal:

- **We match the Farm.** Requires downgrading our mediator below 0.26.5, which
  costs exactly what era E just bought: the ability to say the ceremony passes
  on upstream's current main. Rejected — it trades a claim upstream wants for a
  claim only we want.
- **They advance the Farm's mediator.** One deployment on their side puts every
  component at par and serves both claims at once. This is the ask, and it is
  small: their VTA and VTC are already current, so it is their mediator alone
  that is trailing its own stack.
- **We run both and label every result with its era.** The discipline already
  exists and is not optional either way; it is what makes a mixed-version world
  reportable. It is a mitigation, not parity.

**Position: ask for the advance, keep the era discipline regardless, and do not
downgrade.** Until the Farm's mediator moves, a Farm run is a *protocol* claim —
which is sound, since VTA and VTC are at parity — and not a *delivery* claim.
Anything delivery-shaped seen there (VTI-29, VTI-30, VTI-31) is the old
mediator until proven otherwise.

**Our own end first, though.** Before asking anyone to advance anything, the
repository should be able to state what we ran without someone reading a
changelog. That is a precondition for the feedback, not a chore after it.

## 8. The running stack, not the pin files — what actually differs

The pin manifests are reference bookkeeping. This is the software that is
actually up, compared like for like.

| | Lab | Farm | At par? |
|---|---|---|---|
| `vta-service` | 0.34.1 | 0.34.1 | **yes** — and both equal upstream main |
| `vtc-service` | 0.11.58 | 0.11.58 | **yes** |
| mediator | 0.28.9 | older than 0.26.5 | **no** |
| DID hosting | 0.8.3 (`35244b7`) | not exposed by any probed surface | unknown |
| `cors_allow_origin` | `"*"`, patched in by `up.sh` | default (closed) unless they changed it | **no** — deliberate, ours |
| per-peer queue gate | in the binary, **absent from our config** | **absent from the binary** | **no** — structurally |

### The mediator gap is not a setting, and that is the finding

Per-peer gating arrived with #828 in 0.27.0. The Farm's mediator predates it,
so the gate is not misconfigured there — **it does not exist in that build**.
No amount of tuning our config reproduces a Farm mediator's behaviour while we
run 0.28.9, and no tuning on their side reproduces ours.

So the lab cannot be made to match the Farm by configuration. It is a choice
between two labs:

- **A Farm mirror** — mediator downgraded below 0.26.5 and the CORS wildcard
  dropped. Comparable with the Farm, and unable to say anything about upstream
  main.
- **An upstream-main lab** — what we have. Says the ceremony passes on current
  main, and requires every Farm result to be read as a protocol claim rather
  than a delivery one.

Position unchanged from §7: keep the upstream-main lab, ask them to advance,
and label. But the reason is now sharper — parity with the Farm is not
something we can configure our way into, so pretending otherwise would be the
expensive mistake.

### Our mediator config is a fossil, and that is a third state

`~/vti-stack/mediator/conf/mediator.toml` is **658 lines against upstream's
754**, because `up.sh` generated it with an older `mediator-setup` and nothing
has regenerated it since. It carries no `queued_send_messages_per_peer` at all,
and its `queued_send_messages_soft`/`hard` are 200/1000 where upstream now
ships 2000/10000.

So a 0.28.9 binary is running a 0.26-era configuration: neither current nor
Farm-like, and **not** the "raised sender-queue limit" the findings document
declares as our one deviation — 200/1000 is *below* today's defaults, not above
them. That declaration was true when written and is now wrong in direction.

Regenerating is the fix and it is not free: `mediator-setup --force-reprovision`
mints fresh identity material, so the mediator DID changes and cascades into
every VTA's configuration and `app/.env`, which react-native-config bakes at
native build time. Scheduled rather than done mid-session.

## 9. Cutover done, and the first Farm result

`app/.env` now points at the Farm (`use-stack.sh farm` switches back and
forth; the values are baked at native build time, so each switch needs a
rebuild). The app is built and running against it on the iOS simulator.

**The first run fails at the first step, and the lab could never have found
it.** Connecting the agent reports:

```
Unable to resolve did document for did
'did:webvh:Qmag…:dids.ic3.dev:firstperson-mediator': notFound resolver_error
```

That is the Farm's **mediator** DID, and the phone cannot resolve it.

What has been ruled out, so the next person does not repeat it:

- **Not the server.** `https://dids.ic3.dev/firstperson-mediator/did.jsonl`
  answers `200`, and so do the VTA's and the VTC's.
- **Not the path segment.** The obvious hypothesis was that our resolver cannot
  handle a path-bearing `did:webvh:<scid>:<domain>:<path>`, since every lab DID
  is `did:webvh:<scid>:<domain>` with no path and every Farm DID has one.
  Wrong: `didwebvh-ts` 2.8 resolves **both** shapes in Node, checked against the
  real identifiers.
- **Not a stale build.** The error names the Farm mediator DID, so the new
  `.env` is baked in.

So it is specific to resolution **on the phone**, against a host the lab never
used, with the same library that succeeds off-device. `@credo-ts/webvh`
(0.7.1-pr-2704) wraps `didwebvh-ts`, so the next step is to find which of the
two returns `notFound` under React Native rather than assuming.

**This is the intermediate step paying for itself on day one.** Every DID the
lab resolves is served by our own ngrok tunnels; nothing in it resolves a
third-party host. The failure was waiting behind that and no amount of local
green would have shown it.

## 10. Two corrections, and one hypothesis killed

**The Farm mediator is 0.26.4.** It reports its own version in `/readyz`,
alongside `status` and `uptime_seconds`. §2 said those fields were absent there
— they are not; they sit at the end of the body and the probe that "showed"
their absence had been truncated to 700 characters. The route-probe inference
was sound and its conclusion holds, but it was never necessary: the number was
being served the whole time. Ask before inferring.

**Coalescing is not the explanation for the resolver failure.** The Farm's four
hosts share two Cloudflare addresses and a single `*.ic3.dev` wildcard
certificate, which is exactly the shape that produced our `421` on ngrok, so it
was the leading suspect. Three of those hosts fetched over **one** coalesced
HTTP/2 connection all answer `200`. The Farm edge does not refuse coalesced
requests, and this hypothesis is dead rather than merely unproven.

**So the resolver failure is still open**, with more ruled out than in:
not the server, not the path segment, not the proof suite (both stacks sign
`eddsa-jcs-2022` over Ed25519 `did:key` update keys), not TLS (Google Trust
Services, verified), not connection coalescing, not a stale build.

What remains untested is the one difference my Node check could not reach:
`WebVhDidResolver.resolveDidDoc` passes a **verifier** (`WebVhDidCrypto`) into
`resolveDID`, and the Node check passed none, so it never exercised proof
verification at all. That is the next thing to isolate — and it is a real gap in
the check, not a detail.

## 11. Root cause: Credo rejects a DID-Core-valid `service.type`

The phone's full error, read from the page-source dump rather than guessed at:

```
notFound resolver_error: Unable to resolve did
'did:webvh:Qmag…:dids.ic3.dev:firstperson-mediator':
ClassValidationError: DidCommV2Service: Failed to validate class.
 - property type has failed the following constraints: type must be a string
```

**The document resolved.** The fetch worked, the proof verified, and then
Credo's own class validation threw the whole thing away.

The Farm mediator's DID document carries two services whose `type` is an
**array**, and one whose `type` is a string:

| service | `type` |
|---|---|
| `#tsp` | `"TSPTransport"` |
| `#service` | `["DIDCommMessaging"]` |
| `#auth` | `["Authentication"]` |

Every service in every lab DID document uses a string, which is why nothing
local ever exercised this.

**DID Core permits both.** `type` may be a string or a set of strings, so the
Farm's document is valid and Credo is refusing a conformant document.
`DidDocumentService.type` is decorated with a plain `@IsString()`.

The decisive detail is one property above it in the same class:
`serviceEndpoint` is decorated with `IsStringOrJsonObjectSingleOrArray`, which
accepts exactly the string-or-array shape being denied to `type`. Credo already
implements this pattern and did not apply it here — an oversight rather than a
deliberate restriction, which is worth saying when it is reported.

**It is a hard blocker, not a degradation.** Validation runs over the whole
document, so one array-typed service discards the mediator's key agreement and
its endpoints too. Resolution fails, no DIDComm socket opens, and nothing
downstream is attempted — which is why the CORS question is still unanswered.

**Belongs to `credo-ts`, not to OpenVTC.** The Farm is emitting a valid
document. Two ways forward and they are not equivalent: ask upstream to emit
strings, which is asking a conformant producer to narrow itself for one
consumer; or fix the consumer, which is ours, spec-correct, and does not
require anyone else to move before a demo can run. Take the second and offer
the first upstream as a credo-ts contribution.

**Prior art worth citing when reported.** `vta-browser-plugin`, upstream's own
client, resolves `did:webvh` through `@openvtc/vti-didcomm-js` and reads key
material straight from the hosting service without a DID-document class model
at all — so it never meets this. A Credo-based wallet is the first client to
hit it.

## 12. TSP Rev 3, executed: what is proven and what is not

**The interop oracle is current, and two earlier worries of mine were wrong.**
`@openvtc/vti-tsp-js` does not come from npm (public latest is 0.2.0) nor from
the plugin pin. It is a **vendored tarball at 0.3.0**, built from upstream
commit `bfdb0dc` (2026-09-18). Upstream is eleven commits past that and **none
of them touch `packages/tsp-js`**. So our compatibility oracle is byte-current,
and advancing the plugin pin — which I had recommended — would have changed
nothing.

**Codec-level compatibility is proven.** `tspRev3.test.ts` is green, 15/15,
against their library at that version: the published Appendix A vectors
re-packed byte for byte from `ikmE`, **their `pack()` into our `unpack()`** for
nested and routed kinds, ESSR refusal, AEAD-versus-signature tamper refusal,
Rev 2 back-compat, and the 4095-quadlet ingress boundary. Upstream's own
implementation is the oracle rather than our reading of the specification,
which is what makes this a compatibility claim at all.

**Transport-level is now proven too, on devices.** `run-vrc-exchange-tsp` passes
on two emulators: both wallets report *TSP envelope carriage markers present
(sent + received)* and both hold the resulting VRC.

**What that does NOT prove, and the distinction matters for the demo.** That
exchange is wallet-to-wallet: the TSP envelope is physically delivered over the
existing DIDComm connection, with no `vta-service` counterpart involved. It is
a claim about our TSP stack being real, not about ecosystem interop.

### The lab was made TSP-capable, with one thing that did not follow

`affinidi-messaging-sdk` advanced **0.26.10 → 0.26.12** in `vti-main`, and the
VTA and VTC rebuilt on it. That is the release carrying #838, and it is the
release `tsp_rev3_subtask.md` recorded this work as gated on. Without it a
re-establishing send loses its payload to the peer's own invite about half the
time under load, surfacing as "the peer did not answer".

`tsp = true` is now set on alice, community and bob, and all three restarted
clean. **But their DID documents did not change**: alice still advertises only
`DIDCommMessaging` and `VTARest`, still at one version from 2026-09-18. A VTA
advertises its transports **at mint**, so flipping the flag on an existing agent
does not retro-advertise TSP — the same shape as [VTI-12](../../VTI_UPSTREAM_FINDINGS.md).
The Farm's VTA, minted with TSP on, does carry `TSPTransport`.

**So ecosystem TSP interop needs one of two things**: re-provisioning the lab
agents so they are minted with TSP advertised, or the Farm — which already
advertises it and whose mediator at 0.26.4 is past 0.25.0, the release where
"TSP Rev 3: a uniform delivery refusal, and no DIDComm bridge" landed. The Farm
is the better target and is blocked only on the community ACL.

## 13. Ecosystem TSP: the lab is ready, and the gap is one named component

Wallet-to-wallet was never the question. This is where ecosystem TSP — phone to
`vta-service` and `vtc-service` — actually stands after tonight.

### What was blocking it was not configuration

`tsp` is a **Cargo feature on `vta-service`, and it is OFF by default**:
`default = ["setup", "keyring", "rest", "didcomm", "cli-synthesis"]`. A default
build has **no TSP dispatcher**, so setting `tsp = true` in `config.toml` is
inert — which is why flipping it earlier changed nothing, and why the agents'
DID documents did not move. Upstream says so in its own source: *"Without it
there is no TSP dispatcher (the inbound half lives behind that flag), so
advertising a `#tsp` service would publish a"* promise nothing can keep.

`vtc-service` is the reverse — `tsp` is **on by default**, receive-side, and the
reasoning is worth borrowing: a DID document is a contract, and a binary that
cannot serve what its document advertises drops every conforming client's
message.

### What is now true of the lab

- `vta` rebuilt with `--features tsp`; all three agents restarted and report
  *"TSP relationships restored from the durable store"*.
- `affinidi-messaging-sdk` at **0.26.12** (carries #838).
- **alice and the community VTA now advertise `TSPTransport`** at DID log
  version 2, achieved with `vta services tsp enable --mediator-did …` — offline,
  no re-provisioning, no new DIDs, and therefore no `app/.env` cascade or native
  rebuild. The lab's VTA document now has the same shape as the Farm's:
  `TSPTransport` + `DIDCommMessaging` + `VTARest`.
- The mediator is 0.28.9, well past 0.25.0 where TSP Rev 3 landed.

So **every server-side prerequisite for ecosystem TSP is in place locally**, on
a community we administer.

### The gap, named precisely

Keyring has the TSP **codec** (15/15 against upstream's own library) and TSP
**carriage** wallet-to-wallet (green on devices). What it does not have is the
piece that joins them to the ecosystem: **a resolver that reads `TSPTransport`
from a DID document and routes a Trust Task over it.** `ceremony.ts` says so at
the point it matters — *"the parent plan's §4.2 DID-document resolver
(`TSPTransport`) is not built"*.

That is the whole remaining distance. It is one component, it is already
specified as §4.2 of the parent plan, and as of tonight there is nothing
underneath it left to fix: the codec is proven against upstream, the transport
is proven live, the agents advertise the service, the community receives it,
and the mediator speaks the revision.

### Worth reporting upstream

`vta services tsp enable` on a **self-hosted** VTA prints instructions to fetch
the log with `pnm did-mgmt dids get-log` and *"redeploy did.jsonl to your
host"* — for a DID whose host is the VTA itself. Restarting the agent published
version 2 with no redeployment at all. The advice is right for an externally
hosted DID and misleading for a self-hosted one, and the binary knows which it
is, because it says *"This VTA's DID is self-hosted"* in the same breath.
